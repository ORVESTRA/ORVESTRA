# ORVESTRA Governance

**Repository:** ORVESTRA  
**Access:** Public  
**Framework:** DGCP™  
**Updated:** 08 September 2026

---

## Purpose

This document defines the public governance principles for ORVESTRA.

ORVESTRA is a Business Architecture Lab focused on business models, value capture, productization, growth systems, and structured business analysis.

Public work is intended to provide observable, evidence based analysis of businesses and commercial systems while maintaining clear analytical boundaries.

---

## Public Work Scope

ORVESTRA public work may include:

- Corporate Analysis
- Competitor & Commercial Comparison
- Business Model Studies
- Commercial Structure Analysis
- Business System Studies
- Public Case Materials
- Research Notes
- Supporting Public Sources

ORVESTRA independently selects the businesses, companies, industries, and commercial systems it studies.

Publication does not imply sponsorship, partnership, endorsement, engagement, or authorization by any company or organization referenced.

---

## Evidence Standard

Public analytical work should rely on identifiable and publicly accessible information where appropriate.

Sources may include:

- Company filings
- Annual reports
- Investor materials
- Official company publications
- Regulatory records
- Government publications
- Industry reports
- Credible news reporting
- Public market and commercial data
- Other documented public sources

Material claims should be traceable to supporting evidence where possible.

---

## Evidence Boundaries

ORVESTRA distinguishes between:

1. Documented fact
2. Publicly reported information
3. Structural observation
4. Analytical interpretation
5. Information that is not publicly disclosed

Information that is unavailable should not be inferred as known fact.

Where evidence is incomplete, the limitation should be stated.

---

## Analytical Discipline

ORVESTRA analysis should separate description from interpretation.

Analysis may examine:

- Business structure
- Revenue architecture
- Value creation
- Value capture
- Pricing
- Customer structure
- Distribution
- Unit economics
- Competitive position
- Commercial comparison
- Platform dynamics
- Dependencies
- Constraints
- Operational scalability
- Growth systems

Analytical conclusions should remain proportional to the available evidence.

---

## Competitor Comparison

Competitor and commercial comparison should focus on observable structural differences.

Comparison may include:

- Business models
- Revenue structures
- Pricing models
- Customer segments
- Distribution systems
- Value capture
- Cost structure context
- Unit economics context
- Competitive positioning
- Platform dependence
- Scalability
- Growth mechanisms

Comparison should not imply that one company is universally superior to another.

The purpose is structural understanding rather than ranking for investment purposes.

---

## Public and Private Separation

The ORVESTRA public repository contains material approved for public release.

Internal working material may include:

- Preliminary research
- Working analysis
- Unpublished case material
- Internal model development
- Proprietary analytical methods
- Private engagements
- Restricted commercial information

Internal material is maintained separately from the public repository.

Public publication should not expose proprietary methods, private client information, confidential material, or restricted internal analysis.

---

## Independent Selection

ORVESTRA does not operate on the assumption that every business must be analyzed.

The Lab may select subjects based on:

- Structural interest
- Business model relevance
- Commercial change
- Competitive dynamics
- Emerging market structure
- Productization patterns
- Growth system characteristics
- Analytical value

Selection of a company for study does not indicate a commercial relationship.

---

## Analytical Boundaries

ORVESTRA public work does not constitute:

- Investment advice
- Securities research
- Financial product advice
- A recommendation to buy or sell securities
- Legal advice
- Accounting advice
- Tax advice
- Management representation
- Representation on behalf of any company studied

ORVESTRA analyzes business systems and commercial structures.

Readers remain responsible for their own decisions.

---

## Corrections

Public records should be corrected when a material factual error is identified.

Corrections should preserve transparency and record integrity where practical.

Analytical disagreement alone does not constitute a factual error.

---

## Framework Governance

ORVESTRA operates under the **DGCP™ framework**.

DGCP™ provides the broader governance context for:

- Evidence discipline
- Traceability
- Observation
- Verification
- Analytical boundaries
- Record integrity
- Continuous proof

ORVESTRA applies these principles to business architecture and commercial analysis.

---

## Governance Principle

Public credibility should come from observable work, evidence quality, analytical discipline, and consistency over time.

ORVESTRA does not require every reader to agree with an interpretation.

The work should provide enough structure and evidence for readers to understand how the analysis is bounded.
