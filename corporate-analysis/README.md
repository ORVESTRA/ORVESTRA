## Purpose

ORVESTRA Corporate Analysis studies selected companies to understand how their business and commercial systems operate.

The objective is to examine the structure behind a business rather than simply describe the company.

Analysis may consider how value is created, how value is captured, how revenue is generated, how customers are reached, how the company competes, and what structural conditions influence its ability to operate and grow.

---

## Selection

Companies are independently selected by ORVESTRA based on analytical interest.

Selection may be influenced by:

- Business model structure
- Commercial innovation
- Revenue architecture
- Value capture mechanisms
- Industry change
- Competitive dynamics
- Platform structure
- Productization
- Distribution systems
- Growth mechanisms
- Structural constraints
- Business system dependencies

Selection of a company does not imply sponsorship, partnership, endorsement, engagement, or authorization by that company.

---

## Analytical Scope

A Corporate Analysis may examine:

- Company Overview
- Industry Context
- Business Model
- Value Creation
- Value Capture
- Revenue Architecture
- Customer Structure
- Pricing and Monetization
- Distribution and Go to Market
- Cost Structure Context
- Unit Economics Context
- Competitor and Commercial Model Comparison
- Competitive Position
- Platform Dynamics
- Business Dependencies
- Operational Constraints
- Scalability
- Growth Mechanisms
- Structural Opportunities
- Analytical Limits

Not every record is required to contain every analytical area.

Scope should reflect the available evidence and the structure of the company being studied.

---

## Evidence

Corporate Analysis should use identifiable public sources.

Preferred sources include:

- Regulatory filings
- Annual reports
- Company reports
- Official company publications
- Investor materials
- Government records
- Regulatory records
- Industry documentation
- Credible reporting
- Documented public market information

Material claims should be traceable where appropriate.

Information that is not publicly disclosed should not be invented, estimated as fact, or presented as known.

---

## Fact and Interpretation

Records should distinguish between:

1. Documented information
2. Publicly reported information
3. Structural observation
4. Analytical interpretation
5. Unknown or undisclosed information

Analytical conclusions must remain proportional to the evidence available.

---

## Competitor Comparison

Where useful, a Corporate Analysis may compare the subject company with relevant competitors or alternative commercial models.

Comparison may include:

- Business model
- Revenue structure
- Pricing
- Customer structure
- Distribution
- Value capture
- Unit economics context
- Platform dependence
- Competitive positioning
- Scalability
- Growth mechanisms

The purpose is structural comparison, not investment ranking.

## Record Identification

Corporate Analysis records use sequential identifiers.

Example:

`ORVESTRA_CA_0001`

File naming standard:

`YYYY-MM-DD_ORVESTRA_CORPORATE_ANALYSIS_XXXX_COMPANY-NAME.md`

Example:

`2026-09-08_ORVESTRA_CORPORATE_ANALYSIS_0001_COMPANY-NAME.md`

Records are organized by year.

Example structure:

```text
corporate-analysis/
├── README.md
└── 2026/
    ├── 2026-09-08_ORVESTRA_CORPORATE_ANALYSIS_0001_COMPANY-NAME.md
    └── ...
````
