# ORVESTRA Corporate Analysis 0001: Amazon

**Analysis ID:** ORVESTRA_CA_0001

**Date:** 08 September 2026

**Company:** Amazon.com, Inc.

**Group:** Platform & Marketplace

**Category:** Corporate Analysis

**Access:** Public

**Framework:** DGCP™

**Primary Analytical Lens:** Network Effects • Take Rate • Disintermediation • Seller Dependence • Owned Distribution

## 1. Analytical Question

Where does Amazon's marketplace power actually come from, and how does the company capture value from an ecosystem in which independent sellers now generate most of the sales in its store?

## 2. Structural Summary

Amazon should not be treated as a simple ecommerce marketplace.

Its commercial architecture combines first party retail, third party marketplace activity, seller services, fulfillment and logistics, advertising, subscriptions, physical infrastructure, and technology services.

Within the marketplace layer, Amazon connects consumer demand with independent sellers while operating many of the systems surrounding the transaction: discovery, payments, fulfillment, delivery, customer service, advertising, and Prime related convenience.

This creates several value capture points around the same commercial activity rather than relying on a single marketplace commission.

Amazon states that independent sellers account for more than 60 percent of sales in its store. ([Amazon News][1])

## 3. Business Model

At the marketplace level, Amazon creates value by aggregating:

1. Customer demand
2. Product selection
3. Seller supply
4. Search and discovery
5. Transaction infrastructure
6. Trust mechanisms
7. Fulfillment
8. Delivery
9. Advertising
10. Customer membership benefits

Independent sellers supply a substantial portion of the merchandise, while Amazon controls much of the commercial environment through which buyers discover, evaluate, purchase, receive, and sometimes return those products.

Amazon states in its annual filing that it is not the seller of record for third party marketplace transactions and that it earns fixed fees, percentages of sales, per unit fees, interest, or combinations of these mechanisms through seller programs. ([SEC][2])

## 4. Revenue Architecture

Amazon's marketplace economics are distributed across multiple reported revenue categories.

For 2025, Amazon reported:

| Revenue Category            | 2025 Net Sales |
| --------------------------- | -------------: |
| Online Stores               | $269.3 billion |
| Third Party Seller Services | $172.2 billion |
| Advertising Services        |  $68.6 billion |
| Subscription Services       |  $49.6 billion |
| AWS                         | $128.7 billion |
| Consolidated Net Sales      | $716.9 billion |

Third party seller services include commissions, fulfillment and shipping fees, and other seller services. Advertising is reported separately. ([SEC][2])

The pattern continued into 2026. During the first six months of 2026, third party seller services generated approximately $88.4 billion, compared with $76.9 billion in the same period of 2025. Advertising services generated approximately $37.1 billion, compared with $29.6 billion. ([SEC][3])

This matters because Amazon can capture value from sellers through more than the basic transaction commission.

## 5. Take Rate

A single Amazon marketplace take rate should **not** be inferred from Amazon's reported third party seller services revenue.

Amazon does not publicly report marketplace gross merchandise value in a form that allows a clean consolidated marketplace take rate to be calculated from its financial statements.

Third party seller services also combine several different revenue streams, including commissions, fulfillment, shipping, and other services. Advertising is separately reported.

Amazon's public seller pricing shows that referral fees vary by category. Many categories use a 15 percent referral fee, while others use different rates. Sellers may also incur separate costs for selling plans, fulfillment, advertising, storage, and other optional services. ([Sell on Amazon][4])

Therefore:

**Referral fee ≠ total economic take.**

But the available public information is insufficient to state one reliable platform wide effective take rate.

## 6. Network Effects

Amazon's marketplace contains a classic demand and supply feedback structure.

More customers increase the commercial value of joining the marketplace for sellers.

More sellers increase product selection, availability, and price competition for customers.

More transactions produce additional signals that can improve discovery, advertising relevance, inventory decisions, fulfillment planning, and other platform functions.

However, Amazon's network effect is stronger than a pure buyer seller matching loop because it is reinforced by physical infrastructure.

Prime, fulfillment capacity, delivery speed, customer reviews, search, returns infrastructure, payments, and advertising make the marketplace more difficult to reproduce as a simple listing service.

The network therefore combines **marketplace liquidity with infrastructure density**.

## 7. Seller Dependence

Independent sellers are economically important to Amazon because they now account for more than 60 percent of sales in the Amazon store. ([Amazon News][1])

Dependence can also run in the opposite direction.

A seller may depend on Amazon for:

1. Customer discovery
2. Search visibility
3. Marketplace reputation
4. Transaction processing
5. Prime access
6. Fulfillment
7. Delivery
8. Advertising
9. Returns infrastructure
10. International distribution

The level of dependence differs by seller.

A company with strong owned distribution, direct customer relationships, multiple retail channels, and independent logistics has a different dependency profile from a seller whose customer acquisition and fulfillment are concentrated almost entirely inside Amazon.

The relevant question is therefore not simply whether a business sells on Amazon.

It is:

**Which parts of the seller's commercial system does Amazon control?**

## 8. Owned Distribution

Owned distribution is one of the strongest structural elements in Amazon's model.

Amazon originally developed fulfillment and logistics infrastructure to support its own retail operations and marketplace.

That infrastructure is increasingly becoming a product in its own right.

Amazon Multi Channel Fulfillment allows merchants to use Amazon logistics for transactions originating from their own websites and other platforms. Amazon has expanded this capability across channels including Shopify, Walmart, SHEIN, eBay, Etsy, Temu, and TikTok Shop. ([US Press Center][5])

In May 2026, Amazon went further by launching Amazon Supply Chain Services, opening freight, distribution, fulfillment, and parcel capabilities to businesses beyond the Amazon marketplace. ([US Press Center][6])

This represents an important business architecture shift.

Amazon is not only using distribution to defend its marketplace.

It is **productizing distribution itself**.

## 9. Disintermediation

Marketplace businesses normally face a structural problem.

Once buyer and seller discover each other and establish trust, they may attempt to transact directly.

Amazon reduces this incentive through convenience, Prime benefits, fulfillment, trusted checkout, returns, discovery, and delivery performance.

But Amazon's more interesting response is that it increasingly does not require every commercial interaction to remain inside Amazon.com.

Buy with Prime enables merchants to offer Prime related shopping benefits on their own websites while using Amazon's fulfillment infrastructure. ([Amazon SER][7])

Multi Channel Fulfillment similarly allows Amazon infrastructure to support orders originating outside the Amazon marketplace.

This changes the disintermediation problem.

Instead of relying entirely on:

**Keep the transaction inside Amazon**

the architecture can increasingly support:

**If the transaction moves elsewhere, Amazon may still participate in the infrastructure layer.**

That is a structurally stronger response to disintermediation than transaction retention alone.

## 10. Value Creation

Amazon creates marketplace value through the combination of:

**Demand aggregation**

Customers arrive with purchasing intent.

**Selection**

Independent sellers significantly increase the range of available products.

**Trust**

Reviews, transaction systems, returns, customer service, and brand familiarity reduce transaction friction.

**Convenience**

Prime and delivery infrastructure reduce the time and effort required to purchase.

**Infrastructure**

FBA and related logistics capabilities allow sellers to outsource operational functions.

**Discovery**

Search, recommendations, and advertising connect commercial supply with demand.

The seller provides the product.

Amazon increasingly provides much of the system surrounding the product.

## 11. Value Capture

Amazon can capture value at multiple points around the same ecosystem:

**Marketplace access**
Selling plans and referral fees.

**Transaction activity**
Percentage based and per unit seller charges.

**Fulfillment**
FBA and related logistics services.

**Storage and supply chain**
Warehousing, inventory positioning, freight, and distribution services.

**Demand access**
Advertising.

**Customer membership**
Prime and other subscriptions.

**External commerce infrastructure**
Multi Channel Fulfillment, Buy with Prime, and Amazon Supply Chain Services.

This layered monetization architecture is more important than viewing Amazon as a marketplace charging a commission.

## 12. Platform Dependence Versus Infrastructure Dependence

Amazon's expansion outside its own marketplace creates an interesting distinction.

A merchant may reduce **marketplace dependence** by selling through its own website, Shopify, Walmart, or another channel.

But the merchant may still use Amazon for warehousing, fulfillment, delivery, checkout related capabilities, or Prime benefits.

Therefore, multichannel selling does not necessarily eliminate Amazon dependence.

It can shift dependence from:

**Marketplace dependence**

to:

**Infrastructure dependence**

For ORVESTRA, this is one of the most important structural observations in the Amazon model.

## 13. Growth System

Amazon's marketplace growth system can be simplified as:

**More demand**
→ attracts more sellers

**More sellers**
→ increases selection

**Greater selection and convenience**
→ attracts more demand

**More transaction activity**
→ supports more fulfillment density

**Greater fulfillment density**
→ enables faster delivery

**Faster delivery and Prime utility**
→ reinforce customer demand

**Greater seller activity**
→ creates more advertising demand

**More logistics infrastructure**
→ enables Amazon to sell infrastructure outside its own marketplace

The system therefore grows both vertically and horizontally.

Vertically, Amazon monetizes more layers surrounding a marketplace transaction.

Horizontally, infrastructure originally built for Amazon commerce is extended into commerce occurring elsewhere.

## 14. What Amazon Actually Controls

Amazon has substantial influence over:

1. Marketplace access
2. Product discovery inside Amazon
3. Search visibility
4. Marketplace transaction infrastructure
5. Marketplace rules
6. Fulfillment services
7. Delivery infrastructure
8. Advertising inventory
9. Prime ecosystem benefits
10. Large portions of the customer experience

## 15. What Amazon Does Not Fully Control

Amazon does not inherently control:

1. Seller product innovation
2. Manufacturing
3. Supplier relationships outside Amazon
4. Seller owned customer channels
5. External marketplaces
6. Independent logistics alternatives
7. Consumer demand outside Amazon
8. Seller decisions to diversify distribution

This distinction prevents marketplace power from being interpreted as complete control of the businesses operating within it.

## 16. Structural Risk

Amazon's layered seller monetization can also produce commercial and governance pressure.

In August 2026, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission and 22 states filed a lawsuit alleging that Amazon used undisclosed surcharges in advertising auctions. These are allegations and had not been adjudicated at the time of this analysis. ([Federal Trade Commission][8])

For business architecture analysis, the important point is broader:

The more layers through which a platform monetizes ecosystem participants, the more important transparency, pricing governance, and participant trust become.

## 17. ORVESTRA Structural Observation

Amazon's strongest marketplace advantage does not appear to come from network effects alone.

Its stronger position comes from combining:

**Demand + Marketplace + Trust + Prime + Advertising + Fulfillment + Distribution + Supply Chain Infrastructure**

The marketplace attracts commercial activity.

The infrastructure makes that activity easier to execute.

The advertising system monetizes access to demand.

The logistics network allows Amazon to remain economically relevant even when some transactions move beyond Amazon.com.

This makes Amazon less like a conventional marketplace and increasingly more like a **commercial operating infrastructure surrounding commerce itself**.

## 18. What Makes This Business Interesting

Amazon demonstrates how a platform can evolve beyond matching buyers and sellers.

The company has progressively converted internal capabilities into external products:

Retail infrastructure became marketplace infrastructure.

Fulfillment became FBA.

Fulfillment expanded into multichannel logistics.

The logistics network expanded into broader supply chain services.

Prime capabilities expanded toward merchants' own sites through Buy with Prime.

The structural pattern is:

**Build infrastructure for internal scale → establish operational advantage → externalize the capability → create another revenue layer.**

For ORVESTRA, that pattern is particularly relevant to **Productization** and **Value Capture**.

## 19. Analytical Limits

This analysis does not estimate Amazon's total marketplace GMV or an effective consolidated seller take rate because sufficiently precise public data is not available for those calculations.

Seller economics vary materially by category, fulfillment method, advertising usage, product characteristics, returns, storage requirements, and other factors.

Amazon does not disclose marketplace specific operating profit separately from its broader business segments.

This analysis therefore examines business architecture rather than attempting to reconstruct undisclosed marketplace economics.

## 20. Conclusion

Amazon's marketplace power is not based on a single moat.

It emerges from the interaction between customer demand, seller supply, Prime membership, discovery, advertising, fulfillment, and owned distribution infrastructure.

Independent sellers expand Amazon's selection.

Amazon provides access to demand and a growing set of operating capabilities.

The resulting relationship contains mutual value creation but also asymmetric control over important commercial interfaces.

The most significant structural development may be Amazon's expansion beyond marketplace ownership.

By making its logistics and commerce infrastructure usable outside Amazon.com, Amazon is creating a model in which **commerce does not necessarily need to occur on Amazon for Amazon to capture value from it.**

That materially changes how platform disintermediation should be evaluated.
