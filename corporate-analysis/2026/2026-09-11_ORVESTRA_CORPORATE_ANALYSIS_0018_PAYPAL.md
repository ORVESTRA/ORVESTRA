## 1. Analytical Question

Who captures value when a consumer chooses PayPal rather than interacting directly with the underlying payment rail?

A second question follows:

Can PayPal remain economically important if the underlying money increasingly moves through rails that PayPal does not own?

PayPal sits in a different structural position from Visa and Mastercard.

Visa and Mastercard primarily own global payment-network infrastructure.

PayPal increasingly owns combinations of:

**Consumer Interface**

**Digital Wallet**

**Checkout**

**Merchant Relationship**

**Payment Processing**

**Payment Orchestration**

**Stored Credentials**

while relying on multiple underlying funding and payment rails.

Its architecture can be simplified as:

**Consumer Intent**

→ **PayPal / Venmo / Fastlane Interface**

→ **Funding Selection**

→ **Payment Processing**

→ **Underlying Rail**

→ **Merchant**

The payment rail can change.

PayPal attempts to preserve the relationship above the rail.

## 4. Active Account Boundary

PayPal defines an active account as an account registered directly with PayPal or through a platform access partner that completed a qualifying transaction within the prior 12 months.

The definition excludes gateway-exclusive transactions.

One user may have more than one account.

Therefore:

**439 million active accounts ≠ 439 million unique people.**

The metric includes both consumer and merchant relationships under PayPal's definition.

[S1]

**Consumers**

- Store consumer credentials

## 8. TPV Is Not Revenue

PayPal's Q2 2026 TPV was:

**$486.448 billion**

while net revenue was:

**$8.682 billion.**

TPV measures the value of qualifying payments completed on PayPal's platform or enabled through qualifying partner solutions.

It is not:

- PayPal revenue
- PayPal assets
- Merchant revenue
- Cash owned by PayPal

The distinction is fundamental.

---

## 9. TPV Definition

PayPal defines TPV as the value of payments, net of payment reversals, successfully completed on its payment platform or enabled by PayPal through a partner payment solution.

Gateway-exclusive transactions are excluded.

[S1][S2]

Therefore:

**Merchant Payment Activity**

and:

**PayPal TPV**

are not always identical measures.

---

## 11. Transaction Revenue

PayPal generates transaction revenue primarily from fees charged to merchants and consumers in connection with payment activity.

Fees can include:

- Fixed components
- Variable components
- Currency conversion
- Cross-border activity
- Instant transfer fees
- Cryptocurrency transaction activity
- Other transaction-related charges

[S1][S2]

The company therefore has several different transaction economics inside one revenue category.

## 12. Other Value Added Services

PayPal's second reported revenue category is:

**Revenues from Other Value Added Services**

- Partnerships

## 15. Why Product Mix Matters

Not all PayPal TPV carries the same economics.

Payment volume can originate from:

- PayPal branded checkout
- Venmo
- Braintree
- P2P
- Other products

Some forms of TPV can have:

- Higher transaction pricing
- Higher processing costs
- Different risk
- Different merchant economics

## 16. Branded Versus Unbranded Payments

One of the most important distinctions in PayPal's architecture is between:

**Branded Checkout**

and:

**Unbranded Processing**

In branded checkout, the customer deliberately selects:

**PayPal**

or:

**Venmo**

as the payment experience.

In Braintree processing, PayPal infrastructure can process the transaction while the consumer may never see the PayPal brand.

The company therefore operates at both:

**Consumer Interface Layer**

and:

**Merchant Infrastructure Layer**

---

## 17. PayPal Branded Checkout

PayPal branded checkout places the PayPal identity directly in front of the customer.

The consumer can use stored:

- Payment credentials
- Shipping information
- Account balance
- Eligible financing
- Rewards
- Other funding options

This creates a direct consumer relationship at the point of purchase.

## 18. Why Branded Checkout Is Strategically Important

Branded checkout gives PayPal more than payment-processing activity.

It can provide:

- Consumer recognition
- Stored payment preferences
- Trust
- Buyer protection on eligible purchases
- Funding choice
- Rewards
- Conversion utility

The merchant is not merely buying processing.

The merchant can be buying access to:

**PayPal Consumer Demand and Trust**

## 23. Funding Mix Matters

PayPal's transaction expense varies materially according to how payments are funded.

PayPal states that transactions funded through:

**Credit Cards**

generally cost more than:

**Debit Cards**

which generally cost more than:

**Bank Funding**

or internal sources such as:

**PayPal / Venmo Balance**

or certain PayPal credit products.

[S1]

This creates an important transaction-economics variable.

## 31. Braintree

It allows large merchants and platforms to accept and process combinations of:

- Credit cards
- Debit cards

- Venmo
- Digital wallets
- Alternative payment methods

Braintree can operate without the PayPal brand being visible to the consumer.

## 32. Braintree as Infrastructure

Braintree changes PayPal's role.

PayPal branded checkout:

**PayPal Owns the Consumer Payment Interface**

Braintree:

**Merchant Can Own the Consumer Interface While PayPal Processes the Payment**

This gives PayPal exposure to transactions where:

**PayPal Is Infrastructure Rather Than Brand**

## 34. Braintree Volume Quality

PayPal's 2025 strategy deliberately reduced some lower-quality Braintree volume while renegotiating merchant economics.

In the first half of 2025:

- Braintree volume declined

**More Payment Volume Is Not Always Better Payment Volume**

## 35. Profitable Growth

Braintree is a useful example of PayPal prioritizing:

**Economic Quality**

over:

**Maximum TPV**

Large enterprise processing can generate enormous payment volume but can also carry:

- Thin pricing
- High processing costs
- Merchant bargaining power

The architecture must therefore optimize:

**Revenue and Margin Quality**

not merely:

**Transaction Scale**

## 36. Braintree Cost Structure

PayPal states that Braintree products and services generally have:

**Higher transaction expense rates**

than its other products and services.

[S2]

Therefore rapid Braintree growth can simultaneously increase:

**Revenue**

and:

**Processing Cost**

## 43. Fastlane by PayPal

Fastlane extends PayPal into guest checkout.

The customer can save:

- Payment cards
- Shipping address
- Contact details

and later use them at participating merchants through an email-based recognition flow.

[S6]

## 44. Fastlane Is Not a PayPal Account

PayPal explicitly distinguishes:

**Fastlane Profile**

from:

**PayPal Account**

A Fastlane user can use stored card and shipping information without holding a conventional PayPal wallet relationship.

[S7]

This is strategically important.

PayPal can own part of the checkout identity layer without requiring the customer to become a PayPal wallet user.

## 45. Checkout Identity as Infrastructure

Fastlane changes the architecture from:

**Merchant-Specific Guest Checkout**

toward:

**Portable Checkout Identity**

A customer's stored information can be recognized across participating merchants.

This creates:

**Consumer Convenience**

and:

**Merchant Conversion Infrastructure**

## 75. Credit Exposure Is Hybrid

PayPal's credit model is not one uniform balance-sheet structure.

In the United States:

- Several revolving consumer products and branded credit cards are issued by independent chartered financial institutions
- PayPal can earn revenue-share economics
- PayPal also holds or purchases receivables in selected products

Outside the U.S., PayPal entities can directly extend certain forms of credit.

[S1]

Therefore PayPal has more direct credit exposure than Visa or Mastercard, but less conventional bank-balance-sheet exposure than JPMorgan Chase.

## 151. Analytical Limits

PayPal's operating metrics require strict separation.

TPV should not be interpreted as PayPal revenue, assets or customer balances.

Active accounts should not be interpreted as unique users.

One user may have multiple accounts.

Payment transactions exclude gateway-exclusive transactions under PayPal's definition.

Transactions per active account are an average across consumer and merchant accounts and should not be interpreted as typical individual consumer frequency.

Transaction revenue growth should not be assumed to track TPV growth directly.

Product mix, merchant mix, co-marketing, FX, partner incentives and funding mix can materially affect economics.

PayPal-branded checkout, Venmo and Braintree have different economics.

Braintree TPV should not be treated as economically equivalent to branded checkout TPV.

Transaction expense rate is not PayPal's merchant take rate.

Transaction and credit loss rate is not a pure fraud-loss measure.

Transaction margin dollars and transaction margin are non-GAAP PayPal-defined metrics.

Transaction margin dollars should not be interpreted as GAAP operating income.

Interest on customer balances is rate-sensitive and is separately excluded in one management metric.

Revenue from other value added services should not be compared mechanically with Mastercard's Value-Added Services and Solutions category.

PayPal does not separately disclose standardized revenue or operating profit for:

- Branded Checkout
- Venmo
- Braintree
- Fastlane
- PayPal Open
- PayPal World
- PYUSD
- Agentic Commerce

The Fastlane eligible-account and card figures are eligibility measures, not active-user measures.

Fastlane conversion and speed figures are based on PayPal internal data and should not be generalized to every merchant.

Venmo Debit Card and Pay with Venmo monthly-active-account growth rates should not be interpreted as revenue growth.

PayPal World should not be treated as universally deployed cross-wallet infrastructure.

PYUSD is issued by a third-party issuer and should not be treated as PayPal Holdings-issued deposit money.

PayPal's credit architecture varies by product and jurisdiction.

Partner-issued U.S. credit products should not be treated as PayPal-funded loans in every case.

Agentic commerce capabilities are emerging and should not be treated as evidence of material transaction volume or revenue without additional disclosure.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Universal PayPal take rate
- Branded checkout standalone revenue
- Branded checkout standalone margin
- Braintree standalone margin
- Venmo standalone profitability
- Fastlane revenue
- PayPal Open revenue
- PYUSD revenue
- Customer lifetime value
- Merchant switching cost
- Future market share
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

PayPal Open broadens payment processing into a merchant-commerce platform.

Credit and merchant financing turn transaction history into financial-service opportunities.

PYUSD introduces another form of value into the wallet architecture.

PayPal World attempts to connect otherwise separate wallet systems.

Agentic commerce creates another interface that can sit above the payment.

PayPal's challenge is therefore not simply to process more transactions.

It is to remain the layer that consumers, merchants and software systems trust to decide:

**How the Transaction Should Happen**

In Q2 2026, PayPal processed $486.4 billion of TPV across 6.75 billion payment transactions.

But the quality of that volume matters.

Braintree processing has different costs from branded checkout.

Funding through cards has different economics from bank or balance funding.

Merchant incentives affect revenue.

Fraud and credit create real loss exposure.

PayPal therefore has to optimize the complete transaction, not merely maximize volume.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**PayPal's strategic power comes from sitting above payment rails and turning fragmented financial infrastructure into one usable consumer and merchant experience.**

Its strongest long-term moat may not be ownership of the rail.

It may be ownership of the:

**Identity**

**Checkout**

**Trust**

**Orchestration**

that determines which rail gets used.
