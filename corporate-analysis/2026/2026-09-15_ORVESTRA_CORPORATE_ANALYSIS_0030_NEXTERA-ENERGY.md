NextEra Energy appears to be:

**An Electricity Company.**

That description is incomplete.

Its actual business architecture combines two different economic systems.

---

## FPL

FPL operates:

**Regulated Electricity Infrastructure**

in Florida.

The system includes:

1. Generation
2. Battery storage
3. Transmission
4. Distribution
5. Substations
6. Customer connections

FPL primarily earns revenue through:

**Electricity Delivery Under Regulated Rates.**

Its economic growth is heavily connected to:

**Investment in Utility Infrastructure**

that becomes eligible for recovery through regulated mechanisms.

## NextEra Energy Resources

NEER develops and operates energy infrastructure including:

1. Solar
2. Wind
3. Battery storage
4. Nuclear
5. Natural gas related activities

1. Long-term power contracts
2. Capacity arrangements
3. Merchant exposure
4. Regulated transmission
5. Energy development
6. Infrastructure ownership
7. Tax attributes and incentives where applicable

NEER is therefore closer to:

**Energy Infrastructure Development + Long-Term Asset Ownership**

than:

**Traditional Retail Utility.**

**Utility Infrastructure**

1. Usage

4. battery adoption
5. demand patterns

**35,627 MW**

under the company's methodology.

## Infrastructure Switching Cost

1. Power plant

3. battery facility

1. New permitting
2. new interconnection
3. new financing
4. new construction
5. years of execution

**Time and Capital.**

# 15. What Could Make the Current Model Less Necessary?

This is the Business Model Lab signature question.

NextEra Energy's current model relies on large-scale:

**Centralized or Networked Electricity Infrastructure**

combined with continuous capital deployment.

Several structural changes could reduce the importance of parts of that model.

---

## 15.1 Distributed Generation Could Reduce Centralized Supply Requirements

If customers increasingly deploy:

1. Rooftop solar
2. local generation
3. batteries
4. microgrids

they may buy less energy from centralized generation.

The grid may remain necessary.

But:

**The Utility's Role Could Change.**

---

## 15.2 Storage Could Reduce Peak-Generation Requirements

Cheap, long-duration storage could shift electricity through time.

That could reduce the need for some:

**Peaking Generation Capacity.**

NextEra Energy is partly protected because it is also a major storage developer.

---

## 15.3 Customers Could Bring Their Own Generation

Large-load customers may increasingly pair new demand with:

**Dedicated Energy Assets.**

That could reduce the need for utilities to finance every incremental generation requirement through shared customer rates.

NextEra Energy's NEER platform allows it to participate in:

**The Alternative Model.**

---

## 15.4 Modular Generation Could Reduce Development Advantage

If future power technologies become:

1. Highly standardized
2. modular
3. fast to permit
4. easy to finance

the advantage of large-scale development organizations could decline.

Infrastructure development could become more commoditized.

---

## 15.5 Interconnection Reform Could Reduce Scarcity Value

Today, access to:

**Interconnection**

and:

**Transmission**

can be major bottlenecks.

If grid expansion and interconnection become substantially easier:

**Scarcity Advantage Could Decline.**

More developers could access attractive markets.

---

## 15.6 Lower Electricity Demand Growth Could Reduce Capital Opportunity

NextEra Energy's growth architecture assumes significant future demand.

If:

1. Data-center growth slows
2. electrification slows
3. population growth slows
4. energy efficiency offsets demand

less new infrastructure may be required.

A capital-intensive growth model becomes less valuable when:

**The System Does Not Need More Capital.**

---

## 15.7 Technology Could Reduce Grid Dependence

A combination of:

**Cheap Generation + Cheap Storage + Local Control**

could allow selected customers to operate with less dependence on centralized grids.

The effect would vary by customer and geography.

---

## 15.8 Financing Advantage Could Narrow

NextEra Energy's scale and access to capital are important advantages.

If capital becomes abundant and inexpensive for smaller developers:

**The Financing Moat Could Narrow.**

Development competition could increase.

---

## 15.9 Policy Change Could Reduce Project Economics

Changes to:

1. Tax incentives
2. environmental rules

FPL revenue should not be treated as equivalent to NEER revenue.

FPL rate base should not be interpreted as revenue.

Regulatory capital employed should not be interpreted as earnings or cash flow.

Customer-account growth should not be interpreted as identical to electricity-demand growth.

Average customer usage and number of customer accounts are separate variables.

Net generating capacity should not be interpreted as actual electricity generation.

MW should not be interpreted as MWh.

Storage MW should not be treated as equivalent to generation MW.

A project's nameplate capacity should not be treated as actual output.

Renewables and storage backlog should not be interpreted as operating assets.

The approximately 35.1 GW NEER backlog as of 24 July 2026 should not be treated as current generation capacity.

Projects placed into service should not be added mechanically to backlog without preserving measurement date and project removals.

Contracted generating capacity should not be interpreted as companywide generation capacity.

The approximately 35,627 MW contracted-generation measure at year-end 2025 represents a specific NEER portfolio category.

Power-sales contract duration should not be interpreted as guaranteed profit duration.

Adjusted earnings is a non-GAAP measure.

Adjusted EPS should not be substituted for GAAP EPS.

First-half 2026 capital expenditures, independent power investments, and nuclear fuel purchases combine several investment categories.

The approximately $19.389 billion figure should not be described simply as conventional capital expenditure without preserving the company's disclosed definition.

Estimated future capital expenditures through 2030 are forecasts.

The approximately $54.730 billion FPL and $40.020 billion NEER disclosed future capital estimates are not guaranteed realized spending.

FPL's roughly 21 GW of large-load interest should not be treated as signed customer demand.

The approximately 12 GW in advanced discussions should not be treated as contracted load.

Large-load demand should not automatically be labeled AI demand.

Hyperscale data centers represent only part of possible large-load interest.

The proposed Paducah project's 1.8 GW utility capacity, 1.2 GW-plus compute capacity, and up to 4.6 GW dedicated energy resources are development figures and should not be treated as operating infrastructure.

# Conclusion

NextEra Energy closes Group 6 by shifting the analysis from:

**The Company That Manufactures Physical Infrastructure**

to:

**The Company That Owns and Operates Physical Infrastructure.**

Tesla manufactures vehicles, batteries, and energy products.

Caterpillar manufactures machines.

Deere manufactures productive equipment.

GE Vernova manufactures electricity-system technology.

NextEra Energy deploys capital into:

**The Operating Energy System Itself.**

That creates a fundamentally different business architecture.

FPL serves more than:

**Six Million Customer Accounts**

through a physical system containing approximately:

**35,963 MW of generating capacity**

**93,000 circuit miles of transmission and distribution**

and:

**932 substations**

at year-end 2025.

That infrastructure is not simply:

**Inventory Waiting to Be Sold.**

It remains in service.

It remains on the system.

It creates electricity delivery capacity.

It supports customer relationships.

And continued investment can expand:

**Regulated Capital Employed.**

NEER provides a second architecture.

Rather than operating only inside one utility territory, it repeatedly develops:

**Generation**

**Storage**

**Transmission**

and other energy infrastructure across multiple markets.

At Q2 2026, its renewables and storage backlog stood at approximately:

**35.1 GW.**

The backlog is not operating capacity.

But it represents the pipeline through which:

**Development Capability**

can become:

**Future Physical Infrastructure.**

NextEra Energy therefore operates a type of infrastructure production system.

The inputs are:

**Capital**

**Land**

**Equipment**

**Permits**

**Interconnection**

**Customer Contracts**

and:

**Development Capability.**

The output is:

**A Long-Lived Energy Asset.**

Once completed, that asset can produce commercial value for decades.

The current energy-demand environment makes the architecture particularly visible.

FPL reported approximately:

**21 GW of large-load interest**

by Q2 2026.

Data centers and other large customers can require gigawatts of new physical supply much faster than traditional infrastructure can be constructed.

That creates a new bottleneck.

Digital businesses may scale quickly.

**Electricity Infrastructure Does Not.**

NextEra Energy is responding through several technologies simultaneously.

It is expanding:

**Solar.**

**Battery Storage.**

**Transmission.**

It is pursuing restart of:

**Duane Arnold Nuclear.**

It has announced development funding for up to:

**10 GW of Natural Gas Generation.**

It is participating in architectures where new data-center loads can be paired with:

**Dedicated Power Infrastructure.**

The strategic advantage is therefore not that NextEra Energy has chosen one permanent energy technology.

Its advantage is potentially:

**The Ability to Build Whatever Physical Energy Infrastructure Becomes Necessary Next.**

This leads directly to the Business Model Lab signature question.

What could make the current model less necessary?

Distributed energy can reduce reliance on centralized supply.

Storage can change the value of generation.

Microgrids can reduce dependence on shared infrastructure.

Dedicated customer generation can change the conventional utility-growth model.

Interconnection reform can reduce scarcity advantages.

Technology can change which assets deserve capital.

But those changes do not necessarily make NextEra Energy less relevant.

They can change:

**Which Infrastructure It Needs to Own.**

That is the key.

NextEra Energy does not need today's exact infrastructure mix to remain permanent.

It needs its capability to:

**Finance**

**Develop**

**Build**

**Connect**

and:

**Operate**

large-scale physical energy systems to remain economically valuable.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**NextEra Energy's durable business advantage is not simply electricity generation or renewable-energy ownership. It is the repeated conversion of capital, development capability, regulatory access, supply-chain capacity, and physical infrastructure into long-lived energy assets that customers and the wider economy continue to need after construction is complete.**
