## 3. Mastercard Is Not a Bank

A critical analytical boundary is that Mastercard states that it is:

**Not a financial institution.**

Mastercard does not:

- Issue cards
- Extend credit to account holders
- Determine consumer interest rates
- Receive revenue from issuer interest charges
- Determine other account-holder fees charged by issuers
- Establish merchant acceptance rates charged by acquirers

In most cases, the account-holder relationship belongs to Mastercard's financial-institution customers.

[S1]

This separates Mastercard structurally from JPMorgan Chase later in Group 4.

## 13. Measurement Boundary

Mastercard defines:

**Gross Dollar Volume**

as:

**Purchase Volume + Cash Volume**

on cards carrying Mastercard brands.

Switched transactions measure:

**Transactions initiated and switched through Mastercard's network.**

These metrics are different.

A high-value transaction contributes more GDV than a low-value transaction.

Each contributes one transaction to the switched transaction count.

[S1][S2]

## 18. Four-Party Payment Architecture

A conventional Mastercard card payment typically involves:

1. Account holder
2. Issuer
3. Merchant
4. Acquirer

Mastercard provides the network connecting the financial institutions.

The simplified architecture is:

**Account Holder**

→ **Merchant**

→ **Acquirer**

→ **Mastercard Network**

→ **Issuer**

The network makes interoperability possible between institutions that do not need individual bilateral technical connections.

---

## 19. Authorization

Authorization is the process through which a transaction request is routed to the issuer for approval.

The issuer can evaluate factors including:

- Account status
- Available credit
- Available balance
- Fraud signals
- Internal controls

Mastercard provides network routing and related services.

The issuer generally controls the underlying account decision.

[S2]

---

## 20. Clearing

Clearing determines and exchanges transaction information between:

- Issuer
- Acquirer

after the payment has been conducted.

The network creates standardized information exchange around the transaction.

[S2]

---

## 21. Settlement

Settlement facilitates:

**Determination and Exchange of Funds**

between participating parties.

[S2]

Mastercard therefore participates in the infrastructure through which settlement obligations are coordinated.

That does not make Mastercard the underlying lender to the consumer.

## 23. Brand Versus Processing

## 26. Interchange

A critical payment-economics boundary is that:

**Mastercard does not earn interchange as revenue.**

In the traditional four-party model, interchange is generally collected from acquirers and paid to issuers.

Mastercard can establish default interchange rates where no separate terms exist.

It can also administer collection and remittance through settlement.

But the interchange itself is not Mastercard revenue.

[S1]

---

## 27. Merchant Discount Rate

The merchant discount rate is generally established by the acquirer.

It can reflect:

- Interchange
- Acquirer costs
- Processing
- Additional services

Mastercard does not set the merchant discount rate charged by an acquirer to the merchant.

[S1]

Therefore:

**Merchant Acceptance Cost ≠ Mastercard Revenue**

---

## 28. Why This Distinction Matters

A common simplified interpretation is:

**Merchant Pays Card Fee → Mastercard Receives Card Fee**

That is incorrect.

The payment economics can be divided across:

- Issuer
- Acquirer
- Processor
- Network
- Gateway
- Additional service providers

Mastercard captures only the economics associated with its own contractual roles.

## 131. Analytical Limits

Mastercard operational metrics require strict separation.

Gross Dollar Volume should not be interpreted as Mastercard revenue, assets or funds owned by Mastercard.

Mastercard-branded card count should not be interpreted as unique customers.

The 3.391 billion Mastercard-branded card figure calculated from Mastercard's fiscal 2025 program table excludes Maestro and Cirrus.

Acceptance locations should not be interpreted as unique merchant companies.

Mastercard-branded transaction activity should not be treated as identical to Mastercard-switched transaction activity.

Mastercard switches more than 70 percent of Mastercard and Maestro-branded transactions, not every branded transaction.

Mastercard does not issue cards or extend consumer credit.

Mastercard does not earn issuer interest revenue.

Mastercard does not earn interchange as its own revenue.

Merchant discount rates should not be interpreted as Mastercard revenue.

Domestic assessments, cross-border assessments and transaction-processing assessments are not net revenue.

Rebates and incentives materially affect Mastercard Payment Network revenue.

Payment Network revenue and Value-Added Services and Solutions revenue are Mastercard's reported revenue categories and should not be combined with operational assessment metrics as if they were additive.

The approximately 41 percent VAS&S share of fiscal 2025 revenue is a calculation based on reported category revenue.

Mastercard Move endpoint reach should not be interpreted as unique users or active recipients.

The Mastercard Move reach figures cited are company disclosures stated as valid as of October 2025.

Tokenized-transaction percentage should not be interpreted as unique tokenized customers.

The June 2026 stablecoin settlement announcement describes planned expanded settlement capabilities and should not be interpreted as full deployment across every issuer, acquirer, market or transaction.

The BVNK acquisition was completed in August 2026, but completion does not establish integration, profitability or material revenue contribution.

The June 2026 European agentic-payment transaction demonstrates production execution for a specific implementation and should not be treated as evidence of broad commercial adoption.

Agent Pay for Machines is an emerging service and should not be treated as a material current revenue source without further public evidence.

Mastercard does not publicly disclose standalone operating profitability for:

- Payment Network
- Value-Added Services and Solutions
- Mastercard Move
- Real-time payments
- Open finance
- Stablecoins
- Agent Pay

This analysis does not estimate:

- Universal Mastercard transaction take rate
- Per-transaction profit
- Interchange economics belonging to issuers
- Merchant-level acquiring economics
- Mastercard Move standalone revenue
- Stablecoin standalone revenue
- Agentic-payment revenue
- Customer-level switching cost
- Future market share
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

## 132. Conclusion

Mastercard demonstrates that payment networks should not be analyzed as simple transaction toll businesses.

The core network remains extremely important.

In fiscal 2025, Mastercard-branded gross dollar volume reached:

**$10.6 trillion**

and Mastercard switched:

**175.5 billion transactions.**

But the economic architecture around that network has expanded substantially.

Payment Network revenue was:

**$19.476 billion.**

Value-Added Services and Solutions generated another:

**$13.315 billion.**

By Q2 2026, Payment Network revenue was growing 10 percent while Value-Added Services and Solutions grew 20 percent.

This reveals the direction of the business.

Mastercard can monetize:

**Movement of Money**

and:

**The Capabilities Required to Make That Movement Trusted and Useful.**

Security becomes a product.

Authentication becomes a product.

Transaction data becomes insight.

Account connectivity becomes open finance.

Real-time bank infrastructure becomes another rail.

Mastercard Move extends the business from purchases into broader money movement.

Tokenization allows credentials to survive changes in device and interface.

Stablecoin infrastructure allows Mastercard to participate as new forms of money emerge.

Agent Pay attempts to extend trust from human commerce into machine commerce.

The visible payment method can therefore change without necessarily eliminating Mastercard's role.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**Mastercard's most durable asset may not be any single payment rail. It is the combination of global acceptance, institutional integration, payment standards, security, data and trust that allows Mastercard to remain useful as money moves across an expanding number of rails.**

The deeper business-model transition is:

**Card Network**

→ **Payment Network**

→ **Multi-Rail Infrastructure**

→ **Trust and Services Platform**

That transition will be one of the central questions of Group 4.
