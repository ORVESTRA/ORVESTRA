## Structural Question

Where does Deere's durable economic value actually sit:

**Manufacturing**

**Installed Equipment Base**

**Dealer Network**

**Precision Technology**

**Lifecycle Services**

**Financing**

or:

**The Production System Around the Machine?**

John Deere appears to sell:

**Agricultural and Construction Equipment.**

The deeper business architecture is:

**Manufacturing**

→ **Dealer Distribution**

→ **Installed Equipment Base**

→ **Parts + Service + Precision Upgrades**

→ **Connectivity**

→ **Operations Center**

→ **Automation + AI**

→ **Autonomy**

→ **Lifecycle and Solutions as a Service**

The machine therefore begins the commercial relationship.

It does not necessarily end it.

Deere is attempting to move from:

**Machine Manufacturer**

toward:

**Production-System Technology Company**

without giving up the physical equipment layer that gives the technology access to the real world.

## Manufacturing Operating Leverage

Factory economics depend on:

**Production Volume.**

If production declines:

**Fixed Manufacturing Costs**

must be spread across fewer machines.

If production grows without equivalent fixed-cost expansion:

**Fixed-Cost Absorption Improves.**

This creates cyclical operating leverage.

---

## Agriculture Adds Seasonality

Agricultural equipment demand is influenced by:

1. Planting seasons
2. Harvest seasons
3. Crop prices
4. Farm income
5. Interest rates
6. Input costs
7. Weather
8. Used-equipment inventories

Deere manufactures some products in anticipation of seasonal retail demand.

[S1]

**Forecasting Demand Before the Customer Needs the Machine.**

---

## Early Order Programs

Deere uses early order programs for selected equipment.

[S1]

# 15. What Could Make the Current Model Less Necessary?

This is the central Business Model Lab question.

Deere's current model remains heavily based on:

**Manufacturing and Selling Long-Lived Equipment Through Dealer Networks.**

Several structural developments could reduce the importance of that model.

---

## 15.1 Machine Ownership Could Become Less Necessary

If farmers increasingly purchase:

**Field Work**

rather than:

**Machines**

the economic unit could shift from:

**Tractor Sold**

toward:

**Acres Completed.**

Autonomous equipment could accelerate this change because high-utilization machine fleets can potentially serve several customers.

Deere would then need to monetize:

**Productive Capacity**

rather than relying primarily on individual machine ownership.

---

## 15.2 Small Autonomous Machines Could Challenge Large-Machine Economics

Traditional agricultural productivity has often increased through:

**Larger Machines**

that cover more acreage per hour.

Autonomous systems may create another path:

**More Machines With Less Human Labor.**

If small autonomous machines can work continuously, some applications could become less dependent on:

**Ever-Larger Equipment.**

That could change Deere's historical manufacturing economics.

---

## 15.3 Intelligence Could Separate From the Machine

If:

**AI**

**Autonomy**

**Fleet Management**

become hardware-agnostic layers, customers may choose:

**Physical Equipment From One Company**

and:

**Intelligence From Another.**

Financial Services revenues should not be treated as equipment sales.

Financing receivables should not be interpreted as revenue.

Wholesale equipment shipments to dealers should not be interpreted as final retail sales to end customers.

Dealer inventory can create timing differences between Deere shipments and customer demand.

Dealer economics should not automatically be treated as Deere revenue or Deere profit.

Outside the U.S. and Canada, distribution structures differ by geography and product.

Services and lifecycle economics are not disclosed as one standardized revenue or margin category.

Parts revenue should not be inferred from total equipment revenue.

Precision technology revenue is not comprehensively disclosed as a separate financial segment.

Solutions as a Service should not be treated as a material current revenue stream merely because it is a stated strategic priority.
