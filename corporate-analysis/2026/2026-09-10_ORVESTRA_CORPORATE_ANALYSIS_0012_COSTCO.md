## 1. Analytical Question

What makes a Costco member continue paying for the right to shop?

A second question follows:

How does Costco use membership, purchasing scale, limited assortment, private label, low prices, warehouse distribution, ancillary services, and repeat traffic to create a retail system that can operate at unusually low merchandise margins while maintaining a durable customer relationship?

The central business architecture is:

**Membership**

→ **Price Trust**

→ **Purchase Frequency**

→ **High Sales Volume**

→ **Purchasing Scale**

→ **Operating Efficiency**

→ **Low Prices**

→ **Membership Renewal**

This creates a reinforcing system in which membership is not merely an additional revenue stream.

It is part of the operating architecture that makes the retail proposition possible.

---

## 2. Structural Summary

Costco operates a membership retail system built around:

- Paid memberships
- Warehouse retail
- Limited assortment
- High merchandise volume
- Rapid inventory turnover
- Bulk packaging
- Direct sourcing
- Cross-docking
- Private label
- Gasoline
- Pharmacy
- Optical
- Food court
- Tire services
- E-commerce
- Digitally enabled sales
- Costco Travel
- Business Centers

Unlike conventional retailers, Costco does not primarily attempt to maximize gross margin on each merchandise transaction.

Its stated operating model is to combine:

**Low Prices**

with:

**High Sales Volume**

and:

**Rapid Inventory Turnover**

supported by membership economics and operating efficiency.

Membership is the defining structural element of Costco.

**$65**

**$130 annually**

## 9. Deferred Membership Revenue

Costco does not recognize the annual membership fee entirely when the member pays.

Membership fee revenue is recognized:

**ratably over the one-year membership period.**

This creates an important accounting boundary.

Cash received from memberships and recognized membership revenue occur on different timing schedules.

At 10 May 2026, Costco reported:

**$3.157 billion**

of deferred membership fees on its balance sheet.

[S2]

## 10. Membership Revenue Is Not Merchandise Revenue

Costco has two major revenue streams:

**Merchandise Sales**

and:

**Membership Fees**

The distinction is central.

Membership fees are economically linked to the retail relationship.

But they are not merchandise margin.

Likewise, membership fee revenue should not automatically be interpreted as equivalent to profit.

Costco incurs operating costs across the system that supports both membership and retail activity.

---

## 11. Membership Revenue Relative to the System

Fiscal 2025 membership fees were:

**$5.323 billion**

against total revenue of:

**$275.235 billion.**

Membership fees therefore represented only a small percentage of total revenue.

Yet their strategic importance is much greater than their revenue share alone suggests.

Membership allows Costco to build an operating model where maximizing merchandise markup is not the only route to economic value.

## 12. Low-Margin Retail Architecture

Costco explicitly states that it operates at significantly lower gross margins than many other retailers.

The mechanism is:

**Limited Selection**

→ **High Volume per SKU**

→ **Volume Purchasing**

→ **Efficient Distribution**

→ **Reduced Handling**

→ **Rapid Inventory Turnover**

→ **Low Operating Merchandise Margin**

The business is designed around throughput rather than high markup per individual item.

[S1]

## 14. Price Trust

Costco describes an important concept in its operating philosophy:

**Pricing Authority**

The company seeks to maintain member perception that Costco consistently provides highly competitive value.

This is different from maximizing the highest possible price a customer is willing to pay.

Costco may:

- Absorb some cost increases
- Negotiate with suppliers
- Buy earlier
- Buy larger quantities
- Source differently
- Reduce prices
- Hold prices despite cost increases

when management believes maintaining member value is strategically important.

[S1]

## 15. Price Trust as Brand Power

Costco's brand power differs from luxury or aspiration-based brands.

Its core promise is:

**Trust Me to Offer Strong Value**

This can reduce customer search cost.

A member does not need to perform a complete market-price comparison on every warehouse visit for the system to remain useful.

The economic asset is therefore not simply:

**Low Price**

It is:

**Credibility of Low Price**

---

## 16. Brand Power Without Luxury Pricing

Many strong consumer brands use brand strength to support higher prices.

Costco often uses brand strength in the opposite direction.

The brand promise supports:

**Price Restraint**

rather than:

**Maximum Premium**

This illustrates that brand power does not always appear as higher unit margin.

Brand can instead support:

- Higher traffic
- Greater basket consolidation
- Renewal
- Larger purchase volumes
- Lower customer acquisition friction

## 17. Membership as Commitment

A customer who pays an annual membership fee has already made an explicit commitment to the retail relationship.

This can alter the commercial architecture.

Traditional retail:

**Customer Decides Where to Shop for Each Transaction**

Membership retail:

**Customer First Decides Whether the Retail Relationship Is Worth Maintaining**

Once membership exists, the warehouse becomes part of the customer's available purchasing system for the year.

This does not guarantee spending.

It creates a stronger recurring relationship than an anonymous retail transaction.

## 24. Executive Membership as Spend Reinforcement

The 2% reward can reinforce the purchasing relationship.

A member receives more reward when more qualifying spending occurs inside Costco.

The architecture can therefore become:

**Higher Costco Spend**

→ **Higher Reward**

→ **Greater Perceived Executive Value**

→ **Higher Incentive to Renew or Continue Using Costco**

This does not guarantee behavior for every member.

It creates a commercial mechanism that aligns membership value with shopping activity.

## 27. Limited Assortment

Costco's warehouse model carries:

**fewer than 4,000 active SKUs per warehouse**

in its core warehouse business.

Its online operation carries approximately:

**9,000 to 10,000 SKUs**

with some overlap with warehouse inventory.

[S1]

This is materially different from broad-assortment retail strategies.

---

## 28. Why Fewer SKUs Matter

Limited assortment can concentrate purchasing volume.

Instead of distributing demand across many similar products, Costco can direct large sales volumes toward fewer items.

The structure can improve:

- Supplier negotiating leverage
- Inventory turnover
- Purchasing efficiency
- Warehouse simplicity
- Operational throughput

It also reduces customer choice within a category.

The model therefore exchanges:

**Maximum Selection**

for:

**Curated Value and Scale**

---

## 29. Selection as a Form of Trust

A limited assortment can also shift part of product selection from the customer to Costco.

Instead of asking:

**Which of 30 versions should I buy?**

the customer may encounter only a small number of options.

If the member trusts Costco's buying standards, reduced assortment can lower decision friction.

This turns merchandising discipline into part of the customer proposition.

---

## 30. Volume Purchasing

Costco buys most merchandise directly from suppliers.

Products are routed:

- Directly to warehouses
- Through cross-docking consolidation points referred to as depots

Large concentrated orders support purchasing efficiency.

[S1]

The system is designed to convert membership demand into scale at the supplier level.

---

## 31. Cross-Docking

Costco's distribution architecture emphasizes limited handling.

Merchandise may move through depots rather than conventional long-duration storage systems.

Inside warehouses, products are often displayed directly on pallets while reserve inventory remains on racks above the sales floor.

The objective is to reduce:

- Handling
- Labor
- Complexity

[S1]

---

## 32. Inventory Turnover

Rapid inventory turnover is fundamental to the Costco model.

Costco states that it often sells inventory before payment to suppliers is due while still benefiting from early-payment discounts in some circumstances.

[S1]

This creates favorable working-capital characteristics when the system is operating effectively.

The commercial engine therefore depends not only on margin.

It also depends on:

**Velocity**

---

## 33. Revenue per Item Rather Than Margin per Item

A conventional retailer may seek more profit from each unit.

Costco's architecture allows another route:

**Lower Margin × Very High Volume**

This places emphasis on:

- Member traffic
- Inventory velocity
- Purchasing concentration
- Warehouse productivity

A low-margin product can still be economically attractive if turnover and volume are sufficiently high.

---

## 34. Kirkland Signature

Kirkland Signature is Costco's private-label brand.

Costco states that Kirkland Signature products are intended to provide:

- High quality
- Prices generally below national brands
- Differentiation
- Lower costs
- Generally higher margins than national-brand equivalents

Costco expects to continue increasing private-label sales penetration.

[S1][S7]

---

## 35. Kirkland as Brand Architecture

Kirkland creates an unusual relationship.

The customer may trust:

**Costco**

before knowing the specific manufacturer behind the product.

The retailer's brand therefore becomes the product-level trust layer.

The architecture is:

**Retailer Trust**

→ **Private-Label Trial**

→ **Product Satisfaction**

→ **Stronger Retailer Trust**

Kirkland can reinforce Costco itself.

---

## 36. Exclusive Supply

Kirkland is exclusive to Costco.

That matters structurally.

A nationally branded product can often be purchased through competing retailers.

A Kirkland item cannot be directly purchased from a competing warehouse chain under the same Costco brand.

Private label therefore creates:

**Product Differentiation**

inside a business otherwise heavily focused on commodity value.

---

## 37. Kirkland and Pricing Power

Private label gives Costco more control over:

- Product specification
- Packaging
- Sourcing
- Pricing
- Brand positioning

But Costco uses this control primarily to reinforce its value proposition.

The objective is not simply to extract the highest possible private-label margin.

Kirkland helps make membership itself more distinctive.

---

## 38. Brand Architecture

Costco therefore operates two major brand layers.

### Costco

Represents:

**Membership + Value + Retail Trust**

### Kirkland Signature

Represents:

**Product-Level Quality + Value**

The two brands reinforce each other.

## 42. Membership Gate

Costco's warehouse system is generally built around membership access.

The customer relationship therefore begins before product selection.

The member has already chosen:

**Costco as a Purchasing Environment**

before deciding:

**Which Product to Buy**

This gives Costco control over the commercial context surrounding the merchandise.

## 43. Membership as Distribution Filter

A membership requirement reduces universal accessibility.

That appears restrictive from a traffic-maximization perspective.

But it also creates a more identifiable and committed customer base.

Costco knows that warehouse traffic comes largely from people who have already accepted the annual relationship.

The architecture prioritizes:

**Member Economics**

over:

**Maximum Anonymous Footfall**

## 49. Repeat Purchase Loop

Costco's repeat loop can be represented as:

**Pay Membership**

→ **Visit Warehouse**

→ **Receive Perceived Value**

→ **Purchase Frequently**

→ **Increase Membership Utility**

→ **Renew Membership**

The more frequently a customer uses the warehouse, the easier it may be to justify the annual fee.

Membership and shopping frequency can therefore reinforce each other.

---

## 50. Bulk Packaging

Many Costco consumables are sold in:

- Cases
- Cartons
- Multi-packs

Bulk packaging supports:

- Higher unit volume
- Reduced handling
- Purchasing efficiency

But it also changes household purchase behavior.

A member may purchase more units in a single transaction than at a conventional retailer.

This increases basket size while reducing the number of individual units Costco needs to handle.

---

## 51. Treasure-Hunt Dynamics

Costco's limited and changing assortment can create discovery value.

Not every item remains permanently available.

This can make warehouse visits partly exploratory rather than purely replenishment-driven.

However, Costco does not provide a standardized public financial metric for "treasure-hunt" purchasing.

ORVESTRA therefore treats this as a merchandising characteristic rather than a quantified revenue driver.

## 54. Digital Does Not Replace Membership

Costco's e-commerce system still sits inside the broader membership architecture.

Members can use their memberships across warehouses and Costco e-commerce sites.

The digital channel therefore extends distribution without necessarily creating a separate customer relationship.

This differs from a retailer building an unrelated online marketplace.

**9,000 to 10,000 SKUs online**

**4,000**

- Bulk purchasing
- Limited selection

**Devices + Software + Data + Services**

**Membership + Warehouse + Merchandise + Fuel + Services + Travel + Digital**

## 60. Customer Data

Membership also gives Costco an identifiable customer architecture.

A conventional cash retail purchase can be relatively anonymous.

Membership creates continuity across transactions.

This can support:

- Renewal management
- Member services
- Digital accounts
- Purchase-related functionality
- Executive rewards

Costco's primary advantage, however, remains the economic value proposition rather than attempting to monetize customer data as a standalone advertising platform.

## 61. Retail Media Boundary

Costco should not automatically be analyzed using the same retail-media architecture as Walmart or Amazon.

Its publicly disclosed core business model remains concentrated on:

**Membership + Merchandise Value + Warehouse Efficiency**

Advertising is not disclosed as a major standalone revenue engine comparable with large retail-media platforms.

This difference matters for Group 3 comparison.

## 62. Distribution Control Versus Marketplace Breadth

Costco chooses a relatively narrow assortment.

That means it gives up potential revenue from many products it does not carry.

In exchange, it gains greater control over:

- Purchasing concentration
- Selection
- Quality
- Pricing
- Inventory velocity

This is a deliberate architecture.

Growth does not require carrying everything.

---

## 63. Supplier Relationship

Costco's scale creates substantial purchasing power.

However, Costco still depends on suppliers for:

- Product availability
- Quality
- Costs
- Manufacturing
- Private-label production

Its limited-SKU model can also make individual supplier relationships operationally important.

Distribution control does not eliminate supply dependency.

---

## 64. Pricing Power Reversed

Traditional pricing power is often described as:

**Ability to Raise Price Without Losing Demand**

Costco's architecture suggests another form:

**Ability to Restrain Price and Convert Trust Into Volume and Renewal**

The company can choose not to maximize merchandise margin if lower pricing improves the overall membership system.

This is a different form of value capture.

---

## 65. Membership Fee as Value Capture

Costco can create consumer surplus through low merchandise prices and capture part of the resulting relationship through:

**Membership Fees**

The architecture can be simplified as:

**Deliver Product Value**

→ **Build Member Trust**

→ **Charge for Access to the System**

This differs from retailers that attempt to capture most economic value through product markup.

## 70. Brand Versus Membership

Costco illustrates an important distinction.

Membership creates:

**Contractual Recurrence**

Brand creates:

**Behavioral Trust**

Membership can force an annual decision.

It cannot force renewal.

Brand and value determine whether that annual decision remains favorable.

The two mechanisms therefore reinforce each other.

---

## 71. Membership Versus Loyalty Program

A conventional loyalty program may reward a customer after shopping.

Costco reverses the relationship.

The customer generally pays first.

Then gains access to the purchasing environment.

The membership itself becomes a product.

This creates a higher commitment threshold than a free loyalty account.

---

**Member**

→ **Higher-Spending Member**

→ **Executive Upgrade**

→ **Reward**

→ **Potentially More Consolidated Spending**

This allows Costco to segment its most economically active members without creating a separate retail brand.

New warehouses create both:

- New physical distribution
- New membership-acquisition opportunities

The unit therefore acts as both store and local membership network node.

---

## 74. Warehouse as Acquisition Infrastructure

A new Costco warehouse does more than add selling space.

It can create a new local membership market.

The sequence can become:

**Warehouse Opening**

→ **Membership Sign-Ups**

→ **Recurring Local Traffic**

→ **Sales Volume**

→ **Renewals**

This differs from a store that only generates transactional footfall.

---

## 75. Geographic Replication

Costco has replicated its membership warehouse format across multiple countries.

However, the model is not necessarily identical in every market.

Differences can include:

- Membership pricing
- Executive benefits
- Product assortment
- Fuel availability
- E-commerce
- Consumer behavior
- Regulation

International growth therefore requires adaptation while preserving core economics.

---

## 76. Repeat Purchase Versus Customer Acquisition

Costco requires new members to grow.

But the high renewal rate means much of the operating base does not need to be reacquired annually from zero.

The company can concentrate on:

**Retain Existing Members**

plus:

**Add New Members**

rather than replacing the whole customer base each year.

This is one of the economic advantages of recurring membership.

---

## 77. Retention Before Monetization Expansion

Costco's architecture is notable because it does not appear designed to monetize every possible interaction.

The strategic priority is maintaining member value.

That discipline matters.

A company can destroy membership economics if it maximizes short-term extraction at the expense of the reason customers originally joined.

Costco's low-price philosophy constrains its own monetization behavior.

---

## 78. Monetization Restraint as Strategy

This creates a paradox.

Costco can possess pricing power while deliberately not using all of it at the merchandise level.

Why?

Because the system optimizes:

**Lifetime Membership Relationship**

rather than:

**Maximum Gross Margin on Today's Basket**

For ORVESTRA, this is one of the most important characteristics of Costco.

## 81. Structural Constraints

Costco faces several structural constraints.

### Low Margin

Small movements in merchandise cost and operating expenses can materially affect earnings.

### Member Value

Membership renewal depends on maintaining perceived economic value.

### Supplier Cost

Cost increases can pressure the choice between margin and pricing.

### Limited Selection

Assortment discipline creates efficiency but sacrifices breadth.

### Physical Expansion

Warehouse growth requires real estate and capital.

### Digital Competition

Customers increasingly expect convenience alongside value.

### Renewal Mix

Digitally acquired and promotional memberships may have somewhat different renewal characteristics.

### International Replication

Membership behavior and retail economics vary by geography.

## 82. Growth System

Costco's core growth system can be simplified as:

**Low Prices**  
→ create member value

**Member Value**  
→ supports membership sign-ups and renewal

**More Members**  
→ increase sales volume

**Higher Sales Volume**  
→ increases purchasing scale

**Purchasing Scale**  
→ supports lower costs

**Lower Costs**  
→ support low prices

**Low Prices**  
→ increase shopping frequency

**Higher Shopping Frequency**  
→ increases inventory turnover

**Rapid Inventory Turnover**  
→ improves operating efficiency

**Operating Efficiency**  
→ supports the low-price model

This is a deeply reinforcing economic loop.

## 83. Executive Growth Loop

A second loop exists:

**Member Spending**

→ makes Executive Membership attractive

**Executive Upgrade**

→ creates 2% reward

**More Qualifying Spending**

→ creates larger reward

**Higher Perceived Membership Value**

→ supports renewal

Executive members represented approximately 75 percent of worldwide net sales in Q3 FY2026.

The tier therefore concentrates highly economically significant relationships.

## 84. Kirkland Growth Loop

Another reinforcing loop is:

**Member Trust in Costco**

→ encourages Kirkland trial

**Kirkland Value**

→ strengthens member trust

**Greater Kirkland Penetration**

→ creates differentiation

**Differentiation**

→ makes Costco membership more unique

The private label therefore strengthens both merchandise economics and membership utility.

## 85. Digital Growth Loop

Costco can also extend the system digitally:

**Membership**

→ **Digital Account**

→ **Online Convenience**

→ **More Purchasing Occasions**

→ **Greater Membership Utility**

The digital channel therefore does not need to replace the warehouse.

It can increase the number of ways the existing member relationship produces value.

## 86. Apple Versus Costco

Group 3 already reveals two different repeat-purchase architectures.

### Apple

**Product → Ecosystem → Services → Replacement**

### Costco

**Membership → Value → Frequency → Renewal**

Apple increases retention by making multiple products and services more useful together.

Costco increases retention by repeatedly demonstrating that the annual access fee produces economic value.

Both create recurring relationships.

The mechanisms are fundamentally different.

Costco's brand power supports:

It can produce:

or:

**Higher Frequency**

or:

**Higher Retention**

depending on business architecture.

## 88. Membership as Product

Costco demonstrates that membership is not merely a financing mechanism.

**A Commercial System**

The system includes:

The real product is therefore broader than the card itself.

## 89. The Scarce Asset

Costco does not win by providing maximum product abundance.

It deliberately limits assortment.

Its scarce asset is closer to:

**Trusted Access to Selected Value**

The company performs much of the selection and purchasing work before the member arrives.

That curation allows scale to concentrate.

## 90. ORVESTRA Structural Observation

Costco's most important competitive advantage may not be membership revenue by itself.

Any retailer can create a paid membership program.

The difficult part is creating a retail system good enough that customers voluntarily renew the right to keep using it.

Costco aligns:

**Membership**

**Low Prices**

**Purchasing Scale**

**Limited Assortment**

**Rapid Inventory Turnover**

**Private Label**

**Ancillary Services**

**Frequent Traffic**

into one reinforcing structure.

Removing one element changes the economics of the others.

Membership without low-price credibility would weaken renewal.

Low prices without sufficient scale would weaken profitability.

Large scale without assortment discipline would reduce purchasing concentration.

Private label without customer trust would weaken differentiation.

The advantage is therefore architectural rather than isolated.

---

## 91. What Makes This Business Interesting

Costco is particularly useful for studying:

**Value Capture Through Restraint**

The company could theoretically attempt to increase merchandise margins more aggressively.

But that can damage the very perception that makes membership valuable.

The system instead allows Costco to capture value through:

**Recurring Membership**

while using low merchandise prices to reinforce the reason for membership.

This means:

**Not maximizing one transaction can maximize the relationship.**

That is a very different business architecture from conventional margin optimization.

---

## 92. Group 3 Relevance

The central Group 3 question is:

**What makes the customer come back, and who controls that repeat relationship?**

For Costco, the answer is:

**Membership + Price Trust + Frequency + Exclusive Value**

The annual membership creates a formal recurring relationship.

But the fee is not sufficient by itself.

Renewal depends on the member continuing to believe that Costco delivers more value than the membership costs.

That makes every warehouse visit part of the renewal process.

## 93. Analytical Limits

As of 10 September 2026, Costco had completed fiscal 2026 but had not yet published its Q4 and full-year fiscal 2026 financial statements.

The $297.3 billion fiscal 2026 net-sales figure is drawn from Costco's August monthly sales release and should not be interpreted as the complete fiscal 2026 earnings report.

The latest full annual audited financial analysis used here is fiscal 2025.

The latest quarterly financial statement used here is Q3 fiscal 2026.

Reported membership counts include a small number of memberships expired within the previous 12 months under Costco's disclosed methodology.

Paid memberships are not equivalent to unique customers or active shoppers.

Cardholders are not equivalent to paid memberships.

Renewal rate uses Costco's trailing methodology and should not be interpreted as immediate-period renewal.

Executive membership sales penetration should not be interpreted as percentage of members.

Membership fee revenue should not be treated as profit.

Gross margin is based on merchandise net sales and is affected by category mix, gasoline prices, LIFO, pricing decisions, foreign exchange, and other factors.

E-commerce net sales and digitally enabled sales use different definitions.

Shopping-frequency and traffic measures should not be interpreted as unique-customer counts.

Kirkland Signature generally earns higher margins according to Costco, but Costco does not publicly disclose a standalone Kirkland revenue or gross-margin figure in its annual report.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Customer lifetime value
- Membership customer acquisition cost
- Membership-level profitability
- Executive-member profitability
- Kirkland revenue
- Kirkland standalone margin
- Member-level average spend
- Membership churn beyond disclosed renewal measures
- Warehouse-level return on capital
- Future market share
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

---

## 94. Conclusion

Costco demonstrates one of the clearest membership business architectures in consumer retail.

The customer pays for access.

Costco then has to continually justify that payment.

It does so through:

**Low Prices**

**Limited Assortment**

**Purchasing Scale**

**Rapid Inventory Turnover**

**Kirkland Signature**

**Warehouse Efficiency**

**Fuel and Ancillary Services**

**Digital Convenience**

The membership fee changes the economics of the system.

Costco does not need to extract maximum margin from every merchandise transaction.

It can deliberately preserve consumer value because part of the economic return appears through the continuing paid relationship.

That restraint helps build price trust.

Price trust supports repeat visits.

Repeat visits create volume.

Volume strengthens purchasing power.

Purchasing power helps maintain low prices.

Low prices help justify renewal.

Executive Membership deepens this mechanism by connecting qualifying spending with rewards.

Kirkland creates merchandise that cannot be directly replicated through competing retailers.

Gasoline and consumables create frequent reasons to return.

Digital distribution increases convenience without replacing the warehouse architecture.

By Q3 fiscal 2026, Costco reported 82.9 million paid memberships and 148.5 million cardholders.

Renewal remained above 92 percent in the U.S. and Canada.

Executive members represented approximately three-quarters of worldwide net sales.

By early September 2026, the physical network had expanded to 939 warehouses.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**Costco's membership fee is not the moat by itself. The moat is the operating system that makes millions of customers decide, year after year, that paying for continued access is still economically rational.**
