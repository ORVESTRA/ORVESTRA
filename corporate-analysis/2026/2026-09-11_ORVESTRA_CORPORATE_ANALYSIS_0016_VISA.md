## 10. The Four-Party Model

A typical Visa consumer-to-business transaction involves four principal parties:

1. Consumer
2. Issuer
3. Merchant
4. Acquirer

Visa sits between the financial institutions.

The simplified sequence is:

**Consumer**

→ presents Visa credential to merchant

**Merchant**

→ sends transaction to acquirer

**Acquirer**

→ sends transaction through Visa

**Visa**

→ routes transaction to issuer

**Issuer**

→ decides whether to authorize

Visa therefore coordinates a transaction between institutions that often have no direct technical relationship with each other.

[S1]

---

## 11. Issuer

The issuer is generally the financial institution that provides the customer's payment product.

Depending on product type, the issuer may provide:

- Credit
- Debit access
- Prepaid account access

The issuer generally controls:

- Customer account
- Credit decision
- Account balance
- Consumer relationship
- Account-level risk

Visa does not replace the issuer.

It connects the issuer to the broader acceptance network.

---

## 12. Acquirer

The acquirer supports the merchant's ability to accept payments.

The acquirer can be:

- A bank
- A payment processor
- Another acquiring institution

The acquirer generally handles the merchant-side relationship and submits transaction information into the payment network.

Visa connects the acquirer to the issuer.

---

## 13. Authorization

Authorization answers the question:

**Can this transaction proceed?**

VisaNet sends the transaction request toward the issuer.

The issuer checks factors that can include:

- Available balance
- Credit line
- Account status
- Fraud signals
- Other controls

The transaction is then:

**Approved**

or:

**Declined**

Visa provides critical routing and related technology.

The issuer generally makes the account-level authorization decision.

---

## 14. Clearing

Clearing is the process through which transaction information is exchanged and financial obligations are calculated after authorization.

The network must determine:

- Which issuer owes funds
- Which acquirer should receive funds
- Transaction values
- Applicable network information

Clearing converts millions of individual payment instructions into structured obligations between participants.

---

## 15. Settlement

Settlement is the stage where the resulting financial obligations are funded.

Visa operates systems supporting settlement between participating financial institutions.

Most U.S. dollar settlements with Visa clients settle within the same day.

Settlements involving other currencies generally remain outstanding for approximately one to two business days.

This can create temporary:

- Settlement receivables
- Settlement payables

on Visa's balance sheet.

[S1]

---

## 16. Settlement Does Not Mean Visa Funds the Consumer

Visa's involvement in settlement should not be confused with consumer lending.

Visa does not generally advance the consumer's credit balance.

The issuer provides the underlying funding relationship.

Visa's role is primarily:

**Network Coordination + Processing + Settlement Infrastructure**

not:

**Consumer Credit Provision**

---

## 17. Settlement Risk

Visa does have some network-related settlement exposure.

Under Visa's operating rules, the company indemnifies participating financial institutions for certain settlement losses if another participant fails to fund its obligations.

Visa therefore has:

**Settlement Risk**

even though it does not generally carry:

**Consumer Credit Risk**

These are different forms of financial exposure.

[S1]

---

## 18. Interchange

Another critical distinction is:

**Visa does not earn interchange reimbursement fees as its own revenue.**

In a typical transaction, interchange is generally paid:

**Acquirer → Issuer**

Visa establishes default interchange reimbursement rates in some circumstances.

But those rates are separate from Visa's own network revenue.

[S1]

---

## 19. Merchant Discount Rate

The merchant discount rate is also different from Visa revenue.

The merchant's acquirer generally determines the fees charged to the merchant.

The merchant may receive:

**Purchase Amount**

minus:

**Merchant Discount Rate**

Visa separately charges applicable network and service fees to participating clients.

Therefore:

**Merchant Fee ≠ Visa Revenue**

---

## 20. Why This Boundary Matters

A common misunderstanding is:

**Merchant Pays 2-3% → Visa Receives 2-3%.**

That is structurally incorrect.

A merchant payment can contain economics distributed among:

- Issuer
- Acquirer
- Processor
- Payment network
- Other service providers

Visa captures only the portions related to its own network and service architecture.

## 32. Why a Simple Take Rate Fails

It may appear tempting to calculate:

**Visa Net Revenue / Visa Payments Volume**

and call the result a take rate.

That can be misleading because:

- Service revenue uses prior-quarter volume
- Cross-border economics differ from domestic economics
- Processed transaction revenue depends on transaction count
- Value-added services can be sold separately
- Client incentives reduce revenue
- Some Visa-branded transactions are processed elsewhere
- Visa Direct and other money-movement flows use different economics

Therefore Visa does not have one directly disclosed universal transaction take rate.

---

## 33. Transaction Count Versus Transaction Value

Visa benefits from two different dimensions.

### Transaction Value

Important for areas such as service revenue and cross-border economics.

### Transaction Count

Important for processing economics.

A network can therefore grow even if:

**Average Ticket Remains Flat**

when:

**Transaction Count Increases**

Likewise, higher payment value can increase economics even without equivalent transaction growth.

- Acquirers

- Processors

**One Standardized Network Relationship**

## 38. Network Effect

Visa's network has a strong two-sided reinforcement mechanism.

**More Payment Credentials**

→ make Visa acceptance more valuable to merchants.

**More Merchant Acceptance**

→ makes Visa credentials more useful to consumers and issuers.

The loop becomes:

**Issuers**

→ **Credentials**

→ **Consumers**

→ **Merchant Demand**

→ **Acceptance**

→ **Greater Credential Utility**

## 39. Network Effect Is Not Exclusivity

The network effect should not be interpreted as exclusive lock-in.

A consumer can carry:

- Visa
- Mastercard
- Other payment credentials

A merchant can accept multiple networks.

A bank can participate in several networks.

Payments therefore exhibit substantial:

**Multi-Homing**

Visa's advantage comes from ubiquity and utility rather than requiring exclusivity.

## 136. Analytical Limits

Visa is a payment-network company and its operational metrics require strict measurement separation.

Payments volume should not be interpreted as Visa revenue or assets.

Total payment credentials should not be interpreted as unique customers.

Merchant locations should not be interpreted as unique merchants.

Visa-branded transactions should not be treated as identical to Visa-processed transactions.

Processed transactions include payment and cash transactions under Visa's disclosed definition.

Visa does not earn the interchange reimbursement fee as its own revenue.

Merchant discount rates should not be interpreted as Visa revenue.

Service revenue is primarily based on prior-quarter payments volume and therefore does not align temporally with current-quarter payment-volume growth.

Cross-border volume growth should not be converted directly into international transaction revenue growth.

Value-added services revenue is already contained within multiple Visa revenue categories and should not be added again to net revenue.

Visa Direct transactions should not be treated as standard merchant-purchase transactions.

Visa Direct endpoint capacity should not be interpreted as unique active recipients.

Client incentives are reductions to reported revenue under Visa's accounting architecture and are economically significant.

Settlement receivables and payables should not be interpreted as consumer loans.

Visa's settlement guarantees create settlement exposure but are distinct from issuer consumer-credit exposure.

Payment-network switching costs should not be interpreted as exclusive client lock-in because banks, merchants and consumers can use multiple networks.

The stablecoin settlement figure cited for fiscal 2025 was an annualized run rate and should not be treated as full-year GAAP revenue or realized annual settlement volume.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Visa's universal transaction take rate
- Per-transaction profit
- Merchant-level economics
- Issuer profitability
- Acquirer profitability
- Visa Direct standalone revenue
- VisaNet standalone profitability
- Stablecoin standalone profitability
- Client-specific incentive economics
- Customer lifetime value
- Future market share
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.
