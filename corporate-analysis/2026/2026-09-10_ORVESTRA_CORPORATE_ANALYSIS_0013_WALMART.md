## 1. Analytical Question

What makes customers continue returning to Walmart when they can increasingly purchase the same or similar products through many physical and digital channels?

A second question follows:

How does Walmart convert a historically store-based low-price retail system into an omnichannel commercial architecture combining:

- Physical stores
- Grocery frequency
- Digital fulfillment
- Membership
- Marketplace
- Advertising
- Fulfillment services
- Customer data

without weakening the price trust that originally created the customer relationship?

The emerging architecture can be simplified as:

**Everyday Low Price**

→ **Customer Trust**

→ **High Purchase Frequency**

→ **Store Traffic and Digital Demand**

→ **Fulfillment Density**

→ **Lower Delivery Friction**

→ **Membership and Marketplace Growth**

→ **Advertising and Service Revenue**

→ **Stronger Omnichannel Economics**

The physical store is therefore no longer only a place where merchandise is sold.

It increasingly acts as:

**Store + Inventory Node + Fulfillment Node + Customer Access Point**

## 7. Retail Architecture

Walmart's traditional operating proposition is built around:

**Everyday Low Price**

and:

**Everyday Low Cost**

Walmart defines Everyday Low Price as a pricing philosophy designed to give customers confidence that prices remain low without depending heavily on frequent promotional cycles.

Everyday Low Cost supports that system through operating efficiency.

The basic architecture is:

**Lower Operating Cost**

→ **Lower Customer Price**

→ **Higher Volume**

→ **Greater Scale**

→ **Lower Unit Cost**

[S1]

## 8. Price Trust

Walmart's brand power differs materially from Apple's.

Apple can use brand strength to support premium pricing.

Walmart uses brand strength to support:

**Confidence in Value**

The customer proposition is:

**I can return here and expect competitive everyday pricing across a broad basket.**

This reduces the need for Walmart to win every individual product comparison independently.

The broader basket relationship matters.

---

## 9. Brand Power as Reduced Search Cost

A strong value brand can reduce customer search friction.

The customer does not need to compare:

- Groceries
- Household products
- Pharmacy
- Electronics
- Apparel
- Consumables

across several retailers before every purchase.

If Walmart maintains price credibility, the customer can consolidate purchasing.

Brand therefore creates economic value by reducing:

**Decision Cost**

as well as:

**Purchase Cost**

## 110. Analytical Limits

Walmart operates across multiple business models and geographic markets.

A single retail metric cannot represent the entire company.

Fiscal 2026 membership fee revenue includes multiple membership offerings and should not be interpreted as Walmart+ revenue.

Walmart does not publicly disclose a standardized current Walmart+ member count in its SEC financial statements.

Walmart+ fee-revenue growth should not be converted into an unsupported subscriber count.

Membership and other income includes items beyond membership fees.

Sam's Club membership economics should not be treated as equivalent to Walmart+ economics.

Global advertising revenue includes VIZIO for the applicable period and should not be treated as Walmart Connect U.S. revenue alone.

Advertising arrangements can be recorded either as net sales or as a reduction of cost of sales depending on the arrangement.

Marketplace sales growth should not be interpreted as consolidated Walmart net-sales growth.

Marketplace GMV or seller activity is not equivalent to Walmart first-party net sales.

Walmart Fulfillment Services utilization measures should be interpreted according to Walmart's disclosed reporting context.

E-commerce figures differ between:

- Reported net sales
- Comparable-sales contribution
- Growth rates
- Digital sales mix

and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Store-fulfilled delivery growth is not equivalent to total eCommerce growth.

The 95 percent U.S. household delivery-reach statement represents geographic service capability and should not be interpreted as guaranteed delivery of every product within three hours.

Q2 FY2027 gross profit and operating income were affected by approximately $2.9 billion of tariff refunds and related price investments.

Therefore quarterly margin improvement should not be treated as entirely structural.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Walmart+ membership count
- Walmart+ churn
- Walmart+ customer lifetime value
- Walmart+ standalone profitability
- Walmart Connect standalone margin
- Marketplace standalone profitability
- Fulfillment Services standalone profitability
- Customer acquisition cost
- Store-level digital economics
- Delivery cost per order
- Advertising customer-level economics
- Future market share
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

## 111. Conclusion

Walmart entered digital commerce with an asset that initially appeared to belong to an older retail system:

**Thousands of physical stores.**

Those stores are increasingly becoming one of the central assets of Walmart's digital architecture.

Inventory already positioned close to customers can support:

**Shopping**

**Pickup**

**Delivery**

**Express Delivery**

The same customer relationship can then support:

**Walmart+**

**Marketplace**

**Advertising**

**Fulfillment**

This creates a layered value-capture system.

Walmart's original commercial promise remains important.

Customers still need to trust Walmart on price.

But price alone is no longer the complete proposition.

Walmart increasingly combines:

**Price**

with:

**Proximity**

**Speed**

**Convenience**

**Membership**

**Assortment**

The result is a different form of consumer ecosystem from Apple and a different membership architecture from Costco.

Apple uses accumulated ecosystem utility to encourage retention.

Costco uses paid access and price trust.

Walmart uses extremely frequent household demand and turns physical proximity into omnichannel convenience.

Its higher-margin adjacent businesses then create new economic value around that repeat demand.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**Walmart's most important distribution advantage may be that the infrastructure built to make customers drive to Walmart can increasingly make Walmart come to the customer instead.**

And once that distribution relationship exists, Walmart can capture value not only from what the customer buys, but increasingly from the membership, marketplace, fulfillment, and advertising systems surrounding the purchase.
