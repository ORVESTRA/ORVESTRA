## 1. Analytical Question

Where does Salesforce capture value as enterprise software moves from human-operated SaaS applications toward AI agents that can read data, reason across systems, execute workflows, and consume software without requiring a traditional user interface for every action?

A second question follows:

If frontier models become increasingly interchangeable, can Salesforce preserve economic value by owning the enterprise data, permissions, workflows, APIs, business logic, and systems of record that agents need in order to perform useful work?

---

Its business architecture has expanded into a broader enterprise operating environment spanning:

- Sales
- Customer service
- Marketing
- Commerce
- Data
- Integration
- Analytics
- Collaboration
- Workflow
- APIs
- AI agents
- Security and governance
- Application development

Its major platform assets now include:

- Salesforce CRM
- Agentforce
- Data 360
- Slack
- MuleSoft
- Tableau
- Informatica
- Hyperforce
- Developer APIs
- AppExchange
- Enterprise metadata and workflow infrastructure

This creates a commercial architecture broader than:

**User → SaaS Application → Subscription**

Salesforce is increasingly attempting to support:

**User + Agent → Data + Workflow + Action → Subscription + Consumption**

## 9. Remaining Performance Obligation

Salesforce reported total remaining performance obligation of:

**$72.4 billion**

at 31 January 2026.

At 31 July 2026, reported RPO was:

**$66.3 billion**

with current RPO of:

**$33.5 billion.**

RPO represents contracted revenue that has not yet been recognized.

It should not be interpreted as current revenue.

It is also influenced by:

- Seasonality
- Contract timing
- Renewals
- Contract duration
- Foreign exchange
- Acquisitions

Therefore changes between quarterly RPO balances should not automatically be interpreted as equivalent changes in underlying business demand.

## 11. System-of-Record Position

Salesforce's strongest enterprise position is not simply its graphical CRM interface.

For many organizations, Salesforce contains authoritative operational information such as:

- Accounts
- Contacts
- Leads
- Opportunities
- Cases
- Customer history
- Sales activity
- Service activity
- Marketing relationships
- Commerce information
- Custom business objects

These records sit directly inside business processes.

This gives Salesforce a structurally important position:

**System of Record**

AI may change the user interface.

The underlying operational data can remain essential.

## 12. Why System of Record Matters to AI

An AI model can generate language without knowing an organization's actual business state.

Useful enterprise agents require context.

That context may include:

- Which customer is involved
- What contract exists
- What opportunity is open
- Which case is unresolved
- What permissions apply
- What action is allowed
- Which workflow should run
- What happened previously

Salesforce already stores or connects to many of these signals.

Therefore enterprise AI creates potential value migration toward:

**Trusted Operational Context**

not only toward:

**Frontier Model Intelligence**

## 14. Data as Agent Infrastructure

Data 360 becomes more important as Agentforce expands.

An agent that does not have reliable enterprise context may produce useful language but unreliable business action.

The agent needs access to:

**Data**

plus:

**Identity**

plus:

**Permissions**

plus:

**Workflow**

plus:

**Business Rules**

Salesforce's architecture therefore attempts to place Data 360 underneath Agentforce as a context layer.

The model performs reasoning.

The data environment determines what the reasoning is about.

## 16. Zero Copy

Zero Copy is structurally important because enterprise data does not always need to be physically moved into Salesforce before becoming usable.

Instead, Salesforce can connect to external data environments.

This changes the architecture from:

**Move All Data Into Salesforce**

toward:

**Allow Salesforce Workflows and Agents to Use Data Where It Already Resides**

This reduces one barrier to integration.

It also allows Salesforce to increase relevance without requiring complete ownership of the customer's data estate.

## 23. From Seats to Actions

Traditional enterprise SaaS commonly monetizes:

**User → Seat → Monthly Subscription**

Agents complicate this model.

One employee may operate many agents.

One agent may perform thousands of actions.

A background process may continue even when no human is actively using the software.

The monetization unit therefore begins migrating toward:

**Action**

**Conversation**

**Consumption**

**Workflow Execution**

Salesforce is explicitly building pricing structures around this transition.

## 24. Flex Credits

Salesforce Agentforce offers consumption-based pricing through Flex Credits.

As of September 2026, the standard published structure includes:

**$500 per 100,000 Flex Credits**

with a standard Agentforce action generally consuming:

**20 Flex Credits**

under the applicable standard rate card.

Salesforce also offers different multipliers for certain usage categories.

Pricing terms can change and contractual pricing may vary.

The structural importance is that Salesforce can monetize:

**Machine Work Performed**

rather than only:

**Human Access to Software**

Salesforce can attempt to make:

**The Foundation Model**

more replaceable

while keeping:

**CRM Data + Workflow + Permissions + Integration + Governance**

persistent.

If frontier models become increasingly substitutable, value may migrate away from exclusive model access.

Salesforce is positioned to capture value from the enterprise environment surrounding the model.

## 32. Compute Cost

Salesforce faces AI compute cost differently from Microsoft or Alphabet.

Microsoft and Alphabet own hyperscale cloud infrastructure and invest directly in large AI compute estates.

Salesforce increasingly operates its software using a mixture of:

- Salesforce-operated infrastructure
- Public cloud infrastructure
- Third-party model providers
- Third-party technology services

Salesforce's annual report states that subscription and support cost includes:

- Data-center capacity
- Service-delivery expenses
- Third-party technology
- Third-party services

Therefore AI adoption can increase compute-related economic exposure even when Salesforce does not own the underlying accelerator infrastructure.

[S1]

**Amazon Web Services**

## 34. Compute Without Hyperscale Ownership

This creates a different economic structure from Group 2 companies that own major cloud infrastructure.

Salesforce can benefit from hyperscaler:

- Compute capacity
- Networking
- Geographic infrastructure
- Hardware deployment
- Data-center operations

without needing to build all underlying physical infrastructure itself.

But this also means Salesforce depends partly on external infrastructure economics.

The architecture becomes:

**Salesforce Owns Workflow and Application Layer**

while:

**External Providers Supply Significant Physical Compute**

**AI Revenue**

**Model + Compute + Data + Infrastructure Cost**

## 40. Headless Platform

**Headless Platform**

**Underlying Business Capability**

**Specific User Interface**

- Custom application

## 41. AI Disruption to CRM Interfaces

Traditional CRM requires employees to manually:

- Enter records
- Update opportunities
- Search accounts
- Write notes
- Create reports
- Review cases
- Manage pipelines

AI agents can automate parts of this work.

That creates a potential threat to conventional seat-based CRM activity.

If less human interaction is required, the economic justification for charging solely per human seat may weaken.

Salesforce's consumption pricing is therefore partly a response to its own AI disruption.

---

## 42. From User Interface to Workflow

The important strategic question is not whether employees continue clicking through Salesforce screens.

It is:

**Does the business workflow still execute through Salesforce infrastructure?**

An agent might update an opportunity without a sales representative manually opening the record.

A service agent might resolve a case without a human navigating Service Cloud.

The interface changes.

The system of record may remain Salesforce.

This creates a path for Salesforce to survive interface disruption.

---

## 43. Workflow Ownership

Salesforce already sits inside high-value enterprise workflows including:

- Lead management
- Sales pipeline
- Customer service
- Marketing
- Commerce
- Customer data
- Integration
- Analytics

Agentforce attempts to turn these workflows into environments where agents can perform work.

This moves Salesforce from:

**Software Used to Manage Work**

toward:

**Software That Can Perform Parts of the Work**

That is a substantial value migration.

## 44. Digital Labor Economics

Salesforce frequently describes Agentforce through a digital-labor framework.

The useful analytical distinction is not whether an AI agent is literally equivalent to an employee.

It is that software consumption can increasingly scale independently of employee count.

Traditional SaaS:

**More Employees → More Seats**

Agentic software:

**Same Employees → More Agent Actions**

This can expand the economic ceiling of enterprise software.

---

## 45. Human Seat Cannibalization

The same transition creates a risk.

If agents automate enough work, organizations may require fewer traditional software users for some workflows.

That can pressure seat expansion.

Salesforce therefore needs:

**Consumption Revenue Growth**

to offset any future reduction in:

**Human Seat Growth**

The company is effectively building the monetization model for a world where software activity may grow faster than human user count.

## 52. Application Owner Versus AI Interface Owner

Group 2 creates a major strategic contest.

OpenAI can attempt to become the general AI interface above enterprise applications.

Microsoft can embed AI inside productivity and infrastructure.

Google can embed AI across cloud and workspace.

Salesforce has another position:

**Own the Business Workflow and System of Record**

If the intelligence layer becomes commoditized, Salesforce may still control:

- Customer data
- Business rules
- Permissions
- Workflow
- Actions
- Enterprise context

This is the core defensive logic of Agentforce.

## 53. Model Provider Versus Workflow Owner

A foundation-model provider can deliver reasoning capability.

But it may not know:

- Which customer record is authoritative
- Which field can be modified
- Which discount is allowed
- Which user has approval
- Which workflow must execute
- Which compliance rule applies

The workflow owner controls this operational context.

Therefore economic value in enterprise AI may divide between:

**Model Intelligence**

and:

**Operational Authority**

Salesforce's strongest position is in the second category.

- Proprietary data

- Workflow
- Reliability

Salesforce is positioned across these layers.

This means Salesforce does not necessarily need to own the strongest foundation model to create a strong AI business.

## 57. Value Migration From Human Work to Machine Work

**Machine Work**

The company is beginning to meter work rather than only access.

## 74. Analytical Limits

Salesforce operates across many product categories.

A single SaaS metric cannot represent the entire company.

Subscription and support revenue includes multiple application, platform, integration, analytics, data, and software-license products.

Fiscal 2027 revenue categories differ from the presentation used in fiscal 2026.

Therefore product-level historical comparisons require care.

The Agentforce ARR definition was broadened in Q2 FY2027 and should not be treated as a perfectly unchanged historical measure.

Agentforce and Data 360 ARR should not be interpreted as total Salesforce AI revenue.

Agentic Work Units should not be interpreted as:

- Revenue
- Human-equivalent labor units
- Unique agents
- Completed customer outcomes

Data 360 record-ingestion figures should not be interpreted as unique customer records.

Flex Credit list pricing does not represent every customer contract.

RPO is contracted future revenue and should not be treated as current revenue.

Salesforce does not separately disclose:

- Agentforce profitability
- AI gross margin
- Flex Credit gross margin
- Cost per agent action
- Model-provider expenditure
- Data 360 standalone profitability
- Customer-specific switching costs

The Informatica acquisition contributes to reported growth and should be separated where material.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Agentforce standalone profitability
- Customer ROI
- AI labor substitution
- Future seat reduction
- Future market share
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

## 75. Conclusion

Salesforce entered the AI transition from a fundamentally different position than the model and infrastructure companies in Group 2.

It already owned important enterprise relationships.

It already contained customer records.

It already encoded workflows.

It already managed permissions.

It already exposed APIs.

It already connected thousands of enterprise applications.

AI therefore does not require Salesforce to invent an entirely new distribution system.

The company can place Agentforce into an existing operational environment.

But agentic software changes the economics of that environment.

Traditional SaaS monetizes people through seats.

Agentic software can monetize machines through actions.

Traditional applications require users to navigate screens.

Agents can interact directly with APIs and business logic.

Traditional CRM stores customer information.

Data 360 expands the context agents can use beyond CRM.

Traditional platform lock-in comes partly from data and customization.

Agentic lock-in may increasingly come from deployed workflows, policies, evaluations, actions, permissions, and connected enterprise systems.

Salesforce is therefore attempting to migrate its own architecture before AI weakens the traditional CRM interface.

Its strategy does not require Salesforce to own the best frontier model.

It requires Salesforce to own enough of the enterprise operating context that any useful model still needs to pass through Salesforce-controlled data, permissions, workflows, and actions.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**If foundation models become increasingly interchangeable, Salesforce may capture value by owning the enterprise context that tells the model what the business knows, what the agent is allowed to do, and where the action must be recorded.**
