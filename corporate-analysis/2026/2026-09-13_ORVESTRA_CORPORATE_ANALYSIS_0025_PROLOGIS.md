## 1. Analytical Question

How does Prologis capture economic value from logistics networks without operating the trucks, aircraft, trains, or delivery routes that move the goods?

A second question follows:

Where does scarce logistics capacity actually sit when the critical asset is not the transportation vehicle but:

**The Physical Location Where Inventory Must Be Positioned?**

Prologis occupies a fundamentally different position from the first four companies in Group 5.

UPS operates:

**Parcel Network Capacity**

FedEx operates:

**Integrated Air and Surface Capacity**

Union Pacific operates:

**Fixed Rail Corridor Capacity**

Delta operates:

**Perishable Airline Seat Capacity**

Prologis primarily provides:

**Logistics Location Capacity**

Its core architecture can therefore be simplified as:

**Land**

→ **Building**

→ **Lease**

→ **Inventory Position**

→ **Transportation Network Access**

→ **Customer Distribution Capability**

Prologis does not generally move the customer's goods.

It owns or manages the physical nodes from which those goods move.

## 3. Prologis Does Not Operate the Transportation Network

Prologis should not be analyzed as another UPS or FedEx.

It generally does not determine:

- Delivery routes
- Aircraft schedules
- Train paths
- Package sorting
- Truck dispatch

Its primary role is to provide:

**Physical Space in Locations Where Logistics Networks Need Space.**

The customer operates the logistics activity inside that space.

## 4. Location Is the Product

A logistics building has value partly because of:

- Distance to consumers
- Highway access
- Port access
- Intermodal access
- labor access
- surrounding industrial infrastructure

The same building placed in a different location can have very different economic value.

Therefore:

**Square Footage Alone Does Not Define Capacity Quality.**

---

## 5. Location Capacity

For Prologis, useful capacity can be expressed conceptually as:

**Building Space**

× **Location Utility**

× **Infrastructure Access**

A warehouse located far from:

- Demand
- highways
- ports
- labor

may provide large physical space but weak logistics utility.

A smaller facility near dense demand can create much greater operational value.

## 43. Location Changes Route Economics

Consider two distribution facilities.

### Facility A

Near consumers.

### Facility B

Far from consumers.

Even if both provide identical:

**Square Footage**

the customer's transport economics can differ materially.

Therefore the real-estate asset influences:

**Route Cost Outside the Building.**

---

## 44. Inventory Location Is Network Design

A logistics company must decide:

**Where Inventory Should Wait Before It Moves.**

If inventory is too far from demand:

- Delivery time rises
- route distance rises

If inventory is positioned too close to demand:

- Real estate can be more expensive
- facilities may be smaller
- land can be scarce

This creates a location trade-off.

## 166. Analytical Limits

Prologis metrics require strict measurement separation.

The approximately 1.3 billion square feet reported at Q2 2026 includes the broader platform of properties and development projects and should not be interpreted as stabilized occupied operating space.

The approximately 5,929 buildings reported in the company profile should not be treated as the stabilized operating-portfolio building count.

The Owned and Managed operating portfolio contained approximately 5,476 buildings and 1.222 billion square feet at Q2 2026.

Owned and Managed square footage should not be interpreted as 100 percent Prologis economic ownership.

Prologis Share represents proportionate economic ownership and is different from the broader Owned and Managed portfolio.

AUM should not be interpreted as Prologis consolidated assets.

Square footage should not be interpreted as logistics throughput.

Occupancy should not be interpreted as customer space utilization.

Leased percentage and occupied percentage measure different states.

Customer-reported Space Utilization is a proprietary survey measure and should not be treated as a physical measurement of inventory density or throughput.

Lease square footage signed and lease square footage commenced represent different leasing stages.

Net effective rent change should not be interpreted as portfolio-wide annual rent growth.

Cash rent change and net effective rent change use different methodologies.

Rental revenue should not be interpreted as logistics transaction volume.

Property NOI is not companywide net income.

Same Store Property NOI is a non-GAAP property operating measure.

Core FFO, AFFO, Adjusted EBITDA, NOI, development margin, development yield, and estimated value creation are non-GAAP or management-defined measures and should not be treated as GAAP net earnings.

Development square footage should not be interpreted as stabilized operating capacity.

Development starts should not be interpreted as completed buildings.

Development portfolio leased percentage should not be interpreted as physical occupancy.

Stabilization under Prologis' methodology can occur based on specified timing, ownership, or occupancy conditions and should not be simplified to physical construction completion alone.

Estimated land build-out should not be treated as existing warehouse capacity.

The approximately $40.6 billion broader Owned and Managed build-out TEI estimate is not committed construction spending.

Land ownership or contractual control does not guarantee entitlement, development, leasing, or stabilization.

The data-center power pipeline should not be treated as logistics warehouse capacity.

Advanced-stage power is not secured power.

Secured power should not automatically be treated as operating data-center capacity.

The 5.8 GW power pipeline should not be interpreted as power currently being consumed by operating Prologis data centers.

Amazon's 5.5 percent share of net effective rent should not be interpreted as its percentage of Prologis revenue, square footage, or customer activity.

Customer square footage should not be interpreted as customer logistics volume.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Warehouse throughput
- Packages per square foot
- Pallets per building
- Truck movements
- Tenant inventory turns
- Customer distribution cost savings
- Property-level customer profitability
- Route-level logistics savings attributable to Prologis
- Individual property profitability
- Market rent gap across the complete portfolio

- Future occupancy

- Data-center standalone profitability
- Future market share
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.
