## 1. Analytical Question

What makes a Nike customer come back?

A second question follows:

How does Nike convert global brand power into repeat purchasing while balancing:

- Direct distribution
- Wholesale partners
- Digital commerce
- Membership
- Sports communities
- Product innovation
- Scarcity
- Athlete relationships
- Full-price presentation

The central architecture can be simplified as:

**Sport**

→ **Brand Desire**

→ **Product**

→ **Distribution**

→ **Consumer Experience**

→ **Membership and Community**

→ **Repeat Purchase**

Nike therefore differs from the other Group 3 companies.

It does not primarily depend on:

- Paid membership
- Daily purchase frequency
- Hardware ecosystem lock-in

Its strongest mechanism is:

**Brand Relevance + Product Desire + Distribution Availability**

## 17. Supply as Brand Architecture

### Availability

### Scarcity

The optimal distribution strategy is not:

**Maximum Supply Everywhere**

**Appropriate Supply in Appropriate Channels**

## 20. Functional Replacement

Athletic footwear wears through use.

Running shoes, training shoes and other performance products have physical replacement cycles.

This creates recurring demand linked to:

**Actual Product Consumption**

The more active the athlete, the more frequently certain products may need replacement.

Nike does not publicly disclose one universal replacement cycle.

## 21. Lifestyle Repeat Purchase

A consumer may purchase another shoe because of:

- Style

- Collection
- Collaboration
- Cultural relevance

The existing shoe does not need to be worn out.

This creates:

**Portfolio Ownership**

rather than simple replacement.

## 47. Discounting and Brand Power

Discounting can increase short-term sell-through.

But persistent discounting creates risks.

Consumers may learn to:

- Wait for promotions
- Compare prices
- Avoid full-price launches

For a premium brand, this can weaken pricing architecture.

Nike's marketplace reset directly addresses this tension.

- Increased markdowns across NIKE Direct

- Product supply reduction

## 136. Analytical Limits

NIKE, Inc.'s fiscal 2026 year ended 31 May 2026.

As of 10 September 2026, fiscal 2026 Form 10-K and Q4 fiscal 2026 results are the latest full financial reporting used in this analysis.

NIKE Brand revenue and NIKE, Inc. consolidated revenue are different measures.

Jordan Brand revenue is included within NIKE Brand geographic segment revenue and should not be added again to NIKE Brand revenue.

Converse is reported separately.

NIKE Direct includes both:

- NIKE-owned retail stores
- NIKE Brand Digital

and should not be interpreted as digital sales alone.

NIKE Brand Digital sales should not be interpreted as total Nike eCommerce activity across third-party retailers.

Wholesale revenue represents Nike sales to wholesale customers and should not be interpreted as final consumer retail sales through those partners.

Nike Membership is free and should not be interpreted as subscription revenue.

Nike does not disclose a standardized current global membership count in the fiscal 2026 Form 10-K.

Nike does not publicly disclose:

- Membership profitability
- Member lifetime value
- Member versus non-member revenue
- Nike App standalone revenue
- SNKRS standalone revenue
- Nike Run Club standalone revenue
- Nike Training Club standalone revenue

Demand creation expense includes both brand marketing and sports marketing and should not be treated as pure advertising expense.

Fiscal 2026 reported gross margin was materially affected by the recognition of IEEPA tariff recoveries.

The 874 NIKE Brand-owned store count is calculated from Nike's disclosed U.S. and non-U.S. store tables and excludes Converse.

Nike's more than 5,000 football retail activation locations represent a specific 2026 marketplace activation rather than Nike's permanent wholesale-store count.

Brand value, cultural relevance, customer loyalty and consumer switching costs cannot be reduced to one financial metric.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Customer lifetime value
- Member churn
- Repeat-purchase frequency
- Brand valuation
- Direct-channel customer acquisition cost
- Wholesale retail sell-through not publicly disclosed
- Product-level gross margin
- Individual athlete endorsement ROI
- Future market share

## 137. Conclusion

Nike demonstrates that a powerful consumer relationship can exist without:

- Paid membership
- Daily transactions
- Technical ecosystem lock-in

Its relationship is built around:

**Sport**

**Brand**

**Product**

**Culture**

**Distribution**

**Community**

The consumer can leave easily.

That makes continued relevance essential.

Nike Membership and its digital applications help create a direct relationship.

But membership cannot replace product desire.

NIKE Direct gives the company more control over presentation, data and the transaction.

But direct distribution cannot replace wholesale reach.

Wholesale partners give Nike access to consumers and retail environments it does not own.

But wholesale expansion without supply discipline can weaken price integrity and brand presentation.

Fiscal 2026 illustrates this balance clearly.

NIKE Brand wholesale revenue increased to $27.453 billion.

NIKE Direct declined to $17.720 billion.

NIKE Brand Digital declined to $8.6 billion.

Nike responded not by abandoning direct distribution, but by repositioning digital as a full-price platform while reinvesting in wholesale.

At the same time, the company reduced supply of selected footwear, cleared excess inventory and increased investment around product, brand and sport.

The strategic objective is therefore larger than channel growth.

It is:

**Restore the Health of the Whole Marketplace.**

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**Nike's brand is strongest not when Nike owns every customer transaction, but when the brand remains desirable and available across the places where consumers actually discover, compare, experience and buy sport products.**

The deeper lesson is:

**Distribution control is not maximum channel ownership. It is the ability to design the right balance of owned and partner distribution without losing the customer relationship, pricing discipline or brand meaning.**

That makes Nike's repeat-purchase architecture fundamentally behavioral.

Customers are free to leave.

The business works when they continue choosing to return.
