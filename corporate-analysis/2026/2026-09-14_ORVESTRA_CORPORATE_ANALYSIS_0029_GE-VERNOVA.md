# 15. What Could Make the Current Model Less Necessary?

This is the Business Model Lab signature question.

GE Vernova's current model depends heavily on:

**Selling and Servicing Large, Long-Lived Electricity Infrastructure.**

Several structural changes could reduce the importance of parts of that model.

---

## 15.1 Distributed Generation Could Reduce Some Centralized Infrastructure Needs

If future electricity systems become significantly more distributed through combinations of:

- Local generation
- Batteries
- Microgrids
- Flexible demand

some traditional centralized generation or transmission requirements could change.

This would not eliminate electrical infrastructure.

It could change:

**Which Infrastructure Is Required.**

---

## 15.2 Long Duration Storage Could Reduce Some Gas Capacity Requirements

Gas turbines currently provide:

- Dispatchability
- flexibility
- reliability

If future storage systems can provide comparable grid services economically across longer durations, part of the need for incremental gas generation could decline.

This could reduce future:

**New Turbine Demand.**

The existing gas installed base could still generate service revenue for many years.

---

## 15.3 New Nuclear Architectures Could Change Generation Mix

If new nuclear deployment accelerates materially, some demand otherwise served by gas or other technologies could migrate.

GE Vernova is partly protected from this scenario because it also participates in nuclear technology.

This demonstrates the value of:

**Technology Diversification.**

---

## 15.4 Grid Equipment Could Become Less Scarce

Current transformer and grid-equipment markets have significant supply constraints.

If competitors substantially expand manufacturing capacity and lead times normalize:

**Scarcity Pricing Could Weaken.**

The market could move from:

**Capacity Shortage**

toward:

**More Normal Equipment Competition.**

---

## 15.5 Hardware Could Become More Standardized

If grid hardware becomes increasingly standardized and interoperable:

**Switching Cost Could Decline.**

Value might migrate toward:

- Project integration
- software
- financing
- network orchestration

GE Vernova would need to capture more value above the equipment layer.

---

## 15.6 Independent Service Could Reduce OEM Aftermarket Capture

If customers can maintain installed equipment through capable third-party providers:

**OEM Service Share Could Fall.**

This would weaken one of GE Vernova's strongest installed-base economics.

---

## 15.7 Gas Fleet Utilization Could Decline

A gas turbine can remain physically installed while operating fewer hours.

If:

- Renewable penetration
- storage
- nuclear
- demand patterns

reduce turbine utilization materially, some service requirements could change.

Installed-base count alone therefore does not guarantee constant lifetime economics.

---

## 15.8 Electricity Demand Growth Could Slow

Current manufacturing expansion assumes substantial long-term electricity-infrastructure demand.

If:

- Data-center growth
- industrial growth
- electrification

slow significantly, customers may require less new equipment than current backlog and capacity expansion plans imply.

---

## 15.9 Wind Could Remain Structurally Difficult

Wind demonstrates that large installed demand does not guarantee attractive manufacturer economics.

If:

- Project pricing remains weak
- supply costs rise
- policy uncertainty persists
- offshore project execution remains difficult

the Wind model could remain less economically attractive than Power or Electrification.

---

## 15.10 GE Vernova Could Make the Equipment-Sale Model Less Central Itself

The most important internal transition could be:

**Equipment Manufacturer**

→ **Lifecycle Energy Infrastructure Partner**

The economic unit could move from:

**Machine Delivered**

toward:

**Availability**

**Service Life**

**Power-System Performance**

**Grid Capacity**

**Long-Term Infrastructure Relationship**

The equipment would remain physically necessary.

But the initial hardware transaction could become:

**Only the Entry Point.**

## Contracted Gas Capacity

**53 GW**

## Slot Reservation Is Not Backlog

A slot reservation agreement represents customer commitment to manufacturing capacity under GE Vernova's disclosed commercial architecture.

It is not identical to:

**An Equipment Order in RPO.**

Therefore:

**116 GW Combined ≠ 116 GW Equipment Backlog.**

The correct Q2 state was approximately:

**53 GW Backlog + 63 GW Slot Reservations.**

---

## Gas Manufacturing Capacity

GE Vernova stated that it expected to reach:

**20 GW of annual gas turbine output in Q3 2026.**

It was targeting:

**24 GW in 2028**

and implementing actions intended to support:

**30 GW in 2030.**

[S3]

The 2028 and 2030 figures are future capacity plans.

They should not be treated as current production capability.

---

## Manufacturing Capacity Is Not Revenue Capacity

A factory capable of producing more turbines does not automatically produce:

- Customer orders
- shipments
- recognized revenue
- profit

The state sequence remains:

**Manufacturing Capacity**

→ **Order**

→ **Production**

→ **Delivery**

→ **Installation**

→ **Operation**

→ **Service**

Each state is distinct.

# Analytical Limits

GE Vernova metrics require strict measurement separation.

Total revenue should not be interpreted as equipment-only revenue.

Equipment revenue and services revenue represent different economic activities.

Services revenue should not be interpreted as recurring subscription revenue.

Long term service arrangements can vary substantially by equipment, customer, contract, geography, and duration.

Installed gas-turbine count should not be interpreted as currently operating turbine count.

Installed wind-turbine count should not be interpreted as current Wind revenue or current market share.

The company estimate that its technologies support approximately 25 percent of global electricity generation should not be interpreted as GE Vernova owning or operating 25 percent of global generation.

The generation assets are generally customer owned and operated.

RPO should not be interpreted as current revenue, cash, profit, or guaranteed margin.

Equipment RPO and services RPO have different recognition periods and execution requirements.

Slot reservation agreements should not be treated as equipment RPO.

The Q2 2026 116 GW Gas Power figure combined approximately 53 GW of equipment backlog with approximately 63 GW of slot reservation agreements.

The 116 GW figure should therefore not be described as 116 GW of contracted RPO backlog.

Future gas-turbine annual output targets of 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030 should not be treated as current manufacturing capacity.

The announced expectation of 20 GW annual gas-turbine output in Q3 2026 is a company manufacturing expectation and should not be interpreted as annual recognized revenue or annual delivered generation capacity.

A gas turbine's rated capacity should not be interpreted as actual electricity produced.

Generation depends on utilization, fuel, dispatch, maintenance, grid access, and operating conditions.

Electrification's Q2 2026 reported revenue growth includes the impact of the Prolec GE acquisition.

Reported growth should not be substituted for organic growth.

The Prolec GE acquisition closed on 2 February 2026.

Prolec GE was previously an unconsolidated joint venture.

The approximately $5.275 billion purchase price should not be interpreted as Prolec GE annual revenue or asset value.

Electrification equipment backlog should not be interpreted as currently installed grid capacity.

Transformer and switchgear orders require manufacturing and project execution before becoming operating infrastructure.

Wind orders should not be interpreted as turbine installations.

Wind sales units should not be interpreted as installed fleet additions until project and installation states are confirmed.

Wind installed-base service opportunity should not be interpreted as evidence of segment profitability.

Q2 2026 Wind segment EBITDA was negative.

Segment EBITDA is a management performance measure and should not be interpreted as GAAP operating income.

Adjusted EBITDA, organic revenue, organic EBITDA margin, and free cash flow are non-GAAP measures.

The $5 billion-plus year-to-date data-center order figure should not be described as AI-specific revenue or AI-specific orders.

Data centers can support several workloads.

GE Vernova does not disclose one standardized measure for:

- Turbine-level lifetime value
- Transformer-level gross margin
- Service margin by installed asset
- Revenue per installed gas turbine
- Revenue per installed wind turbine
- Data-center customer profitability
- Grid-project customer lifetime value
- Manufacturing utilization by factory
- Turbine utilization across the complete installed base
- Grid-equipment capacity utilization
- Power-project standalone return
- Wind-project standalone return

This analysis does not estimate:

1. Profit per turbine
2. Profit per transformer
3. Service customer lifetime value
4. Installed-base replacement rate
5. Gas-turbine utilization
6. Wind-turbine utilization
7. Factory utilization
8. Transformer manufacturing utilization
9. Long-term service standalone margin
10. Future data-center electricity demand
11. Future grid-equipment scarcity
12. Future generation mix
13. Future market share
14. Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

**Future Service Relationships.**

**$39.3 billion of equipment**

Electrification demonstrates another architecture.

**Transformers**

**Interconnection**

**Project Execution.**

**GE Vernova's durable business advantage is not simply its ability to manufacture turbines, wind equipment, or transformers.
