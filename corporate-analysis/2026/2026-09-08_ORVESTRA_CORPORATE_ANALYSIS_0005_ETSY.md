## 1. Analytical Question

Where does Etsy's marketplace power come from when the products, brands, inventory, production capabilities, and customer relationships of individual sellers remain largely outside Etsy's ownership?

A second question follows:

How durable is Etsy's position when sellers can operate through multiple marketplaces, social channels, physical venues, wholesale relationships, and their own independent commerce systems?

---

## 2. Structural Summary

Etsy operates a two-sided marketplace connecting independent sellers with buyers seeking unique and creative goods.

The underlying products are generally created, designed, sourced, curated, or supplied by independent sellers.

Etsy does not need to manufacture those products.

Its commercial role is to organize the systems around discovery and transaction.

Those systems include:

- Search
- Discovery
- Marketplace access
- Listing infrastructure
- Payments
- Reviews
- Messaging
- Advertising
- Trust and safety
- Seller tools
- Purchase protection
- Customer support
- Mobile applications
- External marketing distribution

This creates an asset-light marketplace in which Etsy owns relatively little of the underlying merchandise but controls important interfaces between seller supply and buyer demand.

---

## 3. Current Company Scope

Etsy's corporate structure has become simpler.

The company sold Reverb on 02 June 2025.

On 30 July 2026, Etsy completed the sale of Depop to eBay.

Following the Depop transaction, Etsy described the divestiture as enabling exclusive focus on the Etsy marketplace.

The transaction generated approximately $1.4 billion in cash consideration, consisting of a $1.2 billion purchase price plus approximately $200 million of net purchase price adjustments and interest.

For ORVESTRA, this creates an important analytical boundary.

Historical Etsy, Inc. consolidated financial results included multiple marketplaces.

Current business architecture is increasingly centered on the Etsy marketplace itself.

## 7. Marketplace Revenue Architecture

Etsy separates revenue into:

**Marketplace Revenue**

and:

**Services Revenue**

Marketplace Revenue primarily includes required marketplace fees such as:

- Transaction fees
- Payments processing fees
- Listing fees
- Offsite Advertising fees
- Other marketplace-related charges

Services Revenue primarily includes optional seller services such as:

- Etsy Ads
- Shipping labels
- Other value-added services

This distinction is important.

A seller can create revenue for Etsy simply by participating in the marketplace.

A seller can create additional revenue by purchasing services intended to increase sales or simplify operations.

|---|---:|

Services represented approximately 30.4 percent of total revenue.

The increase was primarily driven by higher advertising revenue, including a $62.2 million increase largely associated with higher average price per click on Etsy Ads.

This demonstrates that seller attention and marketplace visibility have become meaningful monetization layers.

## 9. Seller Fee Architecture

Etsy sellers may encounter several different fees.

### Listing Fee

The standard listing fee is:

**$0.20 per listing**

A listing generally remains active for four months unless sold or renewed.

### Transaction Fee

Etsy charges a standard transaction fee of:

**6.5 percent**

of the applicable order amount.

### Payment Processing

Etsy Payments processing fees vary by country.

Etsy states that these fees typically include:

- A percentage of the transaction
- A fixed amount per order

The percentage typically varies between approximately 3.0 percent and 6.5 percent depending on the seller's country.

### Currency Conversion

Additional fees may apply where currency conversion is required.

### Advertising

Additional fees may apply through:

- Etsy Ads
- Offsite Ads

Therefore:

**6.5 percent is not the seller's total potential economic cost of using Etsy.**

---

## 10. Offsite Ads

Offsite Ads create another value-capture layer.

Etsy purchases advertising across external channels including search engines and social platforms.

If a buyer clicks an eligible offsite advertisement and makes a qualifying purchase from the seller within the applicable attribution period, Etsy may charge an additional advertising fee.

The standard Offsite Ads fee structure currently includes:

- 15 percent for sellers below the applicable $10,000 sales threshold
- 12 percent for sellers that have reached the threshold

The fee is capped at $100 per applicable order.

For sellers that cross the $10,000 threshold during an applicable consecutive 365-day period, continued participation in Offsite Ads generally becomes mandatory.

This creates an unusual architecture.

Etsy funds demand acquisition upfront.

The seller pays Etsy when that advertising produces a qualifying transaction.

---

## 11. Take Rate

Etsy publicly reports a metric called:

**Revenue Take Rate**

It is defined as:

**Revenue divided by GMS**

For Q2 2026, reported take rate was:

**25.9 percent**

This increased approximately 130 basis points year over year.

Approximately 80 basis points of that increase reflected the effect of the Reverb divestiture on the comparative reporting structure.

For the Etsy marketplace itself, take-rate expansion was primarily associated with Etsy Ads and Offsite Ads.

The 25.9 percent measure should not be interpreted as one universal seller commission.

Revenue includes several different monetization mechanisms.

Therefore:

**Revenue Take Rate ≠ Standard Transaction Fee**

The standard transaction fee may be 6.5 percent while Etsy's broader economic capture around marketplace activity is significantly more layered.

## 12. Layered Seller Monetization

A seller can potentially generate revenue for Etsy through several stages.

**Listing**

Etsy may receive the listing fee.

**Transaction**

Etsy may receive the transaction fee.

**Payment**

Etsy may receive a payment processing fee.

**Advertising**

Etsy may receive Etsy Ads or Offsite Ads revenue.

**Shipping**

Etsy may earn revenue from shipping labels where available.

The same seller relationship therefore contains multiple possible monetization events.

The business architecture is better understood as:

**Marketplace Access + Transaction + Payments + Demand Access + Seller Services**

rather than simply:

**Marketplace Commission**

## 13. Network Effects

Etsy operates a two-sided marketplace network.

More sellers can increase:

- Product selection
- Product variety
- Unique inventory
- Customization
- Geographic availability
- Price points

Greater selection can attract more buyers.

More buyers create more selling opportunities.

Greater seller opportunity can attract additional sellers.

The basic loop is:

**More Sellers → More Unique Inventory → More Buyer Utility → More Purchases → More Seller Opportunity → More Sellers**

However, Etsy's marketplace has an unusual characteristic.

Its products are highly heterogeneous.

Two handmade products may not be direct substitutes.

This means marketplace value depends not only on the quantity of listings.

It depends on whether buyers can discover the relevant product within a very large inventory base.

---

## 14. Discovery as a Network Constraint

Etsy reported more than 100 million items available for sale at the end of 2025.

Large inventory creates selection.

It also creates a discovery problem.

If buyers cannot identify relevant products efficiently, additional supply may produce limited incremental value.

Search, recommendation, personalization, categorization, and ranking therefore become critical infrastructure.

The marketplace loop is not simply:

**More Supply → More Value**

It is:

**More Useful Supply + Better Discovery → More Value**

This makes search architecture central to Etsy's competitive position.

---

## 15. Search and Matching

Etsy identifies search and discovery as one of its core areas of strategic differentiation.

During 2026, the company continued investing in:

- Buyer profiles
- Search personalization
- Recommendation quality
- Feed freshness
- Inventory understanding
- Machine learning
- AI-enabled curation

By Q2 2026, Etsy reported buyer profiles covering more than 65 million buyers and containing substantially richer preference signals than earlier in the year.

The structural objective is clear.

The marketplace already contains enormous supply.

The commercial problem is increasingly:

**How can Etsy convert inventory breadth into relevant discovery?**

## 16. Seller Dependence

Etsy depends almost entirely on independent sellers for the merchandise that creates marketplace demand.

Sellers determine:

- What products are created
- Product quality
- Product design
- Product pricing
- Inventory
- Production capacity
- Shipping performance
- Packaging
- Customer service
- Product differentiation

Etsy controls the marketplace infrastructure.

It does not control the underlying creative production system.

This creates a fundamental dependency.

**Etsy cannot maintain differentiated supply without independent sellers continuing to create differentiated products.**

## 17. Seller Health

At the end of 2025, Etsy had approximately 5.6 million active sellers.

The number declined year over year but stabilized beginning in Q2 2025.

Etsy attributed some of the earlier decline to the seller set-up fee introduced in 2024, which was intended to encourage a higher level of seller commitment.

The retention rate of prior-year active sellers improved through 2025.

By Q2 2026, active sellers had increased to approximately 5.7 million and were growing year over year for the second consecutive quarter.

Seller quantity therefore matters.

But Etsy is also attempting to improve seller quality and retention rather than maximize seller count without regard to marketplace health.

## 18. Seller Multi-Homing

Seller multi-homing is fundamental to Etsy's business model.

A seller may simultaneously sell through:

- Etsy
- Other marketplaces
- Social networks
- Local markets
- Craft fairs
- Consignment stores
- Wholesale channels
- Direct websites
- Independent commerce software
- Agentic commerce channels

Etsy explicitly recognizes this competitive structure.

The company also competes with software and services that allow small businesses to build independent websites or sell through multiple channels.

Therefore:

**Etsy participation ≠ Etsy exclusivity**

A seller may remain active on Etsy while building commercial independence elsewhere.

## 19. Creator Independence

This makes Etsy particularly interesting for ORVESTRA.

The seller is not simply supply.

The seller can also become an independent business.

A successful Etsy seller may progressively develop:

- A brand
- A customer following
- Social media distribution
- Email marketing
- A standalone website
- Wholesale relationships
- Repeat customers
- Alternative marketplaces
- Independent payment relationships

The marketplace can therefore help create the conditions that eventually reduce seller dependence on the marketplace.

This is a structural tension inherent in creator platforms.

**Marketplace success can help produce stronger independent sellers.**

## 20. Disintermediation

Etsy faces a classic disintermediation risk.

A buyer may discover a seller through Etsy.

The seller and buyer may then develop a direct relationship.

Future transactions could theoretically occur through:

- A seller website
- Social media
- Email
- Physical retail
- Another marketplace
- Direct payment

If that occurs, Etsy loses the transaction and associated monetization layers.

The marketplace therefore creates valuable relationships that can become capable of existing outside the marketplace.

---

## 21. Off-Platform Transaction Policy

Etsy responds to this risk partly through marketplace policy.

Its Off-Platform Transactions policy prohibits activity intended to move Etsy-originated commerce away from the platform.

Examples include:

- Making offers to transact outside Etsy
- Encouraging buyers to purchase through another venue
- Using QR codes to move buyers elsewhere
- Moving payment outside Etsy

A transaction initiated through Etsy may not be completed off-platform.

The Seller Policy similarly prohibits using Etsy Messages to facilitate or direct off-platform transactions.

Policy therefore provides one defense against disintermediation.

But policy is not the only defense.

---

## 22. Trust as a Retention Mechanism

Etsy also provides transaction infrastructure that can make remaining on-platform useful.

This includes:

- Etsy Payments
- Purchase Protection
- Case systems
- Reviews
- Messaging
- Fraud controls
- Trust and safety
- Customer support
- Transaction history

Etsy notes that certain protections are not available for transactions completed outside Etsy.

A direct transaction may reduce platform-related fees.

But it may also remove standardized protections and marketplace trust infrastructure.

This creates a value exchange:

**Platform Fees**

in return for:

**Discovery + Payments + Trust + Transaction Infrastructure**

## 24. Seller Identity as Product Architecture

Etsy's seller identity is not merely background information.

It can become part of the product itself.

For some purchases, buyers may value:

- Who created the item
- How it was created
- Why it was created
- Craftsmanship
- Personalization
- Direct connection with the maker

This means marketplace value may exist partly outside conventional product attributes.

The commercial product becomes:

**Item + Story + Maker + Relationship**

For Etsy, preserving seller individuality can therefore increase marketplace differentiation.

## 25. The Seller Relationship Problem

There is also a structural tension.

Etsy wants buyers to feel a stronger relationship with individual sellers.

Stronger buyer-seller relationships can:

- Increase trust
- Increase repeat purchases
- Increase emotional attachment
- Strengthen differentiation

But stronger direct relationships can also increase the theoretical ability to transact outside Etsy.

Etsy must therefore solve a difficult marketplace problem:

**Strengthen the buyer-seller relationship without making the platform unnecessary to that relationship.**

This is one of the most interesting aspects of Etsy's business architecture.

## 26. Owned Distribution

Etsy does not own physical distribution infrastructure comparable with Amazon.

Its owned distribution is primarily digital.

Important channels include:

- Etsy.com
- Etsy mobile applications
- Email
- Push notifications
- Buyer accounts
- Saved preferences
- Direct brand traffic

These channels allow Etsy to reach existing buyers without purchasing every customer interaction from external platforms.

The mobile application is particularly important.

---

## 27. Mobile App as Owned Distribution

In Q2 2026:

- Mobile app GMS increased 12.5 percent year over year.
- The app generated approximately 47 percent of total GMS.
- App share of GMS increased approximately 210 basis points year over year.

Etsy describes the app as the environment where:

- Personalization
- Direct buyer relationships
- Owned marketing

can compound most effectively.

This matters structurally.

A buyer who begins directly in the Etsy app reduces Etsy's dependence on external acquisition channels.

The relationship becomes:

**Etsy Brand → Etsy App → Discovery → Seller → Transaction**

instead of:

**External Platform → Etsy → Seller → Transaction**

## 28. External Distribution Dependency

Despite strong direct channels, Etsy remains dependent on external distribution systems.

These include:

- Search engines
- Social platforms
- Mobile operating systems
- App stores
- Advertising networks
- External discovery platforms
- AI-enabled interfaces

Etsy identifies dependence on widely adopted third-party mobile, social, search, and advertising platforms as a business risk.

Changes to:

- Algorithms
- Platform policies
- Advertising costs
- Privacy rules
- Search behavior
- AI discovery

can affect Etsy's ability to acquire buyers.

Owned distribution is therefore important because it reduces, rather than eliminates, external dependency.

## 29. AI and Agentic Discovery

Etsy increasingly treats AI-driven discovery as both an opportunity and a distribution risk.

The company is working to make its long-tail inventory easier for external platforms and agentic experiences to understand and surface.

It is also testing conversational shopping within Etsy.

In 2026, Etsy introduced a beta Gifting Assistant designed to help buyers discover products through natural conversation.

This reflects an important structural shift.

The marketplace may no longer control every discovery interface.

The commercial objective becomes:

**Ensure Etsy inventory remains discoverable even when product search begins somewhere else.**

---

## 30. Partner Checkout

Etsy's Advertising and Marketing Policy contains an especially interesting architecture.

Certain third-party platforms participating in Offsite Ads may support:

**Partner Checkout**

Under Partner Checkout, eligible Etsy listings can be purchased through Etsy Payments while the buyer completes checkout on the participating third-party platform rather than being redirected to Etsy.com or the Etsy mobile application.

The seller remains responsible for the underlying listing and transaction obligations.

This creates a notable evolution in marketplace distribution.

Traditional structure:

**External Discovery → Etsy.com → Etsy Checkout → Seller**

Partner Checkout can create:

**External Discovery → External Interface → Etsy Payments → Etsy Seller**

Etsy can therefore participate in the transaction even when the consumer interface is partly owned by another platform.

---

## 31. Marketplace Ownership Versus Transaction Infrastructure

Partner Checkout introduces an important distinction.

Etsy may not own the entire user interface.

But Etsy can remain part of:

- Payment
- Seller relationship
- Marketplace governance
- Transaction processing
- Purchase architecture

This resembles a wider pattern already visible across Group 1.

Platforms increasingly attempt to remain economically relevant even when commerce moves beyond the original marketplace interface.

For Etsy, this may mean extending marketplace inventory and transaction infrastructure into external discovery environments.

---

## 32. Advertising as Value Capture

Advertising is increasingly important to Etsy's economics.

Etsy Ads allows sellers to purchase prominent placement inside Etsy.

The service operates primarily on a cost-per-click basis.

Seller demand for visibility creates another monetizable marketplace asset:

**Buyer Attention**

The seller already contributes inventory.

The seller may then pay Etsy again to increase the probability that the inventory is discovered.

This creates a structural relationship:

**More Sellers → More Listing Competition → Greater Value of Discovery → Greater Potential Advertising Demand**

Advertising therefore monetizes the scarcity of buyer attention within a marketplace containing enormous supply.

---

## 33. Offsite Advertising as Distribution Product

Offsite Ads operates differently.

Instead of selling visibility only inside Etsy, Etsy distributes seller inventory through external platforms.

Etsy pays acquisition costs upfront.

If the advertisement generates an attributed transaction, the seller may pay Etsy an additional fee.

The service therefore combines:

**External Distribution**

with:

**Performance-Based Monetization**

For a small seller without sophisticated marketing infrastructure, Etsy can effectively function as an outsourced acquisition system.

That increases platform utility.

It can also increase seller dependence on Etsy for demand generation.

## 37. Buyer Frequency

Etsy's marketplace challenge is not only customer acquisition.

It is also frequency.

In 2025, active buyers purchased on approximately three days per year on average.

Approximately half of active buyers purchased only once during the year.

Habitual buyers represented a small portion of active buyers but a disproportionately large share of GMS.

This means Etsy can potentially create significant economic value without acquiring a new buyer for every incremental transaction.

Increasing:

- Repeat purchases
- Shopping occasions
- Buyer retention
- Purchase frequency

can materially change marketplace economics.

## 43. Disintermediation Assessment

Etsy faces particularly clear disintermediation risk because its supply consists of independent businesses.

Several commercial states are possible.

### Full Etsy Relationship

**Buyer → Etsy → Seller → Etsy Checkout**

Etsy controls discovery and transaction infrastructure.

### Multi-Homed Seller

**Buyer → Alternative Marketplace → Same Seller**

The seller remains on Etsy but the transaction occurs elsewhere.

### Seller-Owned Distribution

**Buyer → Seller Website → Seller**

The seller controls discovery and transaction.

### Social Distribution

**Buyer → Social Platform → Seller Channel**

A social network becomes the discovery layer.

### Agentic Discovery

**Buyer → AI or Agentic Interface → Commerce Channel**

The buyer may begin product search outside any conventional marketplace.

Etsy therefore cannot assume that controlling its own marketplace interface guarantees control of future product discovery.

## 44. ORVESTRA Structural Observation

Etsy illustrates one of the clearest tensions in marketplace architecture.

The platform benefits when sellers become successful.

Successful sellers create:

- Better inventory
- Greater trust
- Repeat demand
- Marketplace reputation

But successful sellers may also become more capable of operating independently.

They can develop:

- Their own brands
- Their own audiences
- Their own websites
- Their own distribution
- Their own repeat customer relationships

Etsy therefore needs seller success without making Etsy irrelevant to successful sellers.

Its defense is not ownership of the seller.

It is continued usefulness.

That usefulness comes from:

**Demand + Discovery + Trust + Payments + Advertising + Brand + Marketplace Infrastructure**

## 47. Owned Distribution Versus Seller-Owned Distribution

Etsy and its sellers are simultaneously building distribution.

Etsy owns:

- Marketplace traffic
- Application relationships
- Email
- Push notifications
- Brand recognition
- Search and recommendation surfaces

Sellers may own:

- Social audiences
- Email lists
- Direct websites
- Wholesale relationships
- Repeat customer relationships
- Physical distribution

The long-term commercial relationship therefore contains negotiation through architecture rather than formal negotiation.

The question is:

**Which party owns enough demand to remain indispensable to the other?**

---

## 48. Marketplace Power Without Logistics

Etsy differs significantly from Amazon.

Amazon can reinforce marketplace power through physical fulfillment and delivery infrastructure.

Etsy does not operate a comparable broad owned logistics network.

Its stronger control points are digital:

- Discovery
- Trust
- Payments
- Reputation
- Brand
- Buyer relationships
- Advertising

This means Etsy provides a useful test of how strong marketplace power can become when the platform does not own the physical operating layer surrounding the merchandise.

---

## 49. Group 1 Comparative Context

The five Platform & Marketplace analyses now show different architectures of platform power.

### Amazon

**Marketplace + Logistics + Advertising + Infrastructure**

Amazon can remain economically relevant through physical infrastructure even when commerce occurs elsewhere.

### Uber

**Demand + Local Liquidity + Coordination**

Uber organizes distributed transportation capacity without owning most vehicles.

### Airbnb

**Demand + Brand + Trust + Booking Infrastructure**

Airbnb organizes accommodation supply without owning most properties.

### DoorDash

**Marketplace Demand + Local Logistics + Merchant Infrastructure**

DoorDash can serve merchant-owned demand through Commerce Platform.

### Etsy

**Discovery + Human Differentiation + Trust + Payments + Seller Services**

Etsy coordinates independent creator businesses whose underlying commercial assets remain comparatively portable.

The shared question is therefore not simply:

**Which marketplace is largest?**

It is:

**Which parts of the commercial relationship does each platform actually control?**

## 50. Analytical Limits

Etsy operates across:

- Multiple countries
- Multiple seller categories
- Different payment fee schedules
- Different currencies
- Different regulatory systems
- Different advertising participation structures

A single seller fee does not represent the total cost of using Etsy.

The 6.5 percent transaction fee should not be interpreted as Etsy's total economic take.

Similarly, Etsy's reported revenue take rate should not be interpreted as the fee paid by every seller on every transaction.

Historical consolidated results also require care because:

- Reverb was sold in June 2025.
- Depop was sold in July 2026.

Q2 2026 current operations therefore provide a cleaner representation of the Etsy marketplace than prior consolidated results.

Seller economics vary substantially according to:

- Product cost
- Labor
- Materials
- Pricing
- Advertising usage
- Shipping
- Payment fees
- Returns
- Taxes
- Product category
- Direct sales
- Marketplace mix

This analysis does not estimate:

- Seller profitability
- Universal seller acquisition cost
- Universal Etsy take rate
- Future advertising economics
- Future AI distribution outcomes
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

## 51. Conclusion

Etsy's marketplace power does not come from ownership of products, factories, warehouses, or seller businesses.

It comes from organizing fragmented creative supply around buyer demand.

The seller owns the product.

The seller may own the brand.

The seller may eventually own significant independent distribution.

Etsy attempts to remain valuable by owning or controlling important parts of:

**Discovery**

**Search**

**Trust**

**Payments**

**Advertising**

**Marketplace Reputation**

**Buyer Relationships**

**Transaction Infrastructure**

The structural challenge is unusually clear.

Etsy wants its sellers to become successful businesses.

But increasingly successful sellers can become increasingly capable of operating outside Etsy.

The marketplace therefore cannot rely entirely on seller dependence.

It must repeatedly earn its place in the seller's commercial architecture.

At the same time, Etsy is strengthening its own direct distribution through the mobile app, personalized buyer relationships, owned marketing, and external inventory distribution.

Partner Checkout adds another interesting possibility.

The buyer may not need to complete the transaction on Etsy.com for Etsy infrastructure to remain part of the transaction.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**Etsy does not need to own the creator's business if it can remain the most useful place where independent supply becomes discoverable, trusted, and commercially actionable.**
