## 1. Analytical Question

Where does DoorDash's platform power come from when the merchants, products, and most delivery capacity operating through its system remain outside DoorDash's direct ownership?

A second question follows:

Can DoorDash continue capturing value when merchants increasingly want to own their customer relationships, ordering channels, and brands rather than depend entirely on a third-party marketplace?

---

## 2. Structural Summary

DoorDash has developed beyond a restaurant delivery marketplace.

Its current business architecture includes:

- DoorDash Marketplace
- Wolt Marketplace
- Deliveroo Marketplace
- Consumer membership programs
- Advertising
- Grocery and retail commerce
- White-label delivery
- Online ordering
- Branded merchant applications
- Reservation and in-store dining tools
- Customer relationship tools
- Tableside ordering and payment
- Merchant support infrastructure

DoorDash describes its primary offerings as its Marketplaces and Commerce Platform.

Its Marketplaces operate in more than 40 countries.

The Commerce Platform allows merchants to use DoorDash capabilities while operating through their own channels.

This creates two distinct commercial relationships.

**Marketplace Model**

DoorDash helps generate demand and coordinate the transaction.

**Commerce Platform Model**

The merchant may generate the demand while DoorDash provides some of the infrastructure required to execute or manage the transaction.

The underlying business is therefore increasingly broader than delivery.

It is becoming an infrastructure layer for local commerce.

## 5. Deliveroo and Global Marketplace Expansion

DoorDash completed its acquisition of Deliveroo on 02 October 2025.

The acquisition added Deliveroo to a marketplace portfolio that already included DoorDash and Wolt.

This gives DoorDash three major marketplace brands operating across different geographic environments.

The company is also developing a shared global technology platform across its marketplace brands.

During 2026, DoorDash reported progress integrating common infrastructure across areas including:

- Payments
- Fraud
- Support
- Subscriptions
- Merchant tooling
- Logistics

This creates an important structural distinction.

The consumer-facing marketplace brands can remain locally recognizable while more of the underlying infrastructure becomes shared.

The potential operating leverage therefore does not require every market to use the same consumer brand.

## 7. Marketplace Revenue Architecture

DoorDash states that Marketplace revenue reflects commissions charged to partner merchants and fees charged to consumers, less:

- Dasher payout
- Refunds
- Credits
- Promotions

DoorDash also generates Marketplace revenue from:

- DashPass
- Wolt+
- Deliveroo Plus
- Advertising

Commerce Platform revenue is generated separately.

Drive produces the majority of Commerce Platform revenue and charges merchants per-order fees to arrange delivery for demand originating through merchant-owned channels.

This creates multiple revenue relationships around local commerce.

---

## 8. Merchant Commission Architecture

DoorDash's standard U.S. restaurant Marketplace plans currently illustrate how merchant pricing can vary according to the level of marketplace distribution provided.

For eligible restaurants, the standard plans include:

| Plan | Delivery Commission |
|---|---:|
| Basic | 15% |
| Plus | 25% |
| Premier | 30% |

Pickup commission is generally listed at 6 percent across these plans.

Higher commission tiers provide additional marketplace distribution benefits, including broader customer reach and other features.

These standard plans apply to specified restaurant categories and should not be treated as a universal global commission schedule.

Large merchants, international merchants, non-restaurant categories, and individually negotiated commercial relationships may operate under different terms.

## 9. Take Rate and Net Revenue Margin

DoorDash reports a metric called **Net Revenue Margin**.

It defines Net Revenue Margin as:

**Revenue divided by Marketplace GOV**

For 2025, Net Revenue Margin was 13.4 percent.

This should not be interpreted as a universal merchant commission or a pure marketplace take rate.

There are several reasons.

DoorDash revenue includes:

- Merchant commissions
- Consumer fees
- Membership revenue
- Advertising
- Commerce Platform revenue

Marketplace GOV, however, excludes the value of orders and merchant fees generated through the Commerce Platform.

Therefore:

**Net Revenue Margin ≠ Merchant Commission**

and:

**Net Revenue Margin ≠ Universal Transaction Take Rate**

It is a broader financial relationship between reported revenue and Marketplace GOV.

---

## 10. A Measurement Boundary

DoorDash's reporting structure reveals something important about the evolution of the business.

**Total Orders** includes orders completed through both:

- Marketplaces
- Commerce Platform

But:

**Marketplace GOV** excludes Commerce Platform order value.

This means DoorDash can increase the number of transactions its infrastructure supports without all of that commerce appearing inside Marketplace GOV.

For ORVESTRA, this is an important analytical boundary.

A growing share of DoorDash's relevance could theoretically occur outside its marketplace discovery layer.

Marketplace GOV alone would not capture the entire operating footprint of that infrastructure.

## 11. Network Effects

DoorDash has a multi-sided local network.

The primary participants are:

**Consumers**

**Merchants**

**Dashers**

More merchants can increase product selection.

Greater selection can make the marketplace more useful to consumers.

More consumer demand can increase sales opportunities for merchants.

More orders can create earning opportunities for Dashers.

Greater Dasher availability can improve delivery capacity.

Better fulfillment can increase consumer utility.

The basic loop is:

**More Merchants → More Selection → More Consumers → More Orders → More Dasher Opportunity → Better Fulfillment → More Consumer Utility**

DoorDash itself states that an increase in merchants attracts consumers and an increase in consumers attracts merchants.

The network therefore becomes stronger when participation improves the value available to other participant groups.

---

## 12. Local Density

DoorDash's network effects have a physical constraint.

Food, grocery, convenience, and retail delivery occur locally.

A consumer cannot benefit from a restaurant hundreds of kilometres away merely because it exists somewhere on the global platform.

The relevant network is therefore partly:

**Local**

and:

**Time Sensitive**

DoorDash must coordinate:

- Local merchant selection
- Consumer demand
- Dasher availability
- Merchant preparation time
- Route distance
- Delivery sequencing

DoorDash invests in demand prediction, preparation-time forecasting, routing, and order batching.

This means scale becomes economically useful only when it can be converted into local operating density.

---

## 13. Logistics Efficiency

The marketplace does not generate value from matching alone.

Delivery economics also depend on how efficiently physical orders move through the network.

DoorDash identifies several areas affecting platform cost effectiveness:

- Dasher efficiency
- Dasher pay
- Demand prediction
- Merchant preparation timing
- Routing
- Batching
- Defective orders
- Refunds
- Credits

In 2025, DoorDash stated that revenue grew slightly faster than Marketplace GOV partly because of:

- Improved logistics efficiency
- Increasing advertising contribution
- Lower credits and refunds as a percentage of Marketplace GOV

This illustrates how platform economics can improve through operating efficiency without requiring a higher merchant commission on every transaction.

---

## 14. Dasher Dependence

DoorDash does not own most of the delivery capacity operating through its marketplaces.

Dashers provide that capacity.

More than 9 million people completed deliveries through DoorDash during 2025.

This large external supply base creates flexibility for DoorDash.

It also creates dependency.

The platform must offer enough earning opportunity to attract Dashers at the locations and times where consumer demand exists.

DoorDash identifies Dasher pay as a major component of its cost structure.

Therefore:

**Low ownership of delivery assets does not mean low dependence on delivery supply.**

---

## 15. Dasher Multi-Homing

DoorDash explicitly recognizes that delivery workers can use multiple platforms concurrently.

A Dasher may compare:

- Earnings
- Available orders
- Distance
- Waiting time
- Incentives
- Flexibility
- Alternative work
- Competing delivery applications

DoorDash therefore does not permanently own Dasher capacity.

It competes for a flexible participant's time.

This creates an unusual supply relationship.

The Dasher can be part of the DoorDash network without being exclusively committed to DoorDash.

---

## 16. Merchant Multi-Homing

The same structural condition exists on the merchant side.

DoorDash states that merchants may adopt more than one local commerce platform in order to maximize order volume.

A restaurant can potentially participate through:

- DoorDash
- Uber Eats
- Other delivery marketplaces
- Its own application
- Its own website
- Telephone ordering
- In-store ordering
- Its own delivery fleet

DoorDash itself tells merchants that they can use DoorDash alongside other delivery platforms.

Therefore:

**Merchant participation ≠ Merchant exclusivity**

The platform must continuously justify its place in the merchant's commercial system.

---

## 17. Consumer Multi-Homing

Consumers can also maintain multiple ordering channels.

They may compare platforms based on:

- Merchant selection
- Delivery fee
- Service fee
- Promotions
- Delivery time
- Membership
- Product price
- Convenience
- Reliability

DoorDash states that switching between offerings in the industry is relatively easy.

This makes permanent demand lock-in difficult.

The platform therefore needs to create repeated reasons for consumers to return.

Membership is one of its primary tools for doing that.

---

## 18. DashPass as Demand Retention

DashPass reduces delivery and service fees for eligible transactions and provides additional benefits.

DoorDash states that membership is designed to reduce transactional friction.

The company also reports that DashPass members generally order more frequently and have higher retention than comparable non-members.

In the 12 months through Q2 2026, DoorDash added more U.S. paid DashPass members than it had added during the previous 24 months combined.

DoorDash also reported that DashPass members generated approximately 75 percent of Total Orders in its U.S. grocery and retail categories during Q2 2026.

This is structurally important.

DashPass is not simply subscription revenue.

It is also a mechanism for increasing:

- Frequency
- Retention
- Cross-category adoption
- Customer lifetime activity

---

## 19. Membership Economics

Membership introduces an economic trade-off.

DoorDash states that DashPass reduces consumer fees and therefore typically produces a lower gross margin percentage than non-DashPass orders.

However, membership can also increase:

- Order frequency
- Retention
- Customer duration
- Merchant sales
- Lifetime value

The relevant economic question is therefore not:

**Does DoorDash make the maximum margin on every DashPass order?**

It is:

**Does lower transactional friction increase the total economic value of the customer relationship over time?**

This is a different form of value capture from maximizing the fee on an individual order.

---

## 20. Advertising

Advertising adds another layer to DoorDash's commercial architecture.

DoorDash offers advertising through its Marketplaces to:

- Merchants
- Consumer packaged goods companies

The purpose is to increase consumer engagement and merchant visibility.

Advertising converts marketplace demand into another monetizable asset.

A merchant may therefore participate in multiple commercial relationships with DoorDash:

**Marketplace Distribution**

plus potentially:

**Advertising**

plus potentially:

**Commerce Platform Services**

This makes DoorDash's monetization architecture broader than commissions alone.

## 21. Disintermediation

DoorDash faces a fundamental marketplace risk.

A merchant that develops a strong customer relationship may prefer future orders to originate through its own website or application.

This can reduce marketplace commission costs and increase control over:

- Customer data
- Branding
- Loyalty
- Marketing
- Ordering experience

DoorDash itself acknowledges that merchants may develop their own pickup and delivery platforms rather than use a third-party marketplace.

This is classic marketplace disintermediation.

But DoorDash has developed a response that is more structurally interesting than simply trying to prevent merchants from leaving.

---

## 22. Drive On-Demand

Drive On-Demand allows merchants to generate orders through their own:

- Website
- Application
- Online ordering platform
- Other owned channel

and use DoorDash for fulfillment.

The merchant can own customer acquisition.

DoorDash can still provide the delivery infrastructure.

The architecture changes from:

**Consumer → DoorDash Marketplace → Merchant → Dasher**

to:

**Consumer → Merchant-Owned Channel → DoorDash Delivery Infrastructure**

DoorDash currently lists standard U.S. Drive On-Demand pricing as a flat fee per delivery rather than a marketplace commission.

The listed standard range is approximately $6.99 to $10.99 per delivery order, although actual pricing and commercial terms may vary.

This is structurally different from Marketplace monetization.

---

## 23. Online Ordering

DoorDash also offers Online Ordering for merchant-owned websites.

DoorDash describes these orders as commission-free.

The merchant can use its own website as the demand channel while DoorDash provides ordering infrastructure.

This changes the commercial relationship again.

The merchant does not need to choose between:

**Own Customer Relationship**

or:

**DoorDash Infrastructure**

DoorDash increasingly attempts to provide infrastructure underneath merchant-owned distribution.

---

## 24. Commerce Platform

Drive and Online Ordering are part of a broader Commerce Platform.

DoorDash states that the Commerce Platform includes capabilities that can help merchants:

- Establish online ordering
- Build branded mobile applications
- Manage reservations
- Manage in-store dining
- Manage consumer relationships
- Enable tableside ordering
- Enable tableside payment
- Improve customer support
- Fulfill delivery through Drive

This is a significant business architecture expansion.

DoorDash is no longer monetizing only the moment when a consumer discovers a merchant inside the DoorDash Marketplace.

It can increasingly monetize or support merchant operations outside the marketplace.

## 29. Global Infrastructure

DoorDash's ownership of Wolt and Deliveroo creates another form of scale.

The company can operate several marketplace brands while attempting to standardize underlying technology.

This is important because consumer preferences and local brands can differ by geography.

A single global consumer brand is not required for shared infrastructure.

DoorDash can potentially centralize more capabilities while keeping local marketplace identities.

The observed 2026 technology work across payments, fraud, support, subscriptions, merchant tooling, and logistics demonstrates this direction.

The structural pattern is:

**Multiple Marketplaces → Shared Infrastructure**

## 39. Disintermediation Assessment

DoorDash faces substantial disintermediation risk because merchants can increasingly build direct digital relationships with customers.

Several commercial states are possible.

### Full Marketplace Relationship

**Consumer → DoorDash → Merchant → Dasher**

DoorDash controls demand discovery and fulfillment coordination.

### Marketplace Demand + Merchant Fulfillment

**Consumer → DoorDash → Merchant-Owned Delivery**

DoorDash generates demand while the merchant controls fulfillment.

### Merchant-Owned Demand + DoorDash Fulfillment

**Consumer → Merchant Channel → DoorDash Drive**

The merchant controls demand while DoorDash coordinates delivery.

### Merchant-Owned Demand + Merchant-Owned Fulfillment

**Consumer → Merchant → Merchant Delivery**

DoorDash is removed from the transaction.

DoorDash's strategic architecture increasingly attempts to remain commercially useful across more than one of these states.

## 40. ORVESTRA Structural Observation

DoorDash illustrates an important evolution in marketplace architecture.

A conventional marketplace attempts to own the transaction.

DoorDash increasingly attempts to own useful pieces of the commercial infrastructure even when it does not own the transaction origin.

This distinction matters.

If a restaurant builds its own website, that does not necessarily mean DoorDash loses the entire commercial relationship.

DoorDash can provide:

- Ordering technology
- Branded applications
- Delivery
- Reservations
- Customer tools
- Merchant services

The marketplace can therefore evolve into infrastructure.

For ORVESTRA, the deeper architecture is:

**Demand Aggregation + Local Logistics + Membership + Advertising + Merchant Infrastructure**

## 41. What Makes This Business Interesting

DoorDash is particularly useful for studying what happens when the largest structural threat to a marketplace becomes another product opportunity.

Merchant-owned distribution can disintermediate DoorDash Marketplace.

DoorDash Commerce Platform turns merchant-owned distribution into a market for DoorDash infrastructure.

That does not eliminate disintermediation risk.

It changes its economics.

The merchant may control the customer.

DoorDash may still control or supply part of the operating stack.

This creates a structural pattern:

**Build Marketplace**

then

**Build Local Logistics Density**

then

**Build Merchant Tools**

then

**Allow Merchants to Own Demand**

then

**Monetize Infrastructure Underneath That Demand**

DoorDash therefore does not need every local commerce transaction to begin inside the DoorDash application in order to remain commercially relevant.

## 44. Conclusion

DoorDash began with a marketplace problem:

**How can local demand be connected with local merchants and delivered efficiently?**

The business now addresses a broader problem:

**How much of local commerce infrastructure can DoorDash provide?**

The original marketplace still matters.

It aggregates demand.

DashPass increases retention.

Dashers provide distributed fulfillment capacity.

Advertising monetizes marketplace attention.

Grocery and retail increase purchase frequency.

Wolt and Deliveroo expand geographic reach.

Commerce Platform extends DoorDash into merchant-owned channels.

This creates an increasingly layered system.

A merchant can depend on DoorDash for demand.

Or it can own the demand and depend on DoorDash for fulfillment.

Or it can use DoorDash software while maintaining its own brand and customer relationship.

The structural importance of DoorDash therefore extends beyond delivery commissions.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**DoorDash does not need to own every customer relationship if it can become part of the infrastructure through which local commerce operates.**
