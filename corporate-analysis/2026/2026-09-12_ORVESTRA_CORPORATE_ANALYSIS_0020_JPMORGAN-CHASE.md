## 1. Analytical Question

Who captures value when the same institution can hold the customer's money, issue the payment credential, finance the purchase, acquire the merchant, manage corporate liquidity, and connect transactions to multiple payment rails?

A second question follows:

What changes when a payments company also owns a regulated bank balance sheet?

JPMorgan Chase occupies a fundamentally different position from the first four Group 4 companies.

Visa primarily operates:

**Global Payment Network Infrastructure**

Mastercard combines:

**Global Multi-Rail Network + Services**

PayPal combines:

**Wallet + Checkout + Merchant Processing + Orchestration**

Block combines:

**Consumer Financial Network + Merchant Operating System + Selected Banking Infrastructure**

JPMorgan Chase combines:

**Deposits**

**Credit**

**Card Issuing**

**Merchant Acquiring**

**Cash Management**

**Liquidity**

**Clearing**

**Settlement**

**Trade**

**Working Capital**

**Real-Time Payments**

**Blockchain Deposit Infrastructure**

The architecture can therefore be simplified as:

**Account**

→ **Funding**

→ **Payment Instruction**

→ **Rail**

→ **Merchant / Recipient**

→ **Settlement**

while JPMorgan Chase can participate in several layers of the same transaction.

## 31. Card Network Layer Remains External

A Chase card payment can still depend on external card-network infrastructure.

JPMorgan Chase does not own Visa or Mastercard's global card networks.

**Issuer Ownership ≠ Network Ownership.**

## 32. Issuer Economics

As a card issuer, JPMorgan Chase can earn economics associated with:

- Interest on revolving credit
- Interchange
- Annual fees
- Other account economics

It also incurs:

- Rewards
- Partner payments
- Credit losses
- Funding costs
- Fraud costs
- Operating expense

This differs fundamentally from Visa and Mastercard.

---

## 33. Net Interchange Is Not Pure Revenue

JPMorgan Chase's card economics reflect:

**Interchange**

minus:

**Rewards Costs**

and:

**Partner Payments**

among other items.

In 2025, the firm reported that net interchange decreased despite higher card sales volume because increased volume was more than offset by higher rewards costs and partner payments.

[S1]

Therefore:

**Higher Card Spend ≠ Higher Net Interchange One-for-One.**

---

## 34. Rewards Are Distribution Economics

- Top-of-wallet status

Rewards reduce current card economics in exchange for potentially deeper customer engagement.

## 161. Analytical Limits

JPMorgan Chase is a diversified financial institution and payment-related activity is distributed across several reporting businesses.

The $19.3 billion fiscal 2025 Payments revenue figure refers to the Payments business within the Commercial & Investment Bank.

It should not be interpreted as total firmwide revenue generated by all payment-related activity.

Consumer credit card revenue, debit economics, consumer deposits, and other payment-related activities can be reported elsewhere.

Payments revenue includes deposit-related economics and should not be treated as pure transaction-fee revenue.

Total payments transaction volume reported within Consumer & Community Banking is a specific business metric including debit and credit card sales volume and gross outflows of ACH, ATM, teller, wires, BillPay, PayChase, Zelle, person-to-person payments, and checks.

It should not be interpreted as J.P. Morgan Payments institutional transaction volume.

Debit and credit card sales volume overlaps with the total-payments metric and should not be added to it.

Card Services sales volume excludes commercial cards and is different from combined debit and credit card sales volume.

Cards in force should not be interpreted as unique customers.

Active digital and mobile customers should not be interpreted as payment users.

Merchant processing volume should not be interpreted as revenue.

The $2.58 trillion merchant-processing figure cited in this analysis refers to 2024 and should not be represented as 2025 or 2026 volume.

Merchant-acquiring rankings depend on the cited external source, period, market, and methodology.

Total firm deposits should not be confused with CIB average client deposits.

Deposits should not be interpreted as revenue.

Credit card loans create direct balance-sheet exposure and should not be compared mechanically with payment volume.

Net interchange includes the impact of rewards costs and partner payments and should not be treated as gross interchange earned.

JPMorgan Chase does not own Visa, Mastercard, FedNow, The Clearing House RTP, SWIFT, or public blockchain infrastructure.

Providing client access to a rail should not be interpreted as ownership of that rail.

Blockchain Deposit Accounts remain commercial-bank deposit products inside J.P. Morgan's banking framework.

JPM Coin is a bank-issued deposit token and should not be described as a stablecoin or cryptocurrency.

Kinexys cumulative and daily transaction-volume figures should not be interpreted as Kinexys revenue or assets.

The reported Q2 2026 net income included significant items and should not be treated as normalized earnings.

This analysis does not estimate:

## 162. Conclusion

JPMorgan Chase closes Group 4 by demonstrating that the payment system cannot be understood through transaction processing alone.

Visa and Mastercard show the value of:

**Network Interoperability.**

PayPal shows the value of:

**Checkout and Orchestration.**

Block shows the value of:

**Owning Consumer and Merchant Ecosystems.**

JPMorgan Chase adds another layer:

**The Balance Sheet.**

At 30 June 2026, the firm held approximately:

**$2.714 trillion of deposits**

and:

**$5.015 trillion of assets.**

Its payment system therefore begins before the customer presses:

**Pay.**

A consumer can already hold money at Chase.

A corporation can already hold operating liquidity at J.P. Morgan.

A borrower can already have credit from the bank.

The bank can then move the money.

It can issue the card.

It can acquire the merchant.

It can route account payments through ACH.

It can use FedNow or RTP for instant payments.

It can send global institutional payments.

It can manage FX.

It can finance working capital.

It can move commercial-bank deposits through Kinexys.

It can tokenize a bank deposit through JPM Coin.

This creates a payment architecture with unusually deep vertical coverage.

But JPMorgan Chase still does not own every rail.

Visa and Mastercard remain important.

Federal Reserve infrastructure remains important.

The Clearing House remains important.

SWIFT remains important.

Public blockchain infrastructure remains external.

The bank's advantage is therefore not complete ownership of the payment system.

It is:

**The ability to sit at the center of many financial relationships and connect those relationships to whichever payment infrastructure is required.**

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**JPMorgan Chase captures value not only because money moves through its systems, but because it can own the deposit before the payment, the credit behind the payment, the merchant or treasury relationship around the payment, and the liquidity and settlement relationship after the payment.**

That makes the balance sheet itself part of the payment architecture.
