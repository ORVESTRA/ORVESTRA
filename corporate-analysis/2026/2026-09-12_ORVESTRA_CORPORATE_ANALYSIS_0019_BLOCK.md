## 1. Analytical Question

Who captures value when Block connects a consumer financial network with a merchant operating system?

A second question follows:

Can Block create stronger payment economics by owning more of the relationship around the transaction even when it does not own the global card rails underneath it?

Block occupies a different position from Visa, Mastercard, and PayPal.

Visa and Mastercard primarily operate global payment networks.

PayPal primarily combines wallet, checkout, merchant processing, and orchestration.

Block combines:

**Consumer Financial Network**

**Merchant Operating System**

**Payment Processing**

**Lending**

**Banking Infrastructure**

**Commerce Software**

The central architecture can be simplified as:

**Cash App Consumer**

→ **Payment / Financial Relationship**

→ **Commerce**

→ **Square Seller**

with external and internal infrastructure underneath.

Block's strategic opportunity is therefore not simply:

**Process More Payments**

It is:

**Connect More of the Consumer and Merchant Economic Relationship Inside One System.**

The company therefore sits:

**On Top of Payment Rails**

and increasingly:

**Inside More of the Infrastructure Connecting Those Rails to Consumers and Merchants.**

## 18. Merchant of Record Boundary

Square's merchant-of-record role does not mean Square owns the seller's merchandise or underlying customer relationship.

It means Square assumes specific contractual and operational roles in processing the payment.

These can include:

- Transaction processing
- Settlement
- Risk
- Chargebacks
- Network compliance

This is materially different from Visa's role as a payment network.

---

## 19. Square Transaction Economics

Square earns payment-processing fees when sellers complete transactions.

Block then incurs costs including:

- Interchange
- Card-network assessments
- Processing fees
- Bank settlement fees

[S1]

The simplified model is:

**Merchant Fee**

minus:

**Payment Infrastructure Cost**

equals part of:

**Square Payment Gross Profit**

before broader company operating expenses.

---

## 20. Block Pays Interchange on Merchant Processing

This creates an important Group 4 distinction.

For Square merchant transactions, Block can be a payer of:

- Interchange
- Network assessments
- Processing costs

These costs flow to external financial infrastructure.

[S1]

Block therefore sits above Visa and Mastercard on the merchant side.

---

## 21. Cash App Card Reverses the Economics

Cash App Card creates the opposite economic position.

Cash App Card is issued by a bank partner and is linked to the consumer's Cash App balance.

It operates on:

**Visa acceptance infrastructure.**

Block earns interchange revenue when eligible Cash App Card purchases occur.

[S1]

Therefore Block can occupy different sides of card economics depending on the product.

---

## 22. Two Sides of Interchange

### Square

Block pays or absorbs payment-network and interchange-related costs as part of merchant processing.

### Cash App Card

Block earns interchange revenue when customers use the card.

This is one of Block's most interesting transaction-economics characteristics.

The same company participates on:

**Merchant Acceptance Economics**

and:

**Consumer Issuing Economics**

---

## 23. Block Does Not Own the Visa Rail

Cash App Card can be valuable to Block while still relying on:

**Visa Network Acceptance.**

Therefore:

**Cash App Card Economics**

should not be interpreted as evidence that Block owns the payment rail.

Visa provides global acceptance infrastructure.

Block owns more of the consumer relationship.

## 24. Interface Versus Rail

Cash App demonstrates the same distinction seen in PayPal.

**Cash App**

while the payment travels through:

**Visa.**

The user interface and financial relationship can therefore belong to Block while network connectivity belongs to another company.

## 150. Analytical Limits

Block's financial and operating metrics require strict separation.

Total net revenue should not be interpreted as equivalent to gross economic contribution because bitcoin ecosystem revenue carries a high associated cost of revenue.

Gross profit should not be interpreted as operating income or net income.

Cash App and Square are reportable segments, while Commerce Enablement, Financial Solutions, and Bitcoin Ecosystem are revenue categories that cut across segments.

Cash App monthly transacting actives should not be interpreted as unique individuals.

One individual can potentially be associated with more than one Cash App account.

Primary Banking Active is a Block-defined engagement metric and does not establish that Cash App is legally the user's bank or only financial relationship.

Cash App inflows should not be interpreted as revenue, spending volume, or assets owned by Block.

Cash App Commerce Enablement volume should not be treated as equivalent to Cash App inflows or consolidated GPV.

GPV excludes BNPL activity under Block's definition.

Square GPV and consolidated GPV are different measures.

Square GPV should not be interpreted as Block revenue.

The $32.8 billion cumulative Square Loans principal figure is a cumulative historical origination metric and not current loans outstanding.

Consumer Lending origination volume is not revenue or outstanding loan balance.

Transaction, loan, and consumer receivable losses combine multiple types of loss exposure.

Cash App Card operates through external bank and Visa infrastructure.

Cash App interchange revenue should not be interpreted as ownership of the Visa payment rail.

Square merchant processing remains materially dependent on external card networks and acquiring processors.

Square Financial Services is a regulated industrial loan company but should not be treated as equivalent in scale or architecture to a diversified commercial bank.

Square Savings deposits should not be assumed to fund a material share of Block lending without further evidence.

The June 2026 Square Financial Services acquiring transaction represents demonstrated execution of one acquiring transaction.

Block's stated expectation to shift more Square and Cash App acquiring volume to Square Financial Services over future years is forward-looking and should not be treated as completed migration.

Neighborhoods annualized GPV should not be interpreted as Neighborhoods revenue or incremental seller sales.

Neighborhoods follower-spend observations should not be interpreted as proof of causation.

USDC functionality in Cash App should not be interpreted as a Block-issued stablecoin.

Bitcoin ecosystem revenue should not be interpreted as bitcoin gross profit.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Universal Block payment take rate
- Square standalone payment margin
- Cash App standalone operating profit
- Neighborhoods revenue
- Cross-ecosystem incremental GPV
- Square Financial Services acquiring economics
- Consumer lifetime value
- Seller lifetime value
- Ecosystem switching cost
- Future credit losses
- Future market share
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

That creates a fundamentally different architecture from the global card networks.

Visa and Mastercard do not need to own the merchant operating system.

Block does.

PayPal does not need to own a physical point-of-sale environment.

Square does.

JPMorgan Chase will own a much deeper banking balance sheet.

Block instead owns a smaller regulated banking layer embedded inside a technology ecosystem.

Block remains dependent on the existing payment system.

Square pays costs to:

**Card Networks**

**Processors**

**Banks**

Cash App Card itself uses:

**Visa Acceptance Infrastructure.**

Yet the economics differ depending on which side Block occupies.

When Square accepts a card, Block bears payment-network and processing costs.

When a Cash App Card user spends, Block can earn issuer-side interchange revenue.

This gives Block exposure to both sides of card economics.

The more important strategic shift is occurring underneath.

Square Financial Services already originates seller loans and parts of Block's consumer lending.

It now accepts eligible seller deposits.

In June 2026 it processed its first Square acquiring transaction.

That does not mean Block has internalized acquiring at scale.

It does demonstrate the direction:

**Own More of the Infrastructure Where Ownership Improves the Ecosystem.**

Above that infrastructure, Block is connecting its networks.

Neighborhoods links consumer discovery with Square sellers.

Cash App Pay expands merchant commerce.

Direct deposit and Primary Banking Actives increase Cash App financial depth.

Square software makes merchant payment relationships more operationally persistent.

Payments create data.

Data supports risk.

Risk supports lending.

Lending can deepen the relationship.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**Block's payment strategy is not primarily to build another universal rail. It is to own enough of the consumer relationship, merchant relationship, software, data, risk, and financial infrastructure that more economic activity can remain inside the Block ecosystem even when the underlying money still travels across rails owned by someone else.**
