## 1. Analytical Question

Where does Alphabet capture value as artificial intelligence changes the way users search, consume information, interact with software, develop applications, and purchase cloud infrastructure?

A second question follows:

If AI changes the traditional search interface and weakens some historical advertising patterns, can Alphabet capture the value that migrates toward models, APIs, cloud infrastructure, subscriptions, agents, enterprise software, and new AI-mediated interfaces?

---

- Search
- Advertising

- Gemini

- Google Workspace

- Google Services

This structure allows Alphabet to participate economically across several layers of the AI transition rather than depend entirely on one product interface.

Google Search creates value by organizing information.

Alphabet captures part of that value through advertising.

The system historically connects:

**User Query**

→ **Search Results**

→ **Commercially Relevant Ads**

→ **Advertiser Payment**

Search therefore combines:

- Distribution

AI changes the information retrieval layer.

That makes Search one of the most important businesses in the world for studying AI-driven value migration.

## 9. AI Disruption to Search

Generative AI introduces a structural challenge to traditional search.

The historical search interface often requires the user to:

1. Enter a query
2. Review links
3. Visit websites
4. Gather information
5. Form a conclusion

An AI interface can compress these steps.

The structure can become:

**Question → Synthesized Answer**

This creates several possible changes.

Users may:

- Ask longer questions
- Conduct fewer separate searches
- Visit fewer external pages for some tasks
- Expect direct answers
- Use conversational interfaces
- Delegate multi-step research to agents

This could weaken parts of the traditional link-based search architecture.

---

## 10. Alphabet's Search Response

Alphabet has not treated generative AI as a separate product that must remain outside Search.

Instead, it has integrated AI directly into Search through products including:

- AI Overviews
- AI Mode
- Gemini-powered search experiences

By mid-2026, Google reported more than 2.5 billion monthly users of AI Overviews.

AI Mode surpassed 1 billion monthly active users.

Google also reported that AI-powered Search experiences were increasing overall query activity.

This represents a defensive and offensive strategy simultaneously.

Alphabet is allowing AI to change Search before a competitor is able to redefine Search without Google.

---

## 11. Search Revenue Under AI

The central economic concern is whether AI engagement can be monetized effectively.

In Q2 2026:

**Google Search & other revenue increased 17 percent year over year.**

This demonstrates that Search revenue continued growing during rapid adoption of AI features.

However, one quarter of revenue growth does not prove that AI monetization will permanently reproduce traditional search economics.

Alphabet itself states that AI products may monetize differently from historical consumer offerings and may affect revenue growth and margin trends.

For ORVESTRA, this distinction is important.

**Current revenue resilience does not eliminate future business-model migration.**

---

## 12. Query Expansion

Alphabet argues that AI can expand Search usage rather than only cannibalize existing queries.

AI allows users to ask questions that may previously have required:

- Several searches
- Multiple website visits
- Manual comparison
- Follow-up queries

If AI makes Search useful for more complex tasks, the total addressable query environment can increase.

The relevant business question therefore becomes:

**Does AI reduce the number of monetizable interactions, or does it increase the number and complexity of useful commercial interactions?**

Both effects may occur simultaneously.

## 13. Cost Per Search

AI-generated responses require more compute than conventional search results.

This changes Search economics.

The relevant unit is no longer only:

**Revenue per Query**

It increasingly includes:

**Revenue per Query relative to Compute Cost**

Alphabet stated in Q2 2026 that engineering and hardware optimization had reduced the cost of AI Mode responses to the lowest level since launch while adding more advanced capabilities.

This illustrates a central AI business-model variable:

**Inference efficiency can directly affect product economics.**

---

## 14. AI Search Margin Architecture

Traditional search has benefited from highly optimized infrastructure developed over decades.

Generative AI adds additional:

- Model inference
- Memory
- Accelerator usage
- Energy
- Networking
- Context processing

Therefore, Search revenue can grow while Search unit economics change.

The relevant analytical relationship becomes:

**Search Revenue Growth**

relative to:

**Incremental AI Infrastructure Cost**

Alphabet does not publicly disclose a standalone AI Search margin.

The effect must therefore be observed indirectly through infrastructure investment, depreciation, operating costs, and overall segment economics.

## 19. Why Subscription Matters

Advertising monetizes:

**Attention and Intent**

Subscription monetizes:

**Ongoing User Utility**

The two models behave differently.

Advertising revenue depends partly on:

- Advertiser demand
- User activity
- Commercial intent
- Auction economics

Subscriptions depend more directly on:

- Retention
- Product utility
- Pricing
- Recurring customer relationships

AI can therefore generate value for Alphabet even when the economic interaction does not involve an advertisement.

## 34. Physical Constraints

- Land

- Power
- Cooling
- Networking

**AI Demand**

**Available Compute Capacity**

**Commercially Serviceable Demand**

## 35. Compute Efficiency

Because AI infrastructure is expensive, efficiency becomes a business-model variable.

Improved efficiency can reduce:

- Cost per query
- Cost per API request
- Cost per agent action
- Cloud service cost
- Capital required per unit of demand

Alphabet can pursue efficiency through:

- TPUs
- Model architecture
- Quantization
- Serving optimization
- Data-center design
- Networking
- Software optimization

The economic objective is not simply to build more compute.

It is to generate more commercially useful intelligence per unit of compute.

## 42. Advertising Meets AI

AI changes the consumer interface where advertising appears.

Second:

AI can improve the advertising system itself through:

- Creative generation
- Targeting

- Campaign optimization
- Automated bidding
- Product matching

## 43. Value Migration From Link to Answer

Traditional search places substantial value in:

**Ranking Links**

AI places more value in:

**Synthesizing Answers**

This can move value from:

- Publisher selection
- Link navigation
- Manual comparison

toward:

- Model reasoning
- Context synthesis
- Agent action

Alphabet's strategic objective is to keep this value migration inside Google-controlled environments.

AI Overviews and AI Mode are direct examples.

## 44. Web Ecosystem Dependency

Google Search depends on a broader web ecosystem.

Websites and publishers create much of the information Search organizes.

If AI interfaces reduce traffic to external websites for certain queries, publisher economics could weaken.

A weaker publishing ecosystem could eventually affect the supply of high-quality open-web information.

Google reported that AI Search features continued sending billions of clicks to websites each week.

However, the long-term distribution of value between:

**Search Platform**

and:

**Content Producer**

remains an important structural issue.

## 45. Value Migration From Model to Infrastructure

Frontier model performance may become more substitutable over time.

If several models can complete the same task effectively, value may migrate toward:

- Compute
- Data
- Security
- Orchestration
- Distribution
- Agent frameworks
- Enterprise integration

Alphabet is positioned across many of these layers.

The company therefore does not necessarily need every AI workload to derive value only from Gemini model exclusivity.

---

## 46. Value Migration From Search to Agent

Search historically monetizes user intent before the user completes an action.

Agentic systems may go further.

Instead of:

**Search → Compare → Choose → Act**

the architecture can become:

**Request → Agent → Action**

Examples could include:

- Booking
- Purchasing
- Planning
- Scheduling
- Research
- Navigation

If the agent controls the route from intent to action, it may become an even more valuable commercial interface than traditional search.

Alphabet's combination of Gemini, Search, Maps, Android, payments, and other services creates a potential path into this layer.

---

## 47. Value Migration From Advertising to Transaction

Advertising monetizes the path toward a transaction.

Agents may increasingly participate directly in executing transactions.

This raises a major business-model question:

**Will future AI interfaces primarily monetize attention, access, subscription, API consumption, transaction execution, or some combination of these?**

Alphabet already operates businesses capable of supporting several of these models.

This gives the company flexibility.

It does not yet determine which model will dominate.

---

## 48. AI Cannibalization Risk

Alphabet must manage a difficult internal transition.

AI can increase:

- Search utility
- Cloud demand
- Subscription value
- API usage
- Developer engagement

But it can also reduce the importance of:

- Traditional search result pages
- Link-based discovery
- Some advertising formats
- Some website navigation
- Some conventional software interfaces

Alphabet therefore has to disrupt products that remain highly profitable.

The strategic question is not whether AI changes the old system.

It is whether Alphabet captures enough of the new system.

**More API and Enterprise Demand**  
→ increases Google Cloud consumption

**More Cloud Demand**  
→ supports infrastructure investment

**More Infrastructure**  
→ enables more AI workloads

**Better Models and Compute Efficiency**  
→ reduce cost per interaction

**Lower Cost per Interaction**  
→ supports broader AI deployment

**More Enterprise Adoption**  
→ increases backlog and recurring relationships

The system links:

**Advertising + Subscription + API + Consumption + Infrastructure**

The company already owns one of the most valuable information interfaces ever created:

**Google Search**

AI can disrupt that interface.

But Alphabet also owns many of the layers toward which value may migrate:

**Cloud**

**Compute**

**Custom Silicon**

**Models**

**APIs**

**Enterprise Software**

**Consumer AI**

**Browser**

**Mobile Operating System**

**Video**

**Advertising Infrastructure**

This gives Alphabet an architectural hedge.

Value can migrate away from the traditional ten-link search experience without necessarily migrating outside Alphabet.

## 59. What Makes This Business Interesting

Alphabet is particularly useful for studying:

**How an incumbent disrupts its own strongest business before another company does.**

The company could attempt to protect traditional Search.

Instead, it is inserting generative AI directly into Search while simultaneously building Gemini as a separate interface.

This creates short-term economic uncertainty.

But refusing to change Search would create another risk:

**The user interface could migrate elsewhere while Google protects yesterday's monetization model.**

Alphabet is therefore choosing controlled internal disruption.

## 60. Search Versus Gemini

Search and Gemini may increasingly overlap.

But their economics do not need to become identical.

Search can remain highly monetized through advertising.

Gemini can support:

- Subscription
- API consumption
- Cloud usage
- Agentic activity

The economic system may therefore evolve from one dominant information product toward several complementary AI interfaces.

The key question becomes:

**Can Alphabet preserve user intent even if the interface through which intent is expressed changes?**

Alphabet participates on both sides of many of these transitions.

## 65. Analytical Limits

Alphabet operates across several large businesses and cannot be represented by a single SaaS, advertising, or cloud metric.

Google Services combines:

- Advertising
- Consumer subscriptions
- Platform revenue
- Device revenue

Google Cloud combines:

- Consumption-based infrastructure
- Subscriptions
- Enterprise software
- Services
- Certain product revenue

Therefore, Google Cloud revenue should not be interpreted as pure cloud infrastructure consumption.

Google advertising revenue also contains several different properties and commercial formats.

AI Search economics are not separately disclosed.

A standalone margin for Gemini is not publicly disclosed.

A standalone margin for Gemini API is not publicly disclosed.

Gemini monthly active users should not be interpreted as paid subscribers.

Cloud backlog is future contracted revenue and should not be interpreted as current revenue.

Q2 2026 net income was materially affected by large unrealized gains on equity securities and should not be treated as a clean measure of underlying operating growth.

Capital expenditure supports:

- Consumer products
- Cloud customers
- Frontier AI research
- Other workloads

and cannot be precisely allocated to one AI product from public disclosure.

This analysis does not estimate:

- Search AI standalone profitability
- Gemini standalone profitability
- Token-level Alphabet margins
- Customer-specific cloud economics
- Future search market share
- Future advertising market share
- Future AI model rankings
- Investment value

The analysis is limited to observable business architecture and publicly documented information.

## 66. Conclusion

Alphabet entered the AI transition with an unusual combination of assets.

It already controlled:

**Search**

**Advertising**

**YouTube**

**Android**

**Chrome**

**Cloud**

**Workspace**

**Global Consumer Distribution**

AI introduces risks to several of those businesses.

Search interfaces may change.

Advertising formats may change.

External web traffic may change.

Software interfaces may become agentic.

Compute requirements may increase dramatically.

But AI also increases the economic importance of other assets Alphabet already owns.

Cloud becomes more valuable.

TPUs become more valuable.

API distribution becomes more valuable.

Enterprise data and workflow become more valuable.

Consumer AI distribution becomes more valuable.

The Gemini App gives Alphabet a direct AI destination.

AI Mode allows Google to transform Search internally.

Google Cloud allows the company to monetize enterprise AI regardless of whether the end product is a Google consumer application.

The economic architecture therefore extends beyond protecting Search revenue.

Alphabet is attempting to ensure that if value migrates away from the traditional search interface, it encounters another layer of Alphabet infrastructure.

For ORVESTRA, the central observation is:

**Alphabet's strongest defense against AI disruption may not be preserving the old Search interface. It may be owning enough of the new AI stack that value can migrate away from traditional search without necessarily migrating away from Alphabet.**
