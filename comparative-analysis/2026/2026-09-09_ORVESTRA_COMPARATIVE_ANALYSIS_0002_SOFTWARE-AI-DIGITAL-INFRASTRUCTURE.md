## 1. Comparative Question

Where does economic value migrate when artificial intelligence becomes embedded across software, infrastructure, APIs, enterprise data, workflows, and user interfaces?

Microsoft, Alphabet, OpenAI, Salesforce, and Adobe all participate in AI.

But they enter the transition from very different positions.

Microsoft begins with enterprise software, cloud infrastructure, identity, and developer distribution.

Alphabet begins with search, advertising, consumer distribution, cloud infrastructure, and custom compute.

OpenAI begins with frontier models, ChatGPT, and developer APIs.

Salesforce begins with enterprise systems of record, customer data, workflow, and CRM.

Adobe begins with professional creative workflows, documents, marketing systems, and content production.

The useful comparison is therefore not:

**Which company has the best AI model?**

It is:

**Which layers of the AI economic system does each company control, and where can each continue capturing value if intelligence itself becomes increasingly substitutable?**

---

## 2. Comparative Summary

The five companies represent five different architectures of AI value capture.

### Microsoft

**Cloud + Enterprise Software + Identity + Developer Infrastructure + Workflow**

Microsoft is positioned across both the infrastructure below AI and the enterprise workflow above it.

Its primary structural advantage is breadth across the stack.

---

### Alphabet

**Search + Advertising + Consumer Distribution + Cloud + Custom Compute**

Alphabet faces direct disruption to its historical information-discovery business while simultaneously owning many of the layers toward which AI value may migrate.

Its central problem is internal migration from search economics toward broader AI economics.

---

### OpenAI

**Frontier Models + Consumer AI + API + Agents + Expanding Compute Infrastructure**

OpenAI begins closest to the intelligence layer.

Its challenge is converting model leadership into more durable distribution, workflow, infrastructure, and customer relationships before models become easier to substitute.

---

### Salesforce

**Enterprise Data + System of Record + Workflow + Permissions + Agent Execution**

Salesforce does not need to dominate frontier models.

Its strongest position is the operational context in which models become useful inside enterprises.

---

### Adobe

**Creative Workflow + Professional Production + Documents + Brand + Content Supply Chain**

Adobe faces direct AI commoditization of individual creative features.

Its defense is to move economic value from generation toward editing, production, governance, workflow, and enterprise content operations.

## 7. The Seat Is No Longer Enough

Traditional enterprise software often uses:

**Employee → Seat → Subscription**

AI changes this structure.

One human user can create:

- Many agent runs
- Large inference workloads
- Automated workflows
- Continuous background activity
- Thousands of machine actions

This creates a new architecture:

**Human Seat + Machine Consumption**

Every company in Group 2 is exposed to this transition in some form.

## 9. Why Consumption Matters

AI creates economic activity that can grow without increasing human headcount.

A company can have:

**1,000 Employees**

but:

**Millions of Agent Actions**

or:

**Millions of AI Generations**

or:

**Large API Consumption**

Therefore future software growth may become less tied to:

**Number of Users**

and more tied to:

**Amount of Work Performed by Software**

APIs are increasingly becoming distribution infrastructure.

Traditional software distribution:

**Vendor → User Interface → User**

API distribution:

**Vendor Capability → External Application → End User**

This allows the underlying provider to participate economically without owning the final interface.

AI makes this increasingly difficult.

A user may no longer need to manually navigate:

- CRM screens
- Creative menus
- Search results
- Office applications
- Cloud consoles

An agent can call the underlying capability directly.

Therefore the durable asset may become:

**The Function Beneath the Interface**

rather than:

**The Interface Itself**

### Microsoft

### Alphabet

**Consumer Distribution + Data + Cloud + Platform Integration**

### OpenAI

**AI Interface + Workflow + Developer Integration**

**System of Record + Customization + Workflow + Enterprise Integration**

### Adobe

**Professional Files + Creative Workflow + Asset Infrastructure + Brand Systems**

There is no single form of software lock-in.

## 19. Lock-In Versus Utility

The phrase "ecosystem lock-in" can be misleading if treated only as restriction.

Much switching friction comes from accumulated utility.

An enterprise may remain with Microsoft because identity, security, productivity, and cloud already work together.

A Salesforce customer may remain because years of workflows and custom data structures exist.

An Adobe customer may remain because production assets, tools, plugins, and review processes are integrated.

Therefore:

**Switching Cost is often the accumulated shadow of integration value.**

## 21. Model Commoditization

Group 2 repeatedly raises the possibility that foundation models become increasingly substitutable.

This does not require every model to become identical.

It only requires several models to become:

**Good Enough for the Same Commercial Task**

When that happens, customer choice can increasingly depend on:

- Price
- Latency
- Reliability
- Data access
- Governance
- Distribution
- Workflow integration
- Compute availability

The model becomes one component inside a larger system.

---

## 22. Response to Model Commoditization

### Microsoft

Offers multi-model environments while retaining Azure, enterprise data, identity, and workflow.

### Alphabet

Owns its own models but can capture value through Cloud, Search, APIs, consumer distribution, and compute.

### OpenAI

Moves upward into workflow and downward into infrastructure to reduce reliance on model value alone.

### Salesforce

Uses multiple models while attempting to keep enterprise data, workflow, and actions inside Salesforce.

### Adobe

Integrates multiple creative models while retaining professional editing, production, and governance.

Four of the five cases demonstrate some version of:

**Make the Model Replaceable, Keep the Environment Valuable**

- Customer choice

The two strategies are not mutually exclusive.

Microsoft, Alphabet, OpenAI, Salesforce, and Adobe use different combinations.

## 26. The Infrastructure Trade-Off

Owning compute can provide:

- Capacity control
- Cost optimization
- Hardware integration
- Strategic independence
- Infrastructure revenue

But it also creates:

- Capital expenditure
- Energy exposure
- Construction risk
- Depreciation
- Hardware obsolescence

Using third-party infrastructure reduces direct ownership burden.

But it creates:

- Supplier dependency
- Compute pricing exposure
- Less direct hardware control

Each Group 2 company sits at a different point in this trade-off.

## 27. Compute Cost Changes Software Economics

Traditional software is often described as having low marginal distribution cost.

AI complicates that assumption.

Every AI interaction can consume:

- Accelerator capacity
- Memory
- Networking
- Electricity
- Cooling

The economic relationship becomes:

**Revenue per AI Interaction**

relative to:

**Cost per AI Interaction**

This makes engineering efficiency directly relevant to business architecture.

---

## 28. Efficiency as a Commercial Variable

Lower compute cost can enable:

- Lower prices
- Higher margins
- More free usage
- More agent activity
- More complex reasoning
- Wider adoption

Therefore:

**Model efficiency**

**hardware efficiency**

**inference optimization**

are not only technical achievements.

They are commercial capabilities.

## 101. ORVESTRA Comparative Observation

The most important conclusion from Group 2 is that:

**AI does not remove the software stack. It rearranges where economic value sits inside it.**

Models make intelligence more abundant.

Agents reduce the importance of some traditional interfaces.

APIs make software capabilities callable.

Consumption changes pricing units.

Compute makes software more physically intensive.

Enterprise context makes generic intelligence operational.

Workflow determines whether intelligence becomes useful work.

Distribution determines where customer intent enters the system.

The companies most capable of retaining value are therefore not necessarily those that control one AI layer.

They are the companies that can remain economically relevant as value moves between layers.

## 110. Conclusion

Microsoft, Alphabet, OpenAI, Salesforce, and Adobe demonstrate that AI competition is no longer confined to model capability.

The underlying economic contest increasingly spans the entire digital stack.

Microsoft is attempting to ensure that value migrating between enterprise applications, cloud infrastructure, identity, data, and agents remains inside Microsoft.

Alphabet is transforming Search while building Gemini, Cloud, APIs, and compute infrastructure around the possibility that information discovery itself changes.

OpenAI is expanding in both directions from the model layer: downward toward compute infrastructure and upward toward consumer, developer, enterprise, and workflow ownership.

Salesforce is betting that enterprise context, permissions, systems of record, and business workflows remain scarce even when intelligence becomes widely available.

Adobe is betting that raw generation can become abundant while professional editing, brand governance, production, and content workflow remain economically valuable.

The five companies therefore illustrate five different answers to the same question:

**What remains valuable when intelligence itself becomes easier to access?**

For Microsoft, part of the answer is enterprise infrastructure.

For Alphabet, it is distribution and intent.

For OpenAI, it is the ecosystem surrounding frontier intelligence.

For Salesforce, it is operational enterprise context.

For Adobe, it is professional workflow and production.

The common structural pattern is clear.

AI does not simply create a new software category.

It moves economic value between existing layers.

The durable company may therefore not be the company that owns the strongest single model, interface, or application.

It may be the company that controls enough scarce layers around intelligence that when value migrates, the value still passes through its system.

For ORVESTRA, the central Group 2 observation is:

**The strategic question in AI is not only who owns intelligence. It is who owns the scarce infrastructure, context, workflow, distribution, and customer relationship that make intelligence economically useful.**
