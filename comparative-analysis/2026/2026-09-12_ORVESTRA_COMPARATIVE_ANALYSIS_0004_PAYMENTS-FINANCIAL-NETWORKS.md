## 1. Comparative Question

Who captures value when money moves, and which layer of the payment system does each company actually control?

Visa, Mastercard, PayPal, Block, and JPMorgan Chase all participate in payments.

But they do not occupy the same economic layer.

Visa primarily controls:

**Global Payment Network Infrastructure**

Mastercard combines:

**Global Payment Network + Multi-Rail Infrastructure + Services**

PayPal primarily controls:

**Wallet + Checkout + Merchant Processing + Payment Orchestration**

Block combines:

**Consumer Financial Network + Merchant Operating System + Selected Banking Infrastructure**

JPMorgan Chase combines:

**Deposits + Credit + Issuing + Acquiring + Treasury + Multi-Rail Banking Infrastructure**

The central comparison is therefore not:

**Which company processes the most money?**

It is:

**Where in the movement of money does each company create and capture value?**

---

## 2. The Payment System Is Layered

A single payment can involve several independent economic layers.

A simplified architecture is:

**Customer**

→ **Interface**

→ **Account / Credential**

→ **Processor / Acquirer**

→ **Payment Rail**

→ **Issuer / Funding Institution**

→ **Clearing**

→ **Settlement**

→ **Merchant / Recipient**

Additional layers can include:

- Fraud
- Authentication
- FX
- Credit
- Loyalty
- Merchant software
- Treasury
- Liquidity
- Data
- Lending

One company does not need to control every layer to build a powerful payments business.

---

## 3. Structural Positioning

| Company | Primary Structural Position |
|---|---|
| Visa | Global payment network |
| Mastercard | Global multi-rail network + services |
| PayPal | Wallet + checkout + processing + orchestration |
| Block | Consumer network + merchant operating system + selected banking infrastructure |
| JPMorgan Chase | Regulated bank + balance sheet + issuing + acquiring + treasury + multi-rail payments |

These are five different business architectures operating inside the same financial system.

---

## 4. The Rail Is Not the Interface

A central Group 4 distinction is:

**Payment Rail**

and:

**Customer Interface**

are not the same thing.

A consumer may see:

**PayPal**

while the transaction uses:

**Visa**

and ultimately draws funds from:

**JPMorgan Chase.**

A Cash App Card customer may see:

**Cash App**

while the payment travels through:

**Visa infrastructure.**

The company visible to the customer is therefore not necessarily the company operating the underlying financial rail.

---

## 5. The Account Is Not the Rail

Likewise:

**Owning the bank account**

does not mean:

**Owning the payment network.**

JPMorgan Chase can own the customer's:

- Deposit
- Credit line
- Card relationship

while still relying on:

- Visa
- Mastercard
- ACH
- FedNow
- The Clearing House RTP
- SWIFT
- Other rails

Account ownership and network ownership are separate forms of control.

## 17. Interchange Is Not Network Revenue

Visa and Mastercard demonstrate a critical distinction.

Interchange generally flows between:

**Acquirer**

and:

**Issuer.**

Visa and Mastercard operate network infrastructure around the transaction.

They do not simply receive the merchant's entire payment cost.

Therefore:

**Merchant Card Cost ≠ Network Revenue.**

---

## 18. Interchange Can Be Revenue Elsewhere

Block and JPMorgan Chase demonstrate another side of the same architecture.

Cash App Card can generate issuer-side interchange economics.

JPMorgan Chase can earn card-related interchange as an issuer.

Therefore the same economic mechanism that is:

**Not Visa Revenue**

can become:

**Issuer Economics**

for another Group 4 company.

**Trusted multi-rail connectivity and services.**

The companies that preserve control of a necessary layer can continue capturing value.

## 130. Final Comparative Conclusion

Visa, Mastercard, PayPal, Block, and JPMorgan Chase all participate in the movement of money.

But the phrase:

**Payments Company**

conceals fundamentally different businesses.

Visa is powerful because millions of institutions and merchants can rely on one globally accepted network.

Mastercard operates a similar network while increasingly expanding into real-time payments, security, open finance, money movement, stablecoin interoperability, and agentic commerce.

PayPal sits above many rails.

Its value comes from allowing consumers and merchants to interact without needing to understand all the infrastructure underneath.

Braintree, Fastlane, PayPal Open, Venmo, and PayPal's financial products progressively extend that position from wallet toward commerce infrastructure.

Block takes another path.

Cash App builds a consumer financial relationship.

Square builds a merchant operating relationship.

Payments connect the two.

Square Financial Services gives Block an increasing ability to internalize selected banking and acquiring functions while lending creates additional economics and additional risk.

JPMorgan Chase goes deeper into the financial stack.

It can hold the deposit before the payment.

It can provide the credit behind the payment.

It can issue the card.

It can acquire the merchant.

It can manage the corporation's liquidity.

It can route money through cards, ACH, wires, RTP, FedNow, or blockchain-based deposit infrastructure.

It can continue participating after the payment through settlement, lending, liquidity, and treasury services.

None of these companies owns the entire financial system.

That is the point.

The payment system works because specialized institutions control different layers and connect them together.

For ORVESTRA, the central Group 4 conclusion is:

**The strategic question in payments is not simply who moves the money. It is who owns the layer that the rest of the system still needs before, during, or after the money moves.**

Visa owns critical interoperability.

Mastercard extends interoperability into multi-rail services.

PayPal owns important parts of checkout and orchestration.

Block owns increasingly deep consumer and merchant relationships.

JPMorgan Chase owns the account and balance-sheet infrastructure surrounding the transaction.

The strongest payment architecture is therefore not necessarily the company closest to the customer or the company closest to the rail.

It is the company whose layer remains economically necessary even as:

**The Interface Changes**

**The Rail Changes**

**The Credential Changes**

and:

**The Form of Money Changes.**

- JPMorgan Chase Total Payments Transaction Volume

- Visa Payment Credentials

do not represent equivalent customer populations.

No universal payment take rate, customer count, transaction margin, or payment profitability measure should therefore be inferred across the five companies.
