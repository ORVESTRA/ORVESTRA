## 1. Comparative Question

How does a physical technology business turn manufacturing capacity and installed infrastructure into durable economic value after the initial product is sold or built?

A second question follows:

**Where does the durable value actually sit?**

Across Tesla, Caterpillar, Deere, GE Vernova, and NextEra Energy, the answer moves progressively through the physical value chain.

### Tesla

**Manufacturing + Installed Base + Software + Energy Infrastructure + AI-Enabled Fleet**

### Caterpillar

**Manufacturing + Installed Base + Dealer Network + Aftermarket + Financing + Autonomous Equipment**

### Deere

**Manufacturing + Installed Base + Dealer Network + Precision Technology + Lifecycle Solutions + Production-System Automation**

### GE Vernova

**Manufacturing + Installed Energy Infrastructure + Long-Term Services + Grid Equipment + System Bottleneck Exposure**

### NextEra Energy

**Capital + Development + Infrastructure Ownership + Regulated Utility + Contracted Energy + Grid Expansion**

Together, the five companies reveal a structural progression:

**Manufacturing**

→ **Installed Base**

→ **Aftermarket**

→ **Digital and Service Layer**

→ **Infrastructure**

→ **Infrastructure Ownership**

---

# 2. Group 6 Structural Thesis

Physical technology businesses do not become durable merely because they manufacture difficult products.

Manufacturing creates:

**The Initial Asset.**

Durable economics emerge when that asset becomes:

1. An installed base
2. A service relationship
3. A distribution surface for technology
4. A long-lived infrastructure position
5. A productive asset capable of generating recurring economic output

The deeper Group 6 question is therefore not:

**Who Manufactures the Best Physical Product?**

It is:

**Who Continues to Capture Value After the Physical Product Has Already Been Built?**

# 4. Manufacturing Is the Beginning, Not the End

Tesla, Caterpillar, Deere, and GE Vernova all begin with:

**Manufacturing.**

But the strategic value of manufacturing differs.

Tesla uses manufacturing to create:

**A Distributed Installed Base for Software and AI.**

Caterpillar uses manufacturing to create:

**A Long-Lived Aftermarket Population.**

Deere uses manufacturing to create:

**A Physical Production System That Can Be Upgraded Digitally.**

GE Vernova uses manufacturing to create:

**Long-Lived Energy Infrastructure That Can Generate Services for Decades.**

NextEra Energy then takes the model one step further.

It often does not sell the infrastructure at all.

It:

**Owns It.**

This progression reveals a larger Business Model Lab principle.

A physical product is not economically durable merely because it is difficult to manufacture.

Durability comes from remaining attached to:

**The Customer's Continuing Need.**

If the customer no longer needs private vehicle ownership, Tesla must remain attached to:

**Mobility.**

If the customer needs fewer machines, Caterpillar must remain attached to:

**Productivity.**

If farmers need fewer manually operated machines, Deere must remain attached to:

**Agricultural Output.**

If the generation mix changes, GE Vernova must remain attached to:

**The Physical Electricity System.**

If centralized utility architecture changes, NextEra Energy must remain attached to:

**Necessary Energy Infrastructure.**

That is why the Business Model Lab signature question matters:

**What could make the current model less necessary?**

The question does not assume that the underlying customer need disappears.

It asks whether:

**A Different Architecture Can Satisfy the Same Need.**

For ORVESTRA, the central Group 6 conclusion is:

**The most durable physical business model is not the one that protects today's product forever. It is the one that can follow the underlying customer need as value migrates from manufacturing to installed base, from installed base to service, from service to software and automation, from equipment to infrastructure, and ultimately from selling the physical asset to controlling the productive system in which that asset becomes economically necessary.**

- Caterpillar sales or services revenues
