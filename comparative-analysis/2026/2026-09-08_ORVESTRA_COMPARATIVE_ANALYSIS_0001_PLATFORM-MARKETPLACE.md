## 1. Comparative Question

Where does platform power actually come from?

Amazon, Uber, Airbnb, DoorDash, and Etsy all operate marketplace or platform businesses.

But they do not control the same assets.

They do not face the same form of disintermediation.

They do not monetize the same layers.

And they do not depend on suppliers, providers, merchants, hosts, or sellers in the same way.

The useful comparison is therefore not:

**Which platform is largest?**

It is:

**Which parts of the commercial system does each platform control, and which parts remain outside its control?**

---

## 2. Comparative Summary

The five companies demonstrate five different architectures of platform power.

### Amazon

**Marketplace + Demand + Logistics + Advertising + Commercial Infrastructure**

Amazon combines marketplace demand with substantial physical fulfillment and logistics infrastructure.

Its strongest structural advantage is that it can remain economically relevant even when some commerce occurs outside Amazon.com.

---

### Uber

**Demand + Local Liquidity + Coordination + Dispatch**

Uber does not own most of the vehicles providing transportation capacity.

Its power comes from organizing fragmented supply around real-time demand.

The critical asset is marketplace liquidity and coordination.

---

### Airbnb

**Demand + Brand + Trust + Booking Infrastructure**

Airbnb owns little of the accommodation inventory.

Its platform value comes from making fragmented hospitality supply easier to discover, trust, book, pay for, and review.

Brand and direct app usage strengthen its demand position.

---

### DoorDash

**Marketplace Demand + Local Logistics + Merchant Infrastructure**

DoorDash combines marketplace discovery with local fulfillment.

Its Commerce Platform allows merchants to own demand while continuing to use DoorDash infrastructure.

This creates value-capture opportunities beyond the marketplace transaction itself.

---

### Etsy

**Discovery + Trust + Payments + Seller Services**

Etsy coordinates independent creative businesses whose products, brands, and production systems remain largely outside Etsy's ownership.

Its power depends heavily on discovery, trust, buyer demand, payments, and marketplace reputation.

---

## 3. Core Platform Architecture

| Company | Primary Supply | Core Platform Role | Strongest Control Layer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon | Independent sellers and retail inventory | Commerce marketplace and infrastructure | Demand + logistics |
| Uber | Drivers, couriers, merchants, carriers | Real-time coordination | Local liquidity + dispatch |
| Airbnb | Hosts and hospitality providers | Travel marketplace | Trust + demand access |
| DoorDash | Merchants and Dashers | Local commerce coordination | Demand + delivery infrastructure |
| Etsy | Independent sellers and creators | Creative goods marketplace | Discovery + trust |

The important observation is that platform businesses do not require the same form of control.

Some control infrastructure.

Some control demand.

Some control coordination.

Some control discovery.

Some control trust.

Platform power is therefore multi-dimensional.

---

## 4. Asset Ownership

The five businesses show different relationships between ownership and control.

### Amazon

Amazon owns substantial physical infrastructure.

This includes fulfillment and logistics capabilities that reinforce marketplace power.

### Uber

Uber owns relatively little of the transportation capacity used to deliver its core services.

The productive asset remains distributed across external providers.

### Airbnb

Airbnb owns little of the underlying accommodation inventory.

Hosts retain ownership or control of the property.

### DoorDash

DoorDash generally does not own the merchants or most delivery vehicles, although parts of the business include more direct operational exposure.

### Etsy

Etsy owns neither the seller businesses nor most of the products sold through the marketplace.

The underlying creative production system remains decentralized.

This leads to a broader principle:

**Asset-light does not mean dependency-light.**

The less productive capacity the platform owns, the more it may depend on continued participation by external providers.

---

## 5. Network Effects

All five businesses benefit from network effects.

But those effects operate differently.

### Amazon

More buyers attract sellers.

More sellers increase selection.

Logistics and Prime reinforce the marketplace loop.

### Uber

More demand attracts supply.

More supply improves availability.

But liquidity must exist at the correct place and time.

### Airbnb

More hosts increase travel inventory.

More guests increase host opportunity.

But accommodation remains location-specific and date-specific.

### DoorDash

More merchants increase selection.

More consumers create merchant demand.

More orders support Dasher activity.

Physical density determines fulfillment efficiency.

### Etsy

More sellers increase unique supply.

More buyers increase seller opportunity.

But additional inventory creates value only when discovery remains effective.

---

## 6. Network Effect Quality

The comparison reveals that network size alone is insufficient.

A marketplace can have millions of participants and still fail to create useful liquidity.

The more important concept is:

**Network Effect Quality**

This depends on whether the network produces the correct type of supply for the relevant demand.

For Uber:

**Right driver + right place + right time**

For Airbnb:

**Right property + right destination + right dates**

For DoorDash:

**Right merchant + local Dasher capacity + acceptable delivery time**

For Etsy:

**Right product + effective discovery**

For Amazon:

**Relevant product + inventory availability + reliable fulfillment**

The strength of a network therefore depends on usability, not only participant count.

---

## 7. Supply Dependence

Each platform depends on external supply differently.

### Amazon

Seller dependence is significant, but Amazon also operates first-party retail and owns substantial fulfillment capability.

### Uber

Driver and courier participation is fundamental to marketplace liquidity.

### Airbnb

Host supply determines inventory quality, availability, and geographic coverage.

### DoorDash

Merchant supply and Dasher capacity must be available simultaneously.

### Etsy

Independent sellers create virtually all marketplace differentiation.

The platform may control distribution.

The provider often controls the underlying product.

---

## 8. Multi-Homing

Multi-homing exists across all five systems.

### Sellers

Amazon and Etsy sellers may use multiple marketplaces or owned websites.

### Drivers

Uber drivers may participate on competing transportation platforms.

### Hosts

Airbnb hosts may cross-list properties.

### Merchants

DoorDash merchants may use multiple delivery platforms and direct ordering channels.

### Consumers

Consumers can usually maintain accounts across several competing services.

This means platform participation does not necessarily imply exclusivity.

A platform can have a large participant base while still facing continuous competition for each participant's activity.

---

## 9. Disintermediation

Disintermediation risk is present across all five companies but takes different forms.

### Amazon

Seller and buyer relationships may move elsewhere.

Amazon reduces this risk by remaining relevant through logistics and infrastructure.

### Uber

A rider and driver may theoretically transact directly.

Uber's defense is repeated demand, convenience, matching, payments, safety, and liquidity.

### Airbnb

Hosts and guests may transact directly after establishing a relationship.

Airbnb relies heavily on trust, protection, booking infrastructure, and marketplace rules.

### DoorDash

Merchants may direct customers to their own ordering channels.

DoorDash responds by selling infrastructure through Drive and Commerce Platform.

### Etsy

Successful sellers may develop direct websites, audiences, and repeat customer relationships.

Etsy must remain valuable through discovery, trust, payments, advertising, and marketplace demand.

---

## 10. Three Responses to Disintermediation

The group reveals three broad strategic responses.

### Response 1: Keep the Transaction Inside the Platform

Use:

- Rules
- Convenience
- Membership
- Trust
- Buyer protections
- Reputation systems

Airbnb and Etsy rely heavily on this structure.

---

### Response 2: Strengthen the Platform Through Infrastructure

Make the platform difficult to replace because it controls useful operating systems.

Amazon is the clearest example.

---

### Response 3: Monetize Activity Even When Demand Moves Outside the Marketplace

Allow external commerce to continue using platform infrastructure.

Examples include:

- Amazon Multi-Channel Fulfillment
- Uber Direct
- DoorDash Drive and Commerce Platform
- Etsy Partner Checkout

This is one of the most important structural patterns across Group 1.

---

## 11. Marketplace Dependence Versus Infrastructure Dependence

A company can reduce dependence on a marketplace without becoming independent of the platform provider.

Example:

A merchant leaves marketplace discovery.

But still uses:

- Logistics
- Payments
- Delivery
- Checkout
- Software
- Advertising
- Merchant infrastructure

The dependency changes from:

**Marketplace Dependence**

to:

**Infrastructure Dependence**

This distinction is especially important for:

- Amazon
- Uber
- DoorDash

It may also become increasingly relevant for Etsy as external discovery and Partner Checkout develop.

---

## 12. Owned Distribution

Owned distribution differs substantially across the five companies.

### Amazon

Strongest owned distribution infrastructure in the group.

Amazon controls:

- Marketplace traffic
- Prime
- Fulfillment
- Delivery
- Advertising surfaces
- Customer accounts

### Uber

Owns strong demand interfaces and coordination infrastructure but little physical transport capacity.

### Airbnb

Owned distribution is primarily:

- Brand
- Application
- Direct traffic
- Customer accounts

### DoorDash

Controls marketplace demand and membership while also extending infrastructure into merchant-owned channels.

### Etsy

Owned distribution is primarily digital:

- Marketplace
- Application
- Email
- Push notifications
- Buyer profiles
- Brand

This comparison demonstrates that:

**Owned distribution does not always mean owning physical distribution.**

It can also mean owning reliable access to demand.

## 13. Value Creation

The five companies create different forms of value.

| Company | Primary Value Creation |
|---|---|
| Amazon | Selection, convenience, fulfillment, demand aggregation |
| Uber | Real-time coordination and local liquidity |
| Airbnb | Trust and searchable global accommodation supply |
| DoorDash | Local demand aggregation and fulfillment |
| Etsy | Discovery of differentiated creative supply |

All five reduce transaction friction.

But the type of friction differs.

Amazon reduces commerce and logistics friction.

Uber reduces transport coordination friction.

Airbnb reduces accommodation discovery and trust friction.

DoorDash reduces local ordering and delivery friction.

Etsy reduces discovery friction between fragmented creative supply and global demand.

## 15. Monetization Depth

The comparison suggests a useful concept:

**Monetization Depth**

This describes how many different commercial layers surrounding the same participant relationship can generate revenue.

Higher monetization depth does not automatically mean a better business.

It means the platform has more possible value-capture points.

Amazon demonstrates very high monetization depth.

DoorDash and Uber are expanding theirs.

Etsy monetizes several seller layers.

Airbnb remains more concentrated around the booking relationship.

---

## 16. Take Rate Comparison Boundary

Direct comparison of reported take rates across these companies would be misleading.

The metrics differ in definition.

Amazon does not disclose one consolidated marketplace take rate.

Uber's Revenue Margin is not a universal transaction commission.

Airbnb revenue divided by Gross Booking Value does not represent one universal fee.

DoorDash Net Revenue Margin includes revenue sources beyond merchant commissions.

Etsy Revenue Take Rate includes multiple marketplace and service revenue streams.

Therefore:

**Reported take-rate-style metrics are company-specific accounting and operating constructs.**

They should not be treated as directly comparable transaction commissions.

## 18. What Each Platform Actually Owns

The five companies reveal an important distinction between:

**Owning the asset**

and:

**Owning the interface**

Amazon owns more infrastructure than the others.

Uber owns the coordination interface.

Airbnb owns the trusted booking interface.

DoorDash owns demand and local-commerce coordination interfaces.

Etsy owns the discovery and transaction interface.

The underlying asset may remain external.

Commercial power can still accumulate around the interface through which the asset reaches demand.

---

## 19. Provider Independence

Provider independence varies across the group.

### Amazon Seller

Can build:

- Direct website
- Brand
- Other marketplaces
- Independent logistics

But Amazon's demand and logistics scale can create substantial dependence.

### Uber Driver

Can use other platforms.

But individual drivers generally have limited independent demand aggregation.

### Airbnb Host

Can operate direct channels and other platforms.

Professional property managers may have stronger independent distribution.

### DoorDash Merchant

Can maintain owned ordering channels and alternative delivery relationships.

### Etsy Seller

May progressively build a highly portable independent business.

This makes Etsy particularly exposed to increasing seller independence.

---

## 20. Who Owns the Customer Relationship?

This question differs across the group.

### Amazon

Amazon generally owns a significant portion of the consumer interface.

### Uber

Uber owns the consumer application relationship for marketplace-originated activity.

### Airbnb

Airbnb owns the booking interface while the guest also develops a relationship with the host.

### DoorDash

DoorDash may own the consumer relationship through Marketplace, or the merchant may own it through Commerce Platform.

### Etsy

Etsy owns marketplace discovery, while the seller's identity can become a meaningful independent relationship.

The more the provider can independently own the customer, the greater the potential disintermediation risk.

## 21. Competitive Moat Structure

The five companies show different moat combinations.

### Amazon

**Scale + Logistics + Prime + Demand + Advertising**

### Uber

**Liquidity + Demand + Dispatch + Cross-Platform Usage**

### Airbnb

**Brand + Trust + Supply Breadth + Reviews**

### DoorDash

**Local Density + Membership + Logistics + Merchant Infrastructure**

### Etsy

**Unique Supply + Brand + Discovery + Human Differentiation**

There is no single marketplace moat.

Durability depends on the combination of several reinforcing systems.

---

## 22. The Infrastructure Transition

The strongest cross-company pattern is the transition from:

**Marketplace**

toward:

**Infrastructure**

Amazon is the most advanced example.

Capabilities developed for Amazon commerce are increasingly available outside Amazon's marketplace.

Uber Direct allows merchant-owned demand to use Uber delivery infrastructure.

DoorDash Commerce Platform allows merchant-owned demand to use DoorDash ordering and fulfillment infrastructure.

Etsy is experimenting with transaction participation through external discovery environments.

This produces a broader structural pattern:

**Build Marketplace**

then

**Accumulate Demand and Supply**

then

**Build Operating Capability**

then

**Productize the Capability**

then

**Extend Beyond the Marketplace**

---

## 23. Productization as Platform Defense

Productization can convert a platform threat into a revenue opportunity.

A merchant leaving marketplace discovery is a threat.

But if the merchant still purchases logistics, payments, software, or delivery infrastructure, the relationship is not fully lost.

The strongest platform defense may therefore be:

**Do not require every customer to remain inside the original product.**

Instead:

**Make the underlying capability useful beyond the original product.**

Amazon, Uber, and DoorDash demonstrate this particularly clearly.

---

## 24. The Limits of Lock-In

None of the five companies fully controls its ecosystem.

Sellers can leave.

Drivers can multi-home.

Hosts can cross-list.

Merchants can build direct channels.

Buyers can compare alternatives.

Therefore, platform power should not be interpreted as permanent lock-in.

A stronger interpretation is:

**Repeatedly earned relevance**

Platforms remain powerful when participants continue to receive enough value that participation remains commercially rational.

---

## 25. Structural Risk by Company

### Amazon

Risk increases if sellers can access comparable demand and logistics elsewhere without losing meaningful economics.

### Uber

Risk increases if alternative platforms can reproduce local liquidity or autonomous providers control both supply and demand interfaces.

### Airbnb

Risk increases if hosts and guests can reproduce trust, discovery, and booking convenience elsewhere.

### DoorDash

Risk increases if merchants can own demand and obtain cheaper or superior fulfillment alternatives.

### Etsy

Risk increases if creators can build enough independent demand that Etsy discovery becomes less necessary.

## 26. ORVESTRA Comparative Observation

The most important conclusion from Group 1 is that:

**Platform power is not the same as marketplace ownership.**

Platform power can come from control over:

- Demand
- Discovery
- Trust
- Payments
- Logistics
- Coordination
- Membership
- Advertising
- Data
- Infrastructure

The strongest platforms often control several of these layers simultaneously.

The deeper the platform moves into reusable infrastructure, the less dependent it becomes on keeping every transaction inside the original marketplace.

## 29. What This Group Teaches About Disintermediation

Disintermediation should not be evaluated only as:

**Did the transaction leave the platform?**

A better question is:

**Which layers of the original platform remained economically relevant after the transaction moved?**

A transaction may leave marketplace discovery but retain:

- Payments
- Logistics
- Delivery
- Software
- Advertising
- Checkout
- Trust
- Identity

This creates partial disintermediation rather than complete removal.

That distinction is increasingly important in platform analysis.

---

## 30. What This Group Teaches About Owned Distribution

Owned distribution is also broader than physical logistics.

A company can own distribution through:

- A customer application
- Membership
- Brand
- Search habit
- Email
- Direct traffic
- Logistics
- Merchant software

The strategic question is:

**Can the company reach demand without repeatedly renting access from another platform?**

Amazon, Airbnb, DoorDash, Uber, and Etsy all attempt to strengthen this relationship in different ways.

## 31. What This Group Teaches About Value Capture

Value capture becomes stronger when a company can monetize multiple layers around the same commercial relationship.

A platform may begin with:

**Transaction Fee**

and expand toward:

**Transaction + Advertising + Membership + Payments + Logistics + Software**

This does not guarantee economic superiority.

But it increases the number of pathways through which value created in the ecosystem can be captured.

## 33. Conclusion

Amazon, Uber, Airbnb, DoorDash, and Etsy demonstrate that marketplace businesses can look similar from the outside while relying on very different sources of power.

Amazon reinforces the marketplace through physical infrastructure.

Uber organizes real-time distributed capacity.

Airbnb organizes trust around externally owned accommodation.

DoorDash combines marketplace demand with merchant infrastructure.

Etsy organizes creative supply through discovery, trust, and seller services.

The common pattern is not simply network effects.

It is the ability to control enough critical layers between supply and demand that the platform remains useful even when ecosystem participants retain significant independence.

The strongest structural development across the group is the movement beyond marketplace ownership.

Several platforms are increasingly attempting to remain valuable when:

- Sellers own their brands
- Merchants own their demand
- Hosts cross-list
- Drivers multi-home
- Consumers begin discovery elsewhere

For ORVESTRA, the central Group 1 observation is:

**The durable platform may not be the one that keeps every transaction inside its marketplace. It may be the one that remains useful across the greatest number of ways the transaction can occur.**
